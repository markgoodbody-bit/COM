## What the August 2026 payroll study supports

Using high-frequency ADP payroll data through June 2026, the authors report:
- no evidence of widespread economy-wide job displacement associated with AI;
- employment for workers aged 22–25 in highly AI-exposed occupations around 19% below where it would be if it had kept pace with similarly aged workers in less-exposed occupations;
- no comparable gap for experienced workers;
- the adjustment appears mainly through reduced hiring of young workers rather than increased separations;
- declines are concentrated in occupations where observed AI usage is more automating than complementary.

The authors explicitly state these are **descriptive patterns, not causal estimates**. They also note sensitivity to controls/specification, education effects and some differential pre-trends.

Therefore:

~~~text
YOUNG-WORKER EMPLOYMENT GAP
!=
AI CAUSAL EFFECT ESTIMATE

AI-EXPOSED OCCUPATION
!=
AI-CAUSED EMPLOYMENT CHANGE
~~~

## What the 41-country study adds

The newer study analyses 1.25 billion job postings and 154 million employment records across 41 countries.

It reports that foreign affiliates of AI-adopting companies reduce the junior share of their workforce relative to comparable controls.

Crucially, the reported decline in junior share comes primarily from **growth in senior employment rather than a fall in junior employment**, with suggestive evidence of modest overall employment growth.

Therefore:

~~~text
JUNIOR SHARE DOWN
!=
JUNIOR EMPLOYMENT DOWN

RELATIVE ENTRY SHARE SHRINKS
CAN COEXIST WITH
TOTAL EMPLOYMENT GROWTH
~~~

## Why this is a never-built-door pressure case

Mechanical Ethics' unfinished never-built-door edge concerns consequential routes that disappear or narrow without producing a conventional refusal event.

Labour-market adjustment through hiring rather than separations has exactly that measurement shape:

~~~text
LAYOFF
-> VISIBLE EVENT / NAMED WORKER / TERMINATION RECORD

REDUCED ENTRY HIRING
-> FEWER NEW ROUTES / FEWER ENTRANTS
-> OFTEN NO INDIVIDUAL REFUSAL EVENT
~~~

A graduate or early-career worker does not need to be fired for their reachable labour-market future to change.

But the aggregate evidence does **not** let us identify a specific counterfactual job that would otherwise have existed for a specific person.

Preserve:

~~~text
NO LAYOFF EVENT
!=
NO ENTRY-ROUTE CHANGE

NO REJECTION LETTER
!=
NO FORECLOSED OPPORTUNITY

FEWER JUNIOR HIRES
!=
IDENTIFIABLE PERSON WAS DENIED A SPECIFIC JOB
~~~

## Measurement lesson

A labour aperture focused on unemployment/separations can miss a hiring-margin transition.

Potential measurements belong to labour economists and include:
- hiring rates / inflows;
- employment by age/experience;
- junior share;
- vacancy/posting composition;
- occupation/task exposure;
- firm adoption;
- senior/junior decomposition.

The project should not invent its own labour-market metric.

## Relation to the Jubilee / Yang quarry

The debate transcript supplied the hypothesis:

> the easiest worker to remove may be the worker never hired.

The current research does **not** prove that exact causal claim.

It does provide stronger independent evidence that:
- early-career AI-exposed employment patterns have diverged;
- reduced hiring rather than separations is an important observed adjustment margin;
- firm AI adoption can shift workforce composition away from juniors even where junior employment does not literally decline.

So the debate moves from **argument quarry** to **empirically supported measurement pressure**, with causal ceilings preserved.
