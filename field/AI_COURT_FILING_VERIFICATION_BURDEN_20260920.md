## Current world pressure

A 16 September 2026 opinion from Florida's Fourth District Court of Appeal,
`Lisandrillo v. Palozzi`, addresses filings the court says appear to contain
AI-generated components.

The court is explicit that its concern is **not** fabricated cases or false
citations. The authorities cited exist. The court's stated problem is
convoluted, false or frivolous reasoning and the absence of meaningful
independent professional judgment.

The opinion:
- denies the requested extraordinary writ;
- says abusive AI filing threatens orderly administration of justice;
- places responsibility on the attorney for the filed work regardless of whether
  it was AI-generated, AI-assisted or otherwise;
- orders counsel to show cause why sanctions, including possible Florida Bar
  referral, should not be imposed.

## Strongest owner

Courts, procedural rules, professional-conduct rules and legal-ethics bodies own
the operative obligations and sanctions.

Current US federal judiciary guidance independently converges on:
- do not delegate core judicial decision/adjudication functions to AI;
- independently verify AI-generated content;
- the human/judiciary user remains accountable for work performed with AI;
- disclosure, confidentiality, integrity and security remain context-dependent
  obligations.

## Thin relation

The open-source maintainer case already preserves:

```text
GENERATION COST FALLS
-> SUBMISSION VOLUME / PLAUSIBILITY CAN RISE
-> VERIFICATION COST REMAINS HUMAN / SCARCE
-> DEFAULT OPEN INTAKE CAN TRANSFER BURDEN
```

The court case adds a harder receiver:

```text
LOW-COST PLAUSIBLE FILING
-> FORMAL COURT INTAKE
-> JUDGE / CLERK / OPPOSING PARTY MUST PARSE AND TEST IT
-> PROCEDURAL CLOCKS CONTINUE
-> BAD ARGUMENT MAY DELAY / DISTORT / CONSUME LEGAL PROCESS
-> CORRECTION / SANCTION ROUTE ACTIVATES
```

Two distinctions matter:

```text
CITATION EXISTS != ARGUMENT SOUND
SUBMITTER RESPONSIBLE != RECEIVER BURDEN ZERO
```

A rule assigning responsibility to the signer is necessary but does not make the
verification burden vanish. The receiver may still have to discover the defect
before responsibility can be enforced.

## What this does not establish

Do not infer:
- that the Florida lawyer in question definitively used AI; the opinion says the
  court suspects AI-generated components;
- that AI-assisted legal work is generally poor;
- that human-authored filings are sound by default;
- that citation verification is sufficient for legal competence;
- that sanctions eliminate the verification burden;
- that this one case is representative of courts generally;
- that Mechanical Ethics / TRACE improves legal adjudication.
