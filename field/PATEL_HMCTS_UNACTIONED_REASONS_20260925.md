## Bounded factual sequence

- The First-tier Tribunal dismissed two benefit appeals on 14 August 2012.
- On 29 August 2012, within time, Mr Patel emailed the FTT saying he wished to appeal and asking for the full report/judgment.
- The request received an automated acknowledgement.
- The Upper Tribunal records that no further steps appear to have been taken by HMCTS to comply, and there was no evidence Mr Patel was ever given the statement of reasons.
- Mr Patel returned to the matter in 2022; the FTT eventually dealt with it in 2025.
- In 2025 the FTT refused to extend time for a statement-of-reasons request.
- The Upper Tribunal held that this part of the 2025 decision involved a material error: no extension was needed because the request had in fact been made in time in 2012.
- The Upper Tribunal nevertheless did not set that decision aside. By then the original papers were no longer available, the presiding judge had retired, the Department had only limited records, and a fresh statement of reasons was considered impracticable.
- The Upper Tribunal also concluded that the substantive challenge to the 2012 decision had no realistic merits and refused to admit the very late permission-to-appeal application.
- The judgment notes that Mr Patel may have grounds for a formal complaint to HMCTS about the failure to act on the 2012 request.

## Structural reading

```text
REQUEST MADE IN TIME != ROUTE COMPLETED
AUTOMATED ACKNOWLEDGEMENT != ACTION
FORMAL RIGHT != PRACTICALLY PRESERVED RIGHT
ADMINISTRATIVE FAILURE + TIME -> EVIDENCE / RECONSTRUCTION LOSS
PROCEDURAL ERROR FOUND != ORIGINAL DECISION REOPENED
COMPLAINT ROUTE != RESTORATION OF LOST APPEAL ROUTE
```

The sharp point is not merely delay. A required intermediate artifact — the statement of reasons — was requested while the route was live but was never produced. Time then changed the recoverable state: papers disappeared, institutional memory degraded and the original decision became impracticable to reconstruct.

This is a concrete example of a route hardening through loss of the material needed to use it, even though the initiating request was timely.

## Important limits

Do **not** infer:
- that the 2012 FTT decision was substantively wrong;
- that Mr Patel would have succeeded if reasons had been supplied;
- that HMCTS's 2012 failure was the sole cause of the eventual inability to appeal;
- that the Upper Tribunal excused all later delay;
- that this single case establishes a general rate or pattern across HMCTS.

The judgment expressly found the substantive challenge had no realistic prospects and found the later explanation for delay unpersuasive.
