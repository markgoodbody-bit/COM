## Current owner structure

The Infected Blood Compensation Scheme is established in law and operated by the Infected Blood Compensation Authority (IBCA), an independent arm's-length body.

The scheme has evolved through successive sets of regulations and consultation/inquiry recommendations.

Current public material says:
- a fourth set of regulations became law in 2026 and changes how some claims are assessed;
- people already paid who are affected by the new regulations will have their claims reviewed/reworked rather than being treated as permanently closed under the earlier mechanism;
- the four existing UK Infected Blood Support Schemes are scheduled to close/transfer payment responsibility to IBCA in early 2027;
- IBCA states that existing support payments should continue without interruption through that transfer;
- estates and affected/bereaved people have distinct routes, and compensation can extend beyond the original infected person;
- the scheme includes formal feedback routes and has changed following inquiry/consultation recommendations.

As of 27 August 2026, IBCA reported:
- 20,487 registrations of intent to claim;
- 6,321 people asked to start a claim;
- 5,785 claims started;
- 3,605 compensation offers;
- 3,512 people paid;
- £2,258,509,483.29 compensation paid.

These are current administrative figures, not a claim that the compensation process is complete, sufficient or free of dispute.

## Succession structure

The repair obligation is institutional rather than dependent on the personal identity or culpability of the current office-holder.

```text
PAST WRONG / HARM
-> PUBLIC INQUIRY / RECOGNITION
-> STATUTORY COMPENSATION OBLIGATION
-> CURRENT AUTHORITY / ADMINISTRATION
-> CLAIM / PAYMENT / REVIEW
-> SUCCESSOR PAYMENT ARRANGEMENTS
```

Current administrators did not need to be the original actors for the repair obligation to remain in force.

```text
SUCCESSOR_NOT_PERSONALLY_CULPABLE
!=
INSTITUTION_NO_LONGER_OBLIGATED
```

## Purpose can persist while mechanism changes

The scheme's operative mechanisms have changed:
- interim payments preceded the full scheme;
- regulations have been amended;
- eligibility/payment calculations have changed;
- previously paid claims may be recalculated;
- ongoing support-scheme payments are transferring into a new administrative body.

Yet the underlying repair purpose persists.

```text
HONOUR_REPAIR_COMMITMENT != FREEZE_REPAIR_METHOD
FIDELITY_TO_REPAIR_PURPOSE != FIDELITY_TO_FIRST_ADMINISTRATIVE_MECHANISM
```

The current public process gives a real example where changing the mechanism can be part of honouring the commitment rather than evidence that the commitment disappeared.

## Closure / correction boundary

A prior payment does not automatically mean a claim is permanently settled if later lawful scheme changes make additional compensation due.

```text
PAYMENT_MADE != EVERY FUTURE REVIEW CLOSED
RECORD / AWARD FINAL_AT_TIME_T != WORLD / RULES NEVER CHANGE
```

At the same time, this does not imply that every compensation decision should remain permanently reopenable. The applicable law and scheme rules remain the owner.

## Transition continuity

The planned transfer of legacy support schemes to IBCA is also a direct succession problem:

```text
OLD SUPPORT BODY
-> TRANSITION
-> NEW ADMINISTRATIVE OWNER

CONTINUITY OBJECT
= SUPPORT PAYMENT / BENEFICIARY RELATION
NOT
= ORGANISATIONAL FORM
```

IBCA's stated objective is no gap in support payments during the transition.

This is a concrete example of:

```text
DURABLE PURPOSE != INSTITUTION MUST EXIST FOREVER
ROLE / OBLIGATION CONTINUITY != ORGANISATIONAL IDENTITY CONTINUITY
```

## Strongest-owner boundary

The strongest owners are:
- the statutory compensation scheme and its regulations;
- IBCA;
- the Infected Blood Inquiry and its recommendations/history;
- courts and legal advisers for legal disputes;
- audit/parliamentary/public-law institutions for accountability.

The project has no standing to decide compensation levels, eligibility, legal liability or whether a particular claimant has received adequate redress.
