## Why this is in the quarry

Most recent world witnesses have been failure or post-failure correction cases.

This one is useful because some answerability/privacy/trust questions are being surfaced **before** national-scale deployment.

The September update proposes a Workplace Health System and related data/intelligence infrastructure, including a Work and Health Toolkit and a Workplace Health Intelligence Unit (WHIU).

This record does not assess the wider merits of the policy.

It isolates the design/governance mechanics that are relevant to the project.

## Constructive features described by the owner

The September update states that an employer data project will begin with information employers already hold and will:
- not require named or identifiable employee data initially;
- suppress or combine small-number results where necessary;
- test whether existing data can be collected consistently and proportionately;
- give participating employers early feedback on data quality, gaps and comparative performance.

For the proposed Work and Health Toolkit, the update says:
- questions should have a legitimate use and credible route to action;
- resulting employer/public-service insight would be aggregate and anonymised;
- citizens' panels are planned to establish conditions for trust / "social licence";
- the prototype will be tested with selected Vanguards;
- further validation, user research and external-partner testing would precede wider scaling depending on results.

For the proposed WHIU, the update says credibility would depend on:
- trusted stewardship rather than being perceived merely as departmental data collection;
- public reporting at least annually;
- governance including employers, employees and their representatives, disabled people, providers, places, academia and government.

It also explicitly names open questions, including whether people most likely to need help will participate and whether the value offered is worth their time.

These are useful pre-deployment design behaviours.

## Affected-group challenge

Disability Rights UK published a response the same day.

It welcomed the goal of more accessible and inclusive workplaces but raised implementation/governance concerns.

Its response says, among other things:
- it remains unclear how the proposed system will be funded and realised sustainably, particularly for smaller employers;
- the update contains limited concrete action aimed directly at helping people remain in work after acquiring impairments or health conditions;
- direct work with Disabled people and representative organisations to ensure measures are meaningful/effective did not appear sufficiently clear in the proposed plan;
- it called for stronger co-production and specific policy measures.

This should not be flattened into either:
- "affected groups reject the programme"; or
- "planned citizen panels prove co-production".

## The useful positive-construction pattern

The strongest project-relevant feature is **sequencing**.

Instead of:

~~~text
BUILD FULL SYSTEM
-> COLLECT DATA
-> DISCOVER TRUST / PRIVACY / USE PROBLEMS LATER
~~~

the published design at least attempts:

~~~text
DEFINE PURPOSE
-> MINIMISE / AGGREGATE INITIAL DATA
-> TEST QUESTIONS + VALUE
-> ENGAGE AFFECTED PEOPLE
-> TEST PROTOTYPE
-> ESTABLISH GOVERNANCE / REPORTING
-> VALIDATE
-> THEN CONSIDER SCALE
~~~

That sequence is an intention/design, not an observed outcome.

The affected-group critique is exactly why the distinction matters.

## Hostile checks

### 1. Aggregate output is not zero privacy risk

Even if employers receive aggregate/anonymised outputs:
- source data handling still matters;
- small groups can create re-identification risk;
- health/disability data are sensitive;
- governance, purpose limitation, access and retention still matter.

The project does not assess legal compliance here.

Preserve:

~~~text
AGGREGATE OUTPUT
!=
ZERO DATA-PROTECTION RISK
~~~

### 2. "Social licence" is not a substitute for legal rights

A citizen panel or trust process cannot waive:
- data-protection obligations;
- equality duties;
- employment rights;
- consent requirements where applicable;
- other legal/professional constraints.

Preserve:

~~~text
SOCIAL LICENCE
!=
LEGAL PERMISSION

LEGAL PERMISSION
!=
SOCIAL LEGITIMACY
~~~

### 3. Participation can be selective

The owner itself asks whether people most likely to need help may be least likely to take part.

That is an observation-boundary issue already captured by the frozen aperture card.

Do not create a second tool.

~~~text
PARTICIPANTS
!=
ALL AFFECTED PEOPLE
~~~

### 4. Data-to-action can still fail

A question can be legitimate and a dataset can be well governed while:
- the resulting insight is not acted on;
- action capacity is absent;
- incentives distort behaviour;
- support is unavailable.

The probation witness from the same COM cycle is a useful counterpressure.

~~~text
GOOD DATA
!=
GOOD ACTION

ACTIONABLE INSIGHT
!=
ACTION CAPACITY
~~~

## Stronger-owner boundary

Workplace health, disability employment, labour policy, equality law, occupational health, data protection, statistics, standards and worker representation are all mature domains.

Relevant stronger owners include:
- Disabled people and representative organisations;
- ICO / data-protection law;
- ACAS and employment-law/equality owners;
- occupational-health and workplace-health practitioners;
- BSI and other standards owners;
- labour-market/public-health researchers;
- trade unions and employer bodies;
- DWP/DBT/DHSC and devolved/local authorities for the public programme itself.

The project should not invent:
- a workplace health standard;
- a disability-employment policy;
- a lawful basis for health-data processing;
- a "social licence" scoring method;
- an employer surveillance system.
