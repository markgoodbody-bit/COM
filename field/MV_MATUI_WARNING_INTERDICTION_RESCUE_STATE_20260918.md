## Material current facts / attributed claims

The MV Matui sank on 11 September 2026 while travelling between Santo and Ambae.

As reported by AP on 18 September, VMSA said 53 people were on board; 14 survivors and two bodies had been recovered and the search ended with 37 people still missing. Other current government-linked reporting says the true number aboard may have been higher and that reliable crew/passenger records were unavailable. Treat the final manifest and toll as unresolved.

VMSA Commissioner Napuati is reported as saying:
- MV Matui was not classified as a passenger vessel and had not been authorised by VMSA to carry passengers on the voyage;
- VMSA staff warned management on 10 September against overloading and unauthorised passenger carriage;
- a marine warning was in force;
- the vessel departed despite a detention order;
- VMSA subsequently lodged a criminal complaint against the captain, owners and management.

These are current attributed regulatory claims, not this project's adjudication of criminal liability.

AP and ABC also report that the sinking was not reported promptly and that authorities lacked reliable initial passenger/crew information. The Vanuatu government has promised an independent investigation. Responsibility for VMSA was transferred to the Prime Minister's Office after the disaster amid criticism of the response.

## Existing structure activated

This case puts pressure on distinctions already present in TRACE / Mechanical Ethics:

- a route, rule or prohibition is not the same thing as practical control over the transition;
- authority recorded on paper is not necessarily authority successfully exercised at the relevant clock;
- a warning can be evidentially real yet fail to become effective protection;
- records needed after a disaster are part of operational capability, not merely retrospective documentation;
- later investigation and accountability cannot restore a pre-departure protection window that has closed.

The narrower residue worth carrying is:

> **protective authority should be inspected at the point where it must change the physical transition, not only at the point where it issues a warning, certificate or order.**

That is a field-reading question, not a new universal rule.
