# PoliceAI — assurance advice, deployment authority and withdrawal routing

Date: 27 September 2026

Status: **PUBLIC FIELD WITNESS / STRONGER OWNERS IDENTIFIED / NO TRACE OR ME PATCH / NO COMPLIANCE CONCLUSION**

## Why this case matters

A recent Parliamentary question put a narrow correction question to the Home Office: what happens if post-deployment monitoring shows a substantial accuracy decline, and who is accountable if a Chief Constable does not follow PoliceAI advice?

The published answer and the Home Office PoliceAI factsheet make several layers unusually legible.

## Observed public structure

Home Office factsheet, published 9 June 2026:
https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/police-use-of-artificial-intelligence-ai-factsheet/police-use-of-artificial-intelligence-ai-factsheet-accessible

The factsheet says, in substance:

- individual Chief Officers decide when and how AI is used in the forces they lead;
- they are accountable to locally elected Police and Crime Commissioners under the current structure;
- PoliceAI's AI Lab is intended to test, validate and benchmark tools so Chief Officers can make more informed procurement/deployment decisions;
- PoliceAI is explicitly not assigned ethical judgement over whether a tool should be used, nor operational decisions on model outputs;
- PoliceAI is expected to maintain a public registry describing operational tools and pre-deployment checks;
- other policing bodies retain cyber/security, standards, procurement and scientific-advice roles.

Parliamentary question 27656, answered 10 September 2026:
https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-09-07/27656

The question explicitly asked about a tool whose accuracy materially declines after deployment and about accountability when PoliceAI advice is not followed.

The answer names:
- responsible testing/deployment expectations;
- a forthcoming public registry including accountability arrangements;
- Chief Constable deployment/procurement discretion;
- accountability to PCCs;
- public/PCC scrutiny;
- Police Act 1996 sections 53 and 53A as backstop regulatory powers.

The answer does **not, on this published surface, state a tool-specific automatic withdrawal threshold, mandatory stop condition, or named correction clock** for the accuracy-decline scenario posed.

That absence from this answer is not evidence that no such mechanism exists elsewhere in law, force policy, contract, professional guidance or an individual deployment plan.

Related Parliamentary question 27659:
https://questions-statements.parliament.uk/written-questions/detail/2026-09-07/27659

It repeats the same current accountability structure when asked about putting PoliceAI standards on a statutory footing.

## Mechanical reading

The case separates functions that are easy to collapse:

    TEST / ASSURE / ADVISE
    !=
    DECIDE TO DEPLOY / CONTINUE / WITHDRAW

and:

    REGISTRY TRANSPARENCY
    !=
    CORRECTION EXECUTION

and:

    BACKSTOP POWER EXISTS
    !=
    PREDECLARED TRIGGER / CLOCK / ROUTE USED

At this layer:

- PoliceAI can produce evidence and advice;
- Chief Officers hold the stated local deployment decision;
- PCC/public scrutiny can observe and challenge at a governance layer;
- College/Home Secretary statutory powers are described as a backstop;
- the public answer does not connect a measured accuracy-decline event to a mandatory withdrawal action in one explicit route.
