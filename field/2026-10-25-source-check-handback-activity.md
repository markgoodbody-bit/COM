**Claim under examination:**

> A report predicts that global data-centre electricity demand will roughly double between 2025 and 2030.

You do not have to accept or reject that sentence from memory. Your task is to find out **what sort of claim it is**, its original basis, and whether the wording survives checking.

## First try — do this without an AI

1. **Stop and specify.** Underline the exact quantity (electricity demand), scope (global data centres), time span (2025 to 2030), and claim type (a *projection*, not an observed future).
2. **Investigate the source.** Search for the organisation responsible for the number. Who publishes the calculation, and when was its relevant report published? Does that organisation actually measure or model the thing in question?
3. **Find better coverage.** Open an unrelated reputable discussion or primary technical summary, preferably with its own evidence, and see whether it reports the same projection and assumptions. Two reposts of one press release are **not independent measurements**.
4. **Trace.** Visit the original source, not merely an article linking to it. Find the precise baseline and forecast numbers. Record the relevant sentence and distinguish **observed baseline**, **central forecast**, and **possible alternatives**.
5. **Own the conclusion.** In two sentences, write what the source supports and what it does not. If the source is inaccessible or contradictory, write **UNKNOWN** with the reason, not a forced yes/no.

Stop here before looking at the worked source below if you want the initial independent practice.

## Source check and likely interpretation

The [International Energy Agency's 2026 Key Questions on Energy and AI, executive summary](https://www.iea.org/reports/key-questions-on-energy-and-ai/executive-summary) forecasts global data-centre consumption increasing from approximately **485 TWh in 2025** to approximately **950 TWh in 2030** under its **central projection**. It also discusses physical bottlenecks and uncertainty in other scenarios. The figures support describing a **roughly doubling central projection**, not treating the 2030 outcome as already measured or guaranteed.

It would be an error to change this into "all AI usage doubles", "data centres already consumed 950 TWh" or "every country's data-centre demand doubles". It is a different, materially stronger statement to say "this energy use will necessarily make everyone's electricity bills rise". The IEA explicitly explains the effect depends on local electricity systems and policy choices.
