## Bounded source result

The UK government's Policy Insights Unit report, published 11 September 2026, examines delays in employers' handling of reasonable-adjustment requests.

The evidence base is explicitly limited and uneven:
- expert interviews;
- literature review;
- 66 Employment Tribunal judgments;
- Acas conciliator evidence;
- University of York survey work;
- other employee/employer surveys.

The report does **not** establish one national failure rate for adjustment requests.

It does establish a real process pattern:
- some requests are not acknowledged promptly;
- some decisions take more than a month, and a smaller share more than three months in the University of York sample;
- implementation can be delayed by specialist equipment, Access to Work or other external dependencies;
- managers and employees can disagree about what counts as reasonable and how frequently updates should occur;
- line managers can become de facto case managers without training;
- manager turnover can disrupt an adjustment route;
- poor or absent workplace procedures can leave unclear who is responsible for the next action.

The report also cuts against an intent-first explanation:
- intentional employer delay was raised by some participants;
- the research says it found little corroborating evidence that deliberate obstruction is the dominant mechanism;
- its overall conclusion places more weight on knowledge gaps, ambiguity, process weakness and structural pressure.

Acas separately advises employers to meet with the employee, understand the specific situation, avoid assumptions, consider options together, and make responsibility/process clear through policy where appropriate.

## Important limits

Do **not** infer:
- that most employers delay adjustment requests;
- that delay is usually intentional;
- that the cited survey percentages generalise to all UK workplaces;
- that every delayed request is unlawful;
- that one universal response deadline is established by the report;
- that the statutory framework is shown to have failed overall.

The report explicitly says the evidence is limited/patchy and that stakeholder views differ on the scale/significance of delay.
