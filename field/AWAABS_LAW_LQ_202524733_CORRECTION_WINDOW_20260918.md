## Source facts and chronology

The underlying repair history began before Awaab's Law.

- The resident had raised property-condition concerns including mould before the 27 October 2025 commencement date.
- The Ombudsman says the resident told the landlord about health vulnerabilities, including a heart condition, insomnia and PTSD.
- The Ombudsman records that the landlord was aware of reports that mould affected the breathing of the resident's son and that the resident had chest infections.
- Access was refused on several occasions. The Ombudsman also found the landlord failed to follow its own escalation procedure for repeated non-access.

Post-commencement facts material to this field case:

- A damp and mould inspection took place on **25 November 2025**, after Awaab's Law came into force.
- The Ombudsman says the landlord was on notice of mould and needed to take the extra steps required by the new legislation.
- Those steps included considering whether there was a potential emergency or significant hazard, household health/vulnerability, and whether temporary accommodation was needed.
- The Ombudsman says it could not be satisfied the landlord acted in line with its Awaab's Law obligations because it did not complete those assessments.
- Additional gutter and brickwork jobs were raised following the November inspection.
- The underlying guttering/brickwork was completed on **24 March 2026**, which the Ombudsman records as **82 working days after the damp and mould inspection**, without the updates required by the landlord's damp/mould policy.
- The Ombudsman says it took **18 months from the complaint** to resolve the damp and mould.
- The resident said she could not use the back bedroom until March 2026. The Ombudsman had evidence that mould was present but did **not** have evidence establishing that the bedrooms or property were uninhabitable.

Determination:

- maladministration in handling the property-condition concerns;
- service failure in handling rehousing concerns;
- reasonable redress in complaint handling.

The Ombudsman made orders including apology/compensation and further action on outstanding windows/doors.

## Important procedural/function split

The Ombudsman found the stage-2 complaint response was made in line with the landlord's complaint policy, and found reasonable redress for complaint handling.

At the same time it found maladministration in the handling of the underlying property condition.

That gives a real bounded example of:

```text
COMPLAINT_PROCESS_TIMELY_AT_STAGE_2
!=
HAZARD_CORRECTION_TIMELY_OR_COMPLETE

PROCEDURAL_ROUTE_FUNCTIONED_IN_PART
!=
LIVED_PROBLEM_FUNCTIONED_AS_REPAIRED
```

This does not mean the complaint process was useless. It means complaint-process performance and hazard-resolution performance are different claims.

## Awaab's Law orientation

Current government guidance says, for potential significant hazards, landlords must investigate within 10 working days of becoming aware; provide a written summary within 3 working days after the investigation; complete relevant safety work within 5 working days where a significant hazard is confirmed; and begin or take steps to begin supplementary preventative work within 5 working days, subject to specified practical limits/backstops.

For emergency hazards, the investigation and relevant safety work are generally within 24 hours. If the property cannot be made safe within the required period, suitable alternative accommodation must be offered while required safety work is completed.

The guidance also requires attention to resident circumstances and supports renewed/further investigation where needed.

This field record does **not** independently adjudicate which statutory deadline applied to each L&Q action. The Housing Ombudsman's published determination is the source for its conclusion that the required Awaab assessments were not completed.
