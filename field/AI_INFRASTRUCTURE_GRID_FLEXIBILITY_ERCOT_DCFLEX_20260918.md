## Why this case

Recent project work has been comparatively abstract/institutional. This case tests the material layer underneath increasingly capable AI:
- electricity demand;

It is not a claim that all data centres are AI facilities or that all AI infrastructure creates the same grid effect.

## Current owner evidence

ERCOT's June 2026 voltage ride-through assessment says four groups of Large Loads with insufficient ride-through capability could produce load loss exceeding 3,200 MW, the threshold ERCOT identifies as necessary to maintain grid-frequency stability following a disturbance. ERCOT says the identified risk is primarily due to Large Computational Loads.

Source:
https://www.ercot.com/services/comm/mkt_notices/M-A013126-02

Texas has also replaced its older project-by-project study process for qualifying large loads with a Batch Zero framework for loads of 75 MW and above. The framework studies groups together, allocates available grid capacity and identifies transmission upgrades. Data-centre projects in Batch Zero are also subject to an active verification process before advancing.

Sources:
https://www.ercot.com/news/release/06182026-puct-approves-ercots
https://www.ercot.com/services/comm/mkt_notices/M-A090926-01

DOE's 2026 draft National Transmission Needs Study likewise identifies new large loads including data centres as a major reason additional transmission is required.

Source:
https://www.energy.gov/oe/articles/does-office-electricity-publishes-2026-draft-national-transmission-needs-study

## Positive construction / flexibility side

The same physical load can sometimes be operated differently.

Google reports that by March 2026 it had integrated 1 GW of data-centre demand-response capacity into long-term utility contracts, allowing portions of machine-learning workloads to be shifted or reduced when grids need it.

Source:
https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/infrastructure-and-cloud/global-network/demand-response-data-center-milestone/

EPRI's DCFlex programme reports nine active demonstration sites testing how data centres can flex in real time to support reliability and faster grid integration.

Source:
https://dcflex.epri.com/

PJM already operates voluntary demand-response markets/programmes that compensate end-use customers for reducing load during high-price or reliability-stressed periods.

Source:
https://www.pjm.com/markets-and-operations/demand-response.aspx?p=1

## What this case adds to the project

It grounds an existing Open Horizon warning: correction, care and capability are physical-resource dependent.

A decision about AI capability may change:
- MW of load requested;
- transmission/generation build required;
- local reliability exposure;
- hardware/cooling/resource demand;
- the feasible timing/location of deployment;
- whether the load can itself become a flexible grid resource.
