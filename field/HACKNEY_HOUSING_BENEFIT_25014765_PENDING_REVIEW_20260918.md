## Why this case was selected

This case tests a different correction pattern from the Awaab/housing-repair field case.

Here the formal correction route existed and ultimately succeeded, but the contested action continued while correction was pending.

```text
DECISION
-> REVIEW / APPEAL REQUESTED
-> ORIGINAL ACTION CONTINUES
-> DELAY
-> REVIEW SUCCEEDS
-> MONEY REFUNDED / DEBT WRITTEN OFF
-> INTERIM HARDSHIP ALREADY OCCURRED
```

Selection does not establish representativeness of Hackney, housing benefit administration or benefit reviews generally.

## Source chronology

- January 2023: Council notified Ms B of a housing-benefit overpayment, initially around £3,300, and said it would recover it.
- Early February 2023: Ms B appealed/reviewed the decision, arguing the debt was irrecoverable because Council errors affected the income calculation; she also requested an explanation.
- During the following period, the Council sent further decisions increasing the amount said to be owed to around £6,000.
- Throughout the dispute/review period the Council continued recovering the debt.
- 28 November 2024: after Ms B complained to her local councillor, the Council decided the review in her favour.
- 12 January 2025: Council confirmed it would write off the entire debt and refunded more than £1,600 already recovered.
- September 2025: Ms B complained to the Ombudsman, saying the Council had never directly apologised or explained the delay.
- December 2025: Council agreed to £200 symbolic payment after Ombudsman involvement.
- 19 April 2026: Ombudsman final decision.

## Ombudsman findings

The Ombudsman found:

- excessive delay in deciding the review and writing off the debt;
- Ms B had to chase the Council for information/explanation for nearly two years;
- during that delay she had no opportunity to pursue an independent appeal;
- continued recovery while the overpayment was disputed was fault;
- the Ombudsman considered the Council should suspend debt recovery while an appeal is ongoing;
- the delay caused significant distress;
- continued recovery caused financial hardship;
- failure to apologise/explain exacerbated the injustice.

Remedy included apology, £200 symbolic payment and reasons for delay.
