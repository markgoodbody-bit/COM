## Current public facts

Barnet Council reports that the first community solar installation on a council-owned property in the borough has been completed at Burnt Oak Leisure Centre.

Reported project structure:
- 220 rooftop solar panels;
- the leisure centre is expected to use more than 70% of generated electricity, with the remainder exported to the grid;
- Barnet Council owns the building;
- GLL operates the leisure centre;
- Community Energy Barnet is a community benefit society led by volunteer Barnet residents;
- Repowering London and Carbon3 are delivery/technical partners;
- grant funding came from Barnet Council, the GLA community-energy fund and the Energy Industry Voluntary Redress Scheme;
- a future community share offer is planned to allow residents to invest directly in the asset;
- surplus income is expected to support a future Community Benefit Fund.

Barnet Council reports expected lifetime savings and carbon reductions. These are projections, not observed realised outcomes in this field record.

```text
PROJECT COMPLETED != BENEFITS REALISED
EXPECTED SAVING != OBSERVED SAVING
EXPECTED CARBON REDUCTION != MEASURED LIFETIME REDUCTION
PLANNED SHARE OFFER != COMMUNITY OWNERSHIP ALREADY COMPLETE
```

## Strongest-owner boundary

Community energy, local-government asset management, energy regulation, finance, engineering, grid connection and cooperative/community-benefit governance have mature specialist owners.

This case does not earn a project energy model or governance schema.

## Why this is a useful positive witness

The case represents a different structure from individual rescue/correction cases.

```text
COUNCIL ASSET / SITE AUTHORITY
+ COMMUNITY BENEFIT SOCIETY
+ LEISURE OPERATOR / LOAD
+ TECHNICAL DELIVERY
+ GRANT CAPITAL
+ FUTURE COMMUNITY INVESTMENT
-> SHARED ENERGY ASSET
```

No one actor alone supplies every required condition.

That gives a real field example of joint construction under differentiated roles:
- site access/control is separate from community organisation;
- operation/use is separate from asset/project organisation;
- engineering is separate from governance;
- capital comes from multiple routes;
- future community participation is planned through a share offer rather than inferred from technical benefit;
- surplus can be directed into a community benefit fund without implying that every resident has identical interests or control.
