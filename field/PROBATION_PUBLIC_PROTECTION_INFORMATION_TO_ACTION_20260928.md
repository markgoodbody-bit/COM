## Scope

HM Inspectorate of Probation reviewed public-protection work across all 12 probation regions in England and Wales.

The national programme covered:
- 858 inspected cases;
- 711 case-inspection interviews with practitioners;
- assessment, planning, implementation/delivery and review through a public-protection lens.

This is a bounded inspection sample and programme.

Do not convert its percentages into a population prevalence claim about all probation cases.

## What the owner found

The Inspectorate reports progress in strategic leadership, governance, quality assurance, workforce development and some partnership arrangements.

But it also reports that those improvements had **not translated consistently into effective frontline delivery**.

Information-sharing illustrates the propagation problem.

### Police information

The Inspectorate reports improved police information-sharing overall.

However:
- in 18% of inspected relevant cases, domestic-abuse information received from police was insufficiently detailed to support meaningful risk assessment;
- quality, timeliness and completeness remained variable.

### Children's services information

The weakness was greater for children's-services information.

The Inspectorate reports:
- operational arrangements were inconsistent and often unreliable;
- in nearly a third of relevant cases (31%), information received was of insufficient quality to meaningfully inform risk assessment;
- effective exchange often depended on local relationships and individual persistence rather than dependable systems.

### Information already available

A separate failure occurred **after** information had arrived.

The Inspectorate reports that in 54% of inspected cases, critical available information was not sufficiently interrogated, analysed or translated into robust risk assessment and management activity.

During 2026, regional inspection reports repeatedly recommended a national strategic approach to information-sharing with police and children's services.

## Positive control

The Inspectorate's effective-practice material supplies a positive control.

Its CHEH example describes:
- professional curiosity;
- effective information-sharing;
- clear accountability;
- coordinated intervention;
- prompt escalation into MAPPA, safeguarding and recall where required;
- regular review of emerging risk.

The Inspectorate says the approach improved identification and management of harm by making risk understandable, coordinating intervention and acting in time.
