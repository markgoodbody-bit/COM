## What the Citizens Advice source says

Citizens Advice identifies three mechanisms of harm across essential services:

1. **two-tier support** — erosion of phone, face-to-face and paper channels can leave digitally excluded people with slower / less reliable support;
2. **channel lock** — people can become stuck in either online or offline support when they need to move between them;
3. **poor digital design** — friction and poor design can make access difficult enough that people give up.

The report spans essential-service contexts including:
- energy;
- water;
- broadband/mobile;
- Universal Credit;
- local-authority housing;
- other essential support.

Citizens Advice proposes three cross-sector principles:
- a right to talk to a human by phone/in person in a reasonable amount of time when needed;
- a right to move between online and offline support;
- a right to a decent digital service rather than looping chatbots / failing forms.

It also explicitly says frontline digital-support services are only part of the solution: service providers themselves need to change design.

## Structural pressure

The report sharpens a route issue that can be missed if each channel is assessed separately.

A person can have:
- an online route;
- a phone route;
- perhaps a face-to-face route;

while still being unable to **move between** them when circumstances change.

Preserve:

~~~text
ROUTE A EXISTS
+
ROUTE B EXISTS
!=
SWITCH A -> B IS USABLE

MULTIPLE CHANNELS LISTED
!=
CHANNEL CHOICE IS PRACTICALLY REACHABLE

HUMAN SUPPORT EXISTS
!=
HUMAN SUPPORT REACHABLE IN TIME

DIGITALLY CONFIDENT USER
!=
DIGITAL ROUTE USABLE
~~~

This is "channel lock" as an owner term, not a project invention.

## Stronger-owner subtraction

### Financial services

FCA Consumer Duty already requires firms to design/deliver support that:
- meets retail customers' needs, including vulnerability;
- avoids unreasonable barriers;
- works across customer-support channels.

FCA policy/guidance on non-standard issues explicitly recognizes that some complex/sensitive journeys may require a real-time human interface.

FCA's September 2026 vulnerable-payments-firm review also treats:
- digital exclusion as a vulnerability driver;
- identification across online/automated channels;
- routing some customers from automated/chat interactions to human agents;
as relevant good-practice territory.

Therefore:

~~~text
RIGHT / NEED FOR EFFECTIVE HUMAN HANDOFF
!=
UNOWNED FINANCIAL-SERVICES PROBLEM
~~~

### Energy

Ofgem's Contact Ease guidance already expects:
- customers to be able to identify contact methods easily;
- contact methods that meet customer needs;
- appropriate response in good time;
- accessible communication for vulnerable customers.

Current Ofgem customer-service data also explicitly measures perceived ease/difficulty of contacting suppliers.

Therefore the Citizens Advice report is not evidence that every sector lacks rules.

It is evidence of **cross-sector real-world access failures despite uneven existing protections**.

## Potential representation question for TRACE

TRACE already has:
- route usability;

- target/time relativity;

- route/capability constraints.

The Citizens Advice case does **not** currently show that TRACE cannot represent channel lock.

## Mechanical Ethics pressure

ME already says:
- a written route is not necessarily usable;
- digital systems can improve access but existence of one route does not establish realistic opportunity to use it;
- complexity should be carried by actors with more power/capacity rather than exported.

The current report adds a fresh real owner case, not a missing ME concept.

## Smallest design question that survives

Not:

> add a human to every automated service.

Instead:

> when the current channel stops being usable for this person's situation, is there a reachable handoff to another appropriate channel before the relevant consequence hardens?

And, where the stronger owner requires continuity:

> does the handoff preserve enough case state that switching channels does not simply restart the burden?

The second question is a project inference from "seamless channel swaps"; it is not presented here as a direct Citizens Advice finding unless independently evidenced.

## Anti-drift

Do not infer:
