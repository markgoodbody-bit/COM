# Shared Capability Construction Card v0.1

Status: **NON-CANONICAL FIELD CARD / EXTERNAL-OWNER ROUTER / NOT TRACE OR ME / NOT NOVELTY CLAIM**  
Date: 2026-09-03  
Origin case: COM #90 — Great British Energy Local Power Plan

## Purpose

Use this card when public, expert, philanthropic, technical or institutional support is intended to create a capability that should remain useful after the initial helper steps back.

The card does **not** ask whether the recipient becomes self-sufficient. Real projects remain interdependent. It asks whether support leaves the recipient better able to act, choose, govern, reproduce useful work and navigate or replace critical dependencies.

```text
HELPER_STEPS_BACK != ALL_EXTERNAL_RELATIONS_END
CAPABILITY_CREATED != DEPENDENCE_ELIMINATED
INTERDEPENDENCE != CAPTIVITY
```

## External owners to credit first

This card is a compression of problems already substantially owned elsewhere.

- Great British Energy / DESNZ Local Power Plan: public finance, capability/capacity support, shared ownership, scalable business models and local/community ownership.
  - https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/local-power-plan/local-power-plan-accessible-webpage
- Community Energy England / Energy Learning Network: peer mentoring, technical support, skills transfer, training and sector learning for new and growing groups.
  - https://communityenergyengland.org/about/projects/energy-learning-network/
- Community energy governance practice: governance, widening participation and succession planning so knowledge/leadership survive turnover.
  - https://communityenergyengland.org/guidance/how-to-guides/business-governance-and-financial-management/
- Local-authority/community collaboration guidance: public institutions and community groups can combine complementary capacities rather than treating local control as public withdrawal.
  - https://communityenergyengland.org/publications/collaborating-on-community-energy/
- Wider capacity-development practice: external assistance can itself suppress local capacity if it permanently substitutes for it; exit/transition strategy and institutional capacity are established concerns.

Do not use this card to rename those traditions as project discoveries.

## The card

For one supported entity, organisation, community or partnership, answer only what matters to the next consequential design decision.

### 1. What useful thing exists afterward?

Name the concrete output or service created.

Examples: an energy asset, functioning service, trained team, legal vehicle, financing route, maintenance process, data capability, community programme.

Do not count an application, workshop, plan or governance document as capability merely because it was completed.

### 2. What can the recipient now do again?

Identify repeatable capability that remains after the immediate project.

Ask whether the recipient can now, with materially less external substitution:

- identify and shape another opportunity;
- obtain and challenge specialist advice;
- prepare or commission feasibility work;
- negotiate finance/contracts;
- make and record governing decisions;
- deliver, operate, maintain or monitor the result;
- recognise when it needs outside help and obtain it.

A successful first project with no repeatable capability may still be worthwhile. Do not mislabel it as capacity building.

### 3. What governing power changed?

Separate organisational competence from practical control.

Ask:

- Who can set priorities?
- Who can approve material commitments?
- Who can see the evidence and accounts?
- Who can challenge or replace leaders/providers?
- Who can alter benefit distribution?
- Which affected/local people remain outside meaningful participation?

```text
ORGANISATION_MORE_CAPABLE != GOVERNING_POWER_MORE_BROADLY_SHARED
OWNERSHIP_STAKE != EFFECTIVE_CONTROL
```

### 4. Which critical functions are still externally held?

List only dependencies that can materially stop, redirect or capture the work.

Possible functions include capital, grid/market access, legal advice, technical design, procurement, data, operating expertise, land, permissions, insurance, accounting and public authority.

For each critical dependency ask:

- Is the provider replaceable in practice?
- Can the recipient understand enough to challenge it?
- Is there an alternative route if it fails or exploits its position?
- Does the dependency disappear, reduce, remain by design, or become more concentrated after support?

The target is not isolation. A healthy outcome may be durable interdependence with contestable and replaceable external functions.

### 5. Can the capability survive succession?

Ask what happens when the trained founder, officer, volunteer, adviser or public sponsor leaves.

Look for:

- knowledge transferred beyond one person;
- future leaders or operators being developed;
- records usable by successors;
- governance that can replace office-holders;
- participation that can widen rather than harden into an incumbent circle.

```text
CAPABILITY_HELD_BY_ONE_PERSON != DURABLE_ORGANISATIONAL_CAPABILITY
```

### 6. When should the helper step back — and when should it return?

State a transition condition rather than an arbitrary calendar date.

Possible evidence for stepping back includes the recipient independently completing previously substituted functions, choosing competent providers, sustaining governance, raising/servicing finance, or completing another cycle with bounded support.

Also preserve a route for renewed support when circumstances materially change. Successful formation does not require abandonment.

### 7. Who paid for the learning, and who gained power from it?

Capacity formation consumes time, failed attempts, volunteer effort, professional attention, money and political/social credibility.

Ask:

- Who absorbed failed-project costs?
- Who had enough spare time/money/status to participate?
- Did support strengthen already-professionalised incumbents faster than new entrants?
- Did standardisation make entry easier while privileging one organisational form?
- Did an expert intermediary accumulate durable gatekeeping power?

### 8. What would demonstrate that capability was actually created?

Choose a few observable before/after tests rather than one composite score.

Examples:

- a second opportunity originated without the original helper;
- a specialist recommendation was independently questioned or replaced;
- leadership/operating responsibility transferred successfully;
- a new participant entered a governing role;
- another finance/contract cycle completed with less substituted work;
- a material problem was detected and corrected locally;
- an external dependency became more replaceable or answerable.

Preserve adverse evidence. If the same helper still has to perform every critical function, record that plainly even when the first project succeeded.

## Use in the Local Power Plan case

The card does not prescribe DEPLOY FIRST, CAPABILITY FIRST or DUAL PORTFOLIO.

It adds a narrower programme-design requirement: if a funding/support lane claims to build community capability, its evaluation should distinguish at least:

1. project/assets delivered;
2. repeatable organisational capability retained;
3. practical governing/control capability retained or widened;
4. critical dependencies still externally held.

These outcomes may legitimately trade off. Do not hide them in one score.

## Strongest falsifiers

Delete or shrink this card if:

- community-energy/public-investment practice already provides a smaller usable instrument carrying the same distinctions;
- users cannot complete it without theory ingestion;
- it turns every worthwhile one-off project into a failure for not creating a self-sufficient organisation;
- it treats all continuing external dependence as bad rather than testing contestability/replaceability;
- it rewards organisational professionalisation while ignoring participation/capture;
- it becomes another checklist that does not change programme design, monitoring or exit decisions.

## Provisional portable residue

If anything travels beyond energy, it is this question:

> **After support, what can the recipient now do, decide, reproduce and hand on — and which critical functions can it still neither perform, challenge nor replace?**

That is a candidate construction question, not a project primitive.
