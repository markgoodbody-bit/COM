# Shared Capability Construction Card v0.2

Status: **NON-CANONICAL FIELD CARD / EXTERNAL-OWNER ROUTER / NOT TRACE OR ME / NOT NOVELTY CLAIM**  
Date: 2026-09-03  
Origin case: COM #90 — Great British Energy Local Power Plan

## Use

Use when support is supposed to leave behind more than one completed project.

Do **not** ask whether the recipient becomes self-sufficient. Real capability often depends on finance, specialists, public authority, markets and partners. Ask whether those dependencies become understandable, challengeable and replaceable enough that the recipient is not captive to the original helper.

```text
CAPABILITY_CREATED != DEPENDENCE_ELIMINATED
INTERDEPENDENCE != CAPTIVITY
```

## Six questions

### 1. What useful thing exists?

Name the actual output or service. Do not count a workshop, application, plan or governance document as capability merely because it was completed.

### 2. What can the recipient now do again?

What useful action can it repeat with materially less substitution by the original helper?

Examples: originate another opportunity; commission and challenge specialist advice; negotiate finance/contracts; govern a material decision; operate/maintain the result; detect a problem and obtain the right help.

```text
FIRST_PROJECT_SUCCEEDED != REPEATABLE_CAPABILITY_CREATED
```

### 3. What practical control changed?

Who can now set priorities, approve commitments, inspect evidence/accounts, replace leaders/providers, and alter benefit distribution?

Also ask who remains outside meaningful participation.

```text
ORGANISATION_MORE_CAPABLE != GOVERNING_POWER_MORE_BROADLY_SHARED
OWNERSHIP_STAKE != EFFECTIVE_CONTROL
```

### 4. Which critical functions remain external?

List only dependencies that can materially stop, redirect or capture the work: capital, technical/legal expertise, market/grid access, data, land, permissions, procurement, insurance, accounting or public authority.

For each: can the recipient understand it enough to challenge it, replace the provider in practice, or reach an alternative route if it fails?

Healthy support may end in durable **interdependence**, not independence.

### 5. Can the capability survive succession?

What happens when the trained founder, officer, volunteer, expert or sponsor leaves?

Look for knowledge beyond one person, usable records, replaceable office-holders, future leaders/operators and participation that can widen rather than harden into an incumbent circle.

### 6. What evidence lets the helper step back?

Use a transition condition, not an arbitrary date.

Possible evidence: the recipient completes a previously substituted function; chooses or replaces a competent provider; completes another project cycle with less substituted work; transfers leadership successfully; or detects and corrects a material problem locally.

Preserve a route for support to return when circumstances materially change. Successful formation does not require abandonment.

## One before/after record

For a support programme that claims capability-building, keep these outcomes separate rather than combining them into one score:

1. **Output** — what useful asset/service exists?
2. **Repeatability** — what can the recipient do again?
3. **Control** — what material decisions can it make or contest?
4. **Dependency** — which critical functions remain externally held and how captive are they?
5. **Succession** — does the capability survive turnover and widen participation?

Also record who paid for failed learning and whether support disproportionately strengthened already-professionalised incumbents.

## Falsifier

Delete or shrink this card if an external owner already offers a smaller usable instrument carrying the same distinctions, or if using it does not change a real support, monitoring, governance or exit decision.

## Portable residue

> **After support, what can the recipient now do, decide, reproduce and hand on — and which critical functions can it still neither perform, challenge nor replace?**

Candidate construction question only. Not a project primitive.
