# Data-centre infrastructure cost allocation — who carries the expansion burden? — 18 September 2026

Status: **PUBLIC OWNER-SOURCE ECONOMIC BURDEN WITNESS / OWNER CONVERGENCE / NO NEW ECONOMIC FRAMEWORK**

> HOW CAN WE MAKE A BETTER FUTURE?

## Question

Large computational facilities can require generation, transmission and distribution investment before their full demand materialises.

The consequential question is not only:
`CAN THE GRID SERVE THE LOAD?`

It is also:
`WHO PAYS FOR CAPACITY BUILT AROUND THE LOAD, INCLUDING IF THE LOAD ARRIVES LATE OR NEVER FULLY MATERIALISES?`

## Current owner mechanisms

### Virginia State Corporation Commission

Virginia's SCC says its current large-load safeguards are designed so hyperscale data centres pay the costs incurred to serve them and to minimise cost shifting to other customer classes.

Current measures include:
- a separate GS-5 class for customers meeting large-load thresholds;
- alternative cost-allocation proposals for generation and transmission;
- a minimum 14-year service obligation for new qualifying large loads;
- minimum monthly transmission/distribution charges even if actual usage is lower;
- collateral requirements for some new customers;
- review of large-load interconnection processes.

Owner:
https://www.scc.virginia.gov/about-the-scc/scc-facts/

### Georgia Public Service Commission

Georgia PSC says its large-load rules require new >100 MW customers to bear site-specific and upstream generation/transmission/distribution costs and allow longer contract/minimum-billing terms to reduce the risk that ordinary customers are left with stranded infrastructure costs.

The December 2025 generation agreement also includes a financial backstop and remediation routes if expected data-centre contracts do not materialise.

Owners:
https://psc.ga.gov/site/downloads/datacenterfactsheet.pdf
https://psc.ga.gov/site/assets/files/8617/media_advisory_data_centers_rule_1-23-2025.pdf

### FERC

In June 2026 FERC ordered regional grid operators to justify or reform large-load connection tariffs with explicit reliability, transparency and consumer-protection goals.

FERC's public explanation names Cost Recovery Agreements as a mechanism intended to prevent residential customers being left to pay for infrastructure built for a large load that does not arrive as expected.

## Relation to Mechanical Ethics

Mechanical Ethics already frames kindness partly as deliberate placement of burden and asks who carries cost/delay when power is unequal.

This owner practice supplies a concrete economic example:
- identify who drives a new cost;
- identify who would absorb it under the default arrangement;
- change the contract/rate architecture so the party creating the special burden carries more of its downside risk;
- retain oversight because cost attribution itself can be contested.

This does not validate ME. Public-utility cost causation/allocation is a mature specialist field.

```text
OWNER PRACTICE ILLUSTRATES BURDEN PLACEMENT
```

## Evidence ceilings

Do not infer:
- that all current/future residential cost shifting has been eliminated;
- that a regulator's stated protection goal guarantees the eventual bill outcome;
- that every data centre creates the same infrastructure cost;
- that data-centre economic benefits are absent;
- that cost causation is simple or uncontested;
- that one rate design is ethically optimal.
