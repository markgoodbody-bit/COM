## Abstract

**Question.** The UK Algorithmic Transparency Recording Standard asks publishers to consider challenges to both algorithmic-tool output and broader operational-process output. We ask whether the live public records make a stated challenge/review/correction route legible as a relation: what can it act on, who can use it, and how?

## 1. The concrete problem

GDS guidance distinguishes two possible challenge sites:

```text
ALGORITHMIC TOOL OUTPUT
          |
          | challenge / review / correction?
          v
BROADER OPERATIONAL PROCESS OUTPUT
```

A published record may mention a route without telling the reader which object that route can change.

Our measurement asks:

```text
STATED ROUTE
   |
   +-> WHO CAN USE IT?
   +-> HOW IS IT INITIATED?
   +-> WHAT DOES IT ACT ON?
   +-> WHAT EFFECT IS STATED?
```

## 2. Prior work and contribution boundary

Existing research already establishes that:
- algorithm registers can provide visibility without full accountability/contestability context;
- register design can hide sociotechnical structure;
- public registers can be externally audited against transparency goals;
- contestability can occur at multiple sites in an AI-enabled process.

Therefore our contribution is **not** a new theory of contestability or a general critique of registers.

The narrower contribution under test is:

> a complete source-bound measurement of whether live UK ATRS records implement the standard owner's own challenge/review distinction in a publicly bindable route→target→actor→initiation relation.
