## Strong prior work now controlling

### Das et al. — Canadian federal AI Register, full dataset

**Bureaucratic Silences: What the Canadian AI Register Reveals, Omits, and Obscures**  
FAccT 2026.

The authors analyze the complete Canadian federal AI Register dataset of 409 systems using ADMAPS and deductive qualitative coding. Their conclusion explicitly contrasts visibility with contestability and argues that register design can obscure human discretion, training, uncertainty and other sociotechnical context.

Canonical DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1145/3805689.3812336

This owns much of any broad claim that:
```text
REGISTER VISIBILITY != ACCOUNTABILITY
REGISTER DISCLOSURE CAN HIDE SOCIOTECHNICAL CONTEXT
VISIBILITY != CONTESTABILITY
COMPLETE-REGISTER EMPIRICAL AUDIT = NEW IN ITSELF
```

### de Troya et al. — registers and sociotechnical accountability

**Co-constructing sociotechnical AI governance: participatory system mapping using algorithm registers** (2026 preprint).

This work asks what algorithm registers reveal/occlude about sociotechnical systems and reports that participatory system mapping surfaced hazards not visible from the register alone, including inability to contest wrongful decisions.

This owns more of the generic:
```text
REGISTER ALONE != COMPLETE ACCOUNTABILITY MAP
CONTESTATION MAY BE OCCLUDED BY REGISTER REPRESENTATION
```

### Peljto et al. — audit of German transparency initiatives

**Are Algorithm Registers Transparent? Perspectives from Germany** (2026).

The paper repurposes transparency goals into structured audit checklists and applies them externally to two German transparency initiatives, deriving concrete improvement actions.

This owns more of:
```text
ALGORITHM REGISTER CAN BE AUDITED AGAINST EXPLICIT TRANSPARENCY GOALS
STRUCTURED REGISTER AUDIT != OUR GENERAL CONTRIBUTION
```

## Narrow surviving competition delta

The current GDS ATRS guidance contains an unusually explicit implementation requirement for the Appeals and review disclosure:

> publishers should consider both the algorithmic-tool output and whether it can be challenged/appealed, and the broader operational-process output and whether it can be challenged/appealed.

#364 does **not** propose that distinction.

It asks whether current UK public records implement it legibly.

The candidate delta is therefore:

> **A source-bound census of whether each stated challenge/review/correction route in the live UK ATRS register is publicly bound to the thing it can act on, to the actor who may initiate/use it, and to the stated initiation/channel — preserving missing relations as missing rather than completing the graph from coder knowledge.**

That is narrower than the Canadian/German work above.

The potentially distinctive elements are their combination:
1. direct measurement against the **standard owner's own two-site disclosure instruction**;
2. proposition/relation-level coding rather than an overall register/accountability score;
3. explicit `CODER_CAN_INFER != RECORD_MAKES_LEGIBLE` fail-closed binding;
4. separation of:
   - route existence;
   - target site;
   - eligible actor;
   - initiation/channel;
   - internal review;
   - no-route-stated;
   - route effectiveness;
5. full evidence trace from any population count to exact public text;
6. no inference from disclosure quality to legal/compliance/real-world remedy quality.
