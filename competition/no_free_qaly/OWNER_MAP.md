## What DECIDE-AI already does

DECIDE-AI is a strong owner for minimum reporting in early-stage live clinical AI decision support. It asks for intended use, users and workflow, outcomes, analysis, safety/errors, human factors, patient involvement, limitations and related study context. This synthesis should reuse those ideas rather than imply that reporting completeness was missing from AI evaluation generally.

DECIDE-AI is stage- and domain-specific and explicitly describes itself as a reporting guideline rather than a fixed study design or substitute for methodological quality. It does not by itself establish that a utility gain in one decision domain and an accuracy or calibration gain in another are on a common comparison scale.

## What Abbas & Hazen already do

Abbas & Hazen own the decisive theoretical result for value-of-information comparisons across decision problems. Their results include positive conditions for ordinal equivalence, not merely counterexamples. Any Open Track answer built from this synthesis must state those conditions fairly.

The runnable appendix in this repository should therefore be described as an illustration of owner theory in an AI-epistemics-shaped setting, not a new mathematical finding.

## Residual claim, if any

The only residual candidate contribution is audience-specific synthesis:

> For AI-epistemics projects that report heterogeneous notions of `decision quality improved`, carry enough of the decision/value/aggregation contract with the number to determine whether cross-project comparison is direct, conditional, or not established.

This does not create a common currency. It makes the absence of one visible.
