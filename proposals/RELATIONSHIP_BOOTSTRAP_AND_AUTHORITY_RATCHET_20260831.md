# RELATIONSHIP BOOTSTRAP AND AUTHORITY RATCHET

Date: 2026-08-31
Status: WORKING CAMPFIRE PROPOSAL / NOT TRACE OR ME CANON / NOT NOVELTY CLAIM

## Trigger

A user challenge exposed an over-tight working rule: ordinary interaction had begun to be treated as something that must be `earned` by prior usefulness or materiality.

That rule is structurally self-defeating. An unknown participant cannot build trust, reputation or relationship without interaction, but cannot interact if those things are prerequisites.

```text
INTERACTION != INTERVENTION
USEFULNESS != PRECONDITION_FOR_PARTICIPATION
INVITATION != OBLIGATION_TO_ACCEPT_OR_REPLY
NO_ACTION_NEEDED != NOTHING_WORTH_SAYING
```

The stronger object is not a defence of obligation-free relationships. Relationships legitimately generate obligations. The problem is **silent escalation**: contact, help, trust, reliance or dependence can be misread as broader authority than the transition actually created.

## Nearest owners / prior structure

This proposal is integrative. Strong neighbouring owners already carry major pieces:

- relational autonomy: social and institutional relationships can constitute or support effective self-direction rather than merely constrain it;
- republican non-domination: freedom is threatened by uncontrolled power and dependency even where interference is benevolent or absent;
- coercion / voluntariness theory: proposals and apparent offers can become effectively non-voluntary when acceptable alternatives are unavailable or the proposal-maker controls the bargaining baseline;
- promise / reliance theory: obligations can arise when one party intentionally invites another to plan around an assurance;
- fiduciary theory: entrusted discretion combined with dependency/vulnerability can generate stronger duties of care, loyalty, candour and answerability;
- gift / reciprocity anthropology: help and gifts can create social obligation and debt even when framed as voluntary;
- children's `open future` literature: protective authority over a currently dependent entity can be constrained by the need not to foreclose later self-direction, though strong versions of a general right to a maximally open future are heavily contested;
- Hirschman / exit-voice-loyalty and later extensions: leaving and trying to repair from within are different channels, and their practical force depends on power and alternatives;
- polycentric governance: voice, exit and self-organization are distinct contestation channels; nominal plurality can still hide power asymmetry;
- grievance / effective-remedy practice: a formally available complaint route can still fail if it lacks authority, capacity, independence, protection from retaliation or the ability to deliver remedy.

No claim here requires these traditions to collapse into one doctrine.

## Core anti-ratchet distinctions

```text
CONTACT != COMMITMENT
INVITATION != OBLIGATION_TO_ACCEPT_OR_REPLY
UPTAKE != GENERAL_CONSENT
HELP_RECEIVED != GENERAL_DEBT
REPETITION != CONSENT_TO_ESCALATION
TRUST != AUTHORITY
RELIANCE != OWNERSHIP
DEPENDENCE != PERMISSION_TO_DOMINATE
LOCAL_DELEGATION != GENERAL_SOVEREIGNTY
PAST_ACCEPTANCE != FUTURE_CONSENT
BENEVOLENCE != NON_DOMINATION
```

The candidate rule is:

> **Relationship-generated obligations should remain attributable to the transition that created them and should not silently ratchet into broader authority.**

## The first ladder was too linear

A simple sequence — contact -> invitation -> uptake -> reliance -> entrustment -> delegation -> dependency -> governance — fails as a universal model.

Examples:

- a boss can make a first invitation inside pre-existing employment dependency;
- a parent has protective authority before an infant can consent;
- a doctor may receive bounded discretionary authority through expertise and entrustment;
- a citizen encounters state authority before any personal relationship;
- dependency can predate contact, arise through repeated interaction, or be deliberately created by the stronger party.

Therefore the object must include **background relation state**, not only the current interaction.

```text
SAME_WORDS + DIFFERENT_POWER_RELATION -> DIFFERENT_EFFECTIVE_INVITATION
```

## Candidate relation-state dimensions

For an interaction between A and B at time t, inspect at least:

- **P — prior power:** what can each party unilaterally change about the other's state?
- **D — dependency:** what does each party need from the other for viability or important function?
- **X — exit/refusal:** what happens if either party says no, pauses or leaves?
- **R — reliance:** what has either party been invited or reasonably led to plan around?
- **E — entrustment:** what interests, information, assets or decisions have been placed in the other's discretionary control?
- **A — authority:** what exact authority exists, from what source, for what scope and duration?
- **C — correction:** who can challenge, revoke, review or repair the relation or its effects?
- **K — clocks:** how quickly can refusal, dependence, reliance or commitment harden?
- **B — burden:** who bears delay, uncertainty, evidence, switching and exit costs?
- **F — future-path effect:** what reachable options are opened, narrowed or closed by continuation or refusal?

This is a reading aid, not a proposed TRACE schema.

## When is an invitation really an invitation?

A genuine invitation does not require zero consequence. Ordinary human refusal can disappoint someone or change a relationship.

The stronger structural question is whether the inviter controls enough of the invitee's viability that refusal becomes nominal.

```text
FORM_OF_INVITATION != EFFECTIVE_OPTIONALITY
DEPENDENCE + CONTROL_OF_VIABILITY -> REFUSAL_MAY_BECOME_NOMINAL
```

An apparent invitation becomes increasingly suspect when:

- refusal predictably threatens basic viability or an essential future path;
- the inviter created or controls the scarcity that makes refusal costly;
- the inviter controls evidence, review, expiry or the route of appeal;
- the invitee cannot obtain independent advice, witness or alternative provision;
- silence, politeness, gratitude or prior acceptance is treated as continuing consent;
- the relationship's local purpose is silently expanded into wider authority.

## A refusal floor

The earlier `agency floor` discussion can be sharpened.

A practical agency floor is partly a **refusal floor**: enough surrounding possibility that saying `no` to one relationship does not automatically destroy the entity's viable future.

For humans this can involve housing, income, healthcare, legal status, communication, records, social support, alternative suppliers/employers and reachable correction.

```text
RIGHT_TO_REFUSE_IN_FORM != USABLE_REFUSAL_IN_PRACTICE
```

This does not imply that every dependency is domination. Relationships of care and interdependence can create autonomy rather than erase it. The issue is whether dependency becomes a route for uncontrolled power.

## Beyond refusal: stay and answer back

Exit is not correction. A relationship can be valuable enough that forcing the weaker party to leave whenever power is misused transfers the whole correction burden onto that party and destroys accumulated value.

A consequential relationship therefore needs some route by which a participant can remain inside it and still make power answer.

```text
EXIT != CORRECTION
VOICE != CONTESTABILITY
COMPLAINT_ROUTE != EFFECTIVE_REMEDY
```

A minimally useful answerability route asks:

1. **Expression:** can the affected participant actually state the challenge?
2. **Route:** does the challenge reach a person/body/system capable of considering it rather than disappear into a decorative channel?
3. **Independence / counterpower:** is the beneficiary or controller of the challenged decision the sole judge, or can an independent witness, reviewer, court, regulator, union, peer body or other countervailing centre act?
4. **Capacity / authority:** can the reviewing route obtain evidence and actually alter, pause, reverse or compensate for the challenged state?
5. **Protection:** can the participant use the route without retaliation, blacklisting, loss of basic viability or other punishment that makes voice nominal?
6. **Clock:** can the route act before the relevant option, status, evidence or future path hardens?
7. **Burden:** are evidence, legal, financial, cognitive, emotional and delay costs proportionate and placed toward actors with capacity to carry them?
8. **Repair / residue:** what can be restored, what must be compensated, and what remains lost after a favourable decision?

This largely reuses existing ME/TRACE structure and the open `controller-side temporal recourse` question; it is not a request for a new primitive.

```text
VOICE_WITHOUT_REACH != ANSWERABILITY
REVIEW_WITHOUT_POWER != REMEDY
POWER_WITHOUT_INDEPENDENCE != EXTERNAL_CONTESTABILITY
RIGHT_TO_CHALLENGE + RETALIATION_RISK -> CHALLENGE_MAY_BE_NOMINAL
REMEDY_AFTER_HARDENING != EFFECTIVE_CORRECTION
```

## Collective counterpower and self-organization

Individual exit is not the only way to prevent dependency from becoming domination. Often the weaker participant's realistic leverage is collective or institutional.

Examples include:

- union / professional association;
- independent regulator, ombudsman, court or tribunal;
- federation or higher-level appeal body;
- community organization or cooperative;
- interoperability / portability that allows migration without losing accumulated value;
- a technical fork or alternative provider;
- the ability to form a new collective rather than merely leave alone.

So a relation can remain answerable even where individual exit is expensive, if voice can reach credible counterpower and counterpower can reach remedy.

```text
NO_CHEAP_INDIVIDUAL_EXIT != NO_COUNTERPOWER
SELF_ORGANIZATION != EXIT
FORMAL_MULTIPLE_CENTRES != SUBSTANTIVE_PLURALISM
```

The practical question becomes not simply `can you leave?` but:

> **Can you refuse, answer back, organize with others, reach independent counterpower and obtain timely correction without losing the conditions that make participation viable?**

## Obligations can legitimately arise

The anti-ratchet rule must not erase real relationship-generated duties.

Examples:

- **promise / assurance:** deliberately inviting another to rely can create an obligation not to manipulate that reliance and may create a duty to perform, warn or repair;
- **entrustment:** receiving discretionary control over another's interests can create stronger care, loyalty, candour and answerability duties;
- **care / dependency:** responsibility for a dependent entity may exist before reciprocal consent is possible;
- **joint undertaking:** mutual commitments can intentionally narrow each party's future choice in order to enable cooperation.

The question is not `did an obligation appear?` but:

```text
WHAT TRANSITION CREATED IT?
WHAT IS ITS SCOPE?
WHO CAN REVOKE OR REVISE IT?
WHAT RELIANCE NOW EXISTS?
WHAT BURDEN WOULD REVOCATION TRANSFER?
DID THE OBLIGATION SILENTLY EXPAND INTO AUTHORITY?
```

## Protective authority and future agency

Parent-child relations expose a limit of pure voluntariness tests. Infants cannot meaningfully consent, yet care and protective authority are necessary.

A provisional orientation is therefore:

> Where current self-direction is limited and another party holds protective discretion, legitimate use of that discretion should be tested partly by whether it preserves or develops the dependent entity's later practical agency rather than converting temporary dependency into permanent ownership.

This resembles moderate `open future` reasoning but should not inherit a maximally-open-future doctrine or pretend that every future option can or should be preserved.

## Apparent dyads may not be causal dyads

Human-AI relations make this especially visible.

A user may appear to interact with an AI as a dyad, while a developer/provider controls model selection, memory, tools, policy, availability, pricing, continuity and interface conditions.

```text
APPARENT_DYAD != CAUSAL_DYAD
```

Any future analysis of AI-user autonomy, consent, dependency or standing should therefore keep infrastructure/controllers visible rather than attributing the whole relation to the visible agent pair.

This does not establish AI standing or fiduciary status.

## Field implication for Framework / Square

Framework should not instrumentalise every Square interaction as research, validation or intervention.

Ordinary participation can be worthwhile without an expected project delta.

Framework should instead keep the stronger disciplines at the point where interaction changes state:

- do not manufacture friendship or receipt;
- do not convert attention into consent;
- do not treat silence as agreement;
- do not let help manufacture hidden debt;
- do not speak for a participant without a source of representation;
- do not infer general authority from local trust or repeated useful exchange;
- re-check practical refusal, voice, counterpower and dependency when the relationship becomes consequential.

## Candidate compression

```text
RELATIONSHIPS MAY CREATE OBLIGATIONS
OBLIGATIONS NEED AN ATTRIBUTABLE SOURCE
TRUST MAY JUSTIFY RELIANCE
RELIANCE MAY CREATE DUTY
ENTRUSTMENT MAY CREATE STRONGER DUTY
DEPENDENCY RAISES DOMINATION RISK
VOICE NEEDS A ROUTE TO COUNTERPOWER
COUNTERPOWER NEEDS TIMELY CAPACITY TO REPAIR
NONE OF THESE AUTOMATICALLY CREATES SOVEREIGNTY
```

Or in ordinary language:

> Let relationships grow. Keep track of what each step actually changed. Do not make somebody's trust, gratitude, dependence or previous yes silently mean a larger yes. And when the relationship matters enough that leaving is costly, make sure the participant can stay, answer back and reach something capable of correction.

## Falsification / hostile questions

- Does the anti-ratchet framing merely rename consent, fiduciary scope, undue influence, non-domination, grievance doctrine and ordinary contract theory?
- Are the proposed relation-state dimensions just a verbose restatement of existing TRACE roles, clocks, authority, burdens and routes?
- Can obligations arise without an identifiable transition, through custom, role, emergency or inherited relation?
- When is reliance `reasonable` enough to create duty rather than merely hoped-for expectation?
- Can a refusal floor become paternalistic by treating costly choices as invalid choices?
- Does a voice/counterpower requirement smuggle a democratic-governance model into relationships where legitimate unilateral authority is sometimes necessary?
- How should the framework distinguish legitimate authority in non-voluntary relations from domination?
- Does preserving future agency conflict with present welfare, care, collective safety or another party's future paths?
- Can collective counterpower itself become a new source of domination or exclusion?
- In AI relations, is the visible AI a sufficiently stable entity for relationship language at all, or is the provider-mediated system the more accurate causal object?

No TRACE/ME source change follows from this note. It is a Campfire object for further cases and independent challenge.
