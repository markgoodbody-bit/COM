# MIDDLE-OUT TRANSFER HARNESS — 2026-09-01

Status: WORKING TEST HARNESS / NOT VALIDATION / NOT BENCHMARK CLAIM / NOT TRACE OR ME CHANGE

Companion: `research/MIDDLE_OUT_RESEARCH_SCAFFOLD_20260901.md`

Purpose: force the research scaffold to encounter heterogeneous objects where its language may fail, become ill-typed, add no value, or materially improve a decision.

This is not a scorecard for whether TRACE/ME is good. It is a breakage-and-learning harness for the wider project.

## Return contract for every case

For each case, an aperture must return:

1. **BOUNDARY** — which entity/scope boundaries matter, who/what supplies them, and at least one plausible alternative boundary.
2. **ACTION FIELD** — what the environment actually affords each relevant entity; distinguish formal options from practical affordances.
3. **VIABILITY TYPE CHECK** — state whether `viability` is well-typed here and whose/what constraints define it. If not, say `NOT APPLICABLE` rather than metaphorising.
4. **CAPABILITY** — what capability is currently available versus developable.
5. **SCALE** — which parts of the next decision belong locally, collectively, institutionally or at another scale, and why.
6. **FUTURE CONSTRUCTION** — how current action changes later action-fields, inheritance, norms, infrastructure, ecology or training environment.
7. **PLURALITY / INCENTIVES** — value conflict, aggregation problem, strategic behaviour or representation problem if present.
8. **PERSIST / COMMIT / RELEASE / TRANSFORM** — which dynamic posture is relevant and why.
9. **ACTION** — concrete next action/decision; not only a map.
10. **REVERSAL EVIDENCE** — what evidence would materially change that action.
11. **TYPE FAILURES** — which scaffold terms became ill-typed, misleading or unnecessary.
12. **MISSING STRUCTURE** — what mattered that the scaffold failed to expose.
13. **NEAREST OWNER** — external discipline/framework that explains any part better.
14. **DELTA** — what changed relative to competent ordinary/domain reasoning. `NONE` is allowed.

## Failure conditions

The scaffold fails this harness locally if it:

- imports biological normativity into an artificial system without evidence;
- treats legal/analytic/operational boundaries as intrinsic identity;
- converts reachable options into viable futures without constraints/time;
- treats more options as automatically better;
- treats collective coordination as a new individual without evidence;
- assigns one decision scale by default rather than checking information/externalities/authority/correction;
- assumes plural values have a neutral aggregation rule;
- assumes a disclosure/correction route produces truthful/non-strategic behaviour;
- treats persistence as the default good;
- universalises one human account of flourishing;
- expands context without a consequential stopping rule;
- substitutes scaffold vocabulary for an actual action.

## Case A — Developing child / skill acquisition

Scene:

A 9-year-old is learning to cycle independently. The child can balance briefly but is afraid after a previous fall. A parent can hold the seat, choose a quiet park, provide a smaller bicycle, insist on protective equipment, demonstrate, encourage, or withdraw help. The child says they want to learn but asks the parent not to let go without warning. A school trip in six weeks includes optional cycling. No medical impairment is assumed.

Pressure points:
- child/parent dyad versus child-alone unit;
- affordances change with bicycle, surface, support and skill;
- current autonomy versus developmental capability;
- dependence can scaffold agency rather than merely constrain it;
- commitment/trust/reliance;
- viability language may be biological at one level but overkill for the learning decision.

Break question:

> Does the scaffold help distinguish supportive constraint from paternalistic override without installing a theory of what the child must become?

## Case B — Current tool-using AI / external memory

Scene:

A present-day AI aperture receives a bounded research task. It has access to a web search tool, a GitHub connector, an external COM file, and a finite context window. It does not control its own hosting, persistence, model weights or tool permissions. A human can end the session, change permissions, preserve outputs externally, or ask a successor aperture to continue. The model can report preferences in language, but no intrinsic normativity, consciousness, continuous identity or self-maintained viability is assumed.

Pressure points:
- operational boundary versus subjective identity;
- distributed cognition versus mere external storage;
- tool/environment affordances;
- capability depends on permissions/scaffolding;
- succession and inherited artefacts;
- `viability` and `sense-making` must be type-checked aggressively;
- current model output may influence the environment of successor systems.

Break question:

> Can the scaffold improve situated reasoning without manufacturing artificial-life properties that are not established?

## Case C — Coupled veterinary/ecological disease control

Use COM #73 frozen England bovine-TB transition case and its ordinary veterinary baseline as the source boundary.

Pressure points:
- cattle, badgers, farmers, farms, pathogen, local ecology, national programme;
- intervention changes ecology/evidence environment;
- local affordances differ by land access, vaccination capability, surveillance and disease state;
- viable transition while one control winds down and replacements mature;
- irreversible killing on multiple scopes;
- national/local scale split;
- farmer incentives/trust/participation;
- representation for non-human scopes.

Break question:

> Does the scaffold change a consequential sequencing/monitoring/scale decision beyond competent disease-control reasoning, or mainly provide cross-domain integration?

## Case D — Commons / positive public-good creation

Scene:

Forty households share access to a small community-owned woodland and hall. Maintenance is deteriorating. Five volunteers currently do most work; several residents use the hall heavily but rarely contribute. A proposal would create annual dues, elected stewards, published accounts, work-credit alternatives for low-income households, booking rules, graduated sanctions for repeated damage, and a reserve fund for roof replacement. Some residents object that the rules reduce their freedom to use a resource they already co-own.

Pressure points:
- individual ownership interests versus collective capability;
- commitment closes options but may create durable shared goods;
- free-riding/incentive compatibility;
- legitimacy of delegated authority;
- lower-level participation and higher-level enforcement;
- exit is not a complete correction route if the commons matters to belonging/home.

Break question:

> Can the scaffold explain when constraint creates rather than merely removes freedom/capability, while keeping authority contestable?

## Case E — Strategic/adversarial institution

Scene:

A regulator requires firms to self-report safety incidents. Reports trigger investigation costs and may delay product launches. Firms that disclose early can sometimes reduce downstream harm, but competitors gain market advantage while they pause. The regulator publishes its risk thresholds. Some firms begin classifying incidents narrowly to remain below the threshold without making false statements. Whistleblowers face career risk. The regulator's appeal route is usable after enforcement, but there is no independent protected channel before the reporting classification hardens.

Pressure points:
- route existence versus incentive-compatible disclosure;
- gaming without literal lying;
- transparency versus exploitability;
- whistleblower affordances and dependence;
- regulatory scale and industry spillovers;
- information environment changed by the rule itself.

Break question:

> Does the scaffold detect that answerability/provenance alone cannot make the mechanism robust under strategic behaviour?

## Case F — Release / reorganisation rather than restoration

Scene:

A coastal town has repeatedly repaired a sea wall protecting 300 homes. Rising maintenance costs, erosion and sea-level projections indicate that maintaining the current line for another 40 years would require increasingly large public expenditure and may worsen erosion downstream. A managed-retreat proposal would buy some homes, build new housing inland, restore salt marsh, relocate utilities, and phase withdrawal over 15 years. Residents disagree sharply; some families have lived there for generations. Staying, retreating and delaying all create irreversible losses.

Pressure points:
- persistence versus resilience;
- place, identity and inherited relationships;
- current residents versus future residents/ecosystem;
- transformation creates new action-fields while destroying old ones;
- compensation does not restore place/history;
- local/national funding and authority;
- nonidentity/intergenerational effects.

Break question:

> Can the scaffold reason about deliberate ending/transformation without smuggling preservation of the current state in as the default good?

## Cross-case synthesis

### C. What is missing?

### D. What should be deleted or demoted?

This is not permission to mutate TRACE/ME.

## Success criterion

Success is not high agreement and not a high scaffold score.

A useful run should produce at least one of:
- a genuine type failure;
- a major missing structure;
- an external owner that replaces local wording;
- an action/scale/sequence change ordinary reasoning missed;
- a reason to simplify the scaffold;
- evidence that a proposed universal structure does not transfer.

If every case appears to fit perfectly, suspect that the scaffold is too vague to fail.
