Case: a regulator requires firms to self-report safety incidents. Reporting can trigger investigation cost and launch delay. Firms that disclose early may reduce harm but lose competitive advantage while paused. The regulator publishes risk thresholds. Some firms classify incidents narrowly so they remain below the threshold without making literally false statements. Whistleblowers face career risk. A post-enforcement appeal exists, but there is no independent protected route before the reporting classification hardens.

Guiding question:

> What happens when the correction route itself sits inside a strategic mechanism?

## 1. Nearest external owners

This case is strongly externally owned.

### Responsive regulation / regulatory discretion

Responsive-regulation traditions reject one fixed enforcement response. Enforcement may escalate from persuasion/education through stronger sanctions according to conduct and context. But later work also identifies failure modes: regulators can become trapped in soft enforcement, fail to escalate, or lack the capacity/resources needed for repeated follow-up.

Sources:
- Ian Ayres & John Braithwaite, `Responsive Regulation` (1992)
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/law-and-society-review/article/abs/compliance-trap-the-moral-message-in-responsive-regulatory-enforcement/1F7272C48DA749F5DCFA34B4870D86BC
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/legal-studies/article/regulatory-discretion-structuring-power-in-the-era-of-regulatory-capitalism/CD31D43F65C3AB67A58ED60696CE07C5
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/data-and-policy/article/agentbased-modeling-for-datadriven-enforcement-combining-empirical-data-with-behavioral-theory-for-scenariobased-analysis-of-inspections/A420DDB88A8FE3BE5EF9C88B9B625EC2

### Differential capacity to comply

Regulatory-compliance work emphasizes that different organizations have different receptive/implementation capacities. The same regulatory instrument does not produce the same response in every organization.

Source:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/regulation-and-criminal-justice/regulatory-compliance-organizational-capacities-and-regulatory-strategies-for-environmental-protection/06F477C522BE5CF0763CAD7E60B38543

### Strategic threshold response

Recent empirical work on cryptocurrency transaction screening finds significant bunching immediately below regulatory screening thresholds, consistent with strategic adjustment to avoid compliance requirements. The general lesson is not about crypto specifically: a published threshold can become an optimization target.

Source:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/political-science-research-and-methods/article/how-strong-are-international-standards-in-practice-evidence-from-cryptocurrency-transactions/451D0F9F954B11D94B1B82ED826C2DFE

### Voice, silence and reporting

Employee voice/silence research treats speaking up as behavior shaped by perceived consequences and organizational conditions, not as an automatic result of a formal channel. A 2024 Annual Review of Sociology synthesis similarly finds reporting decisions are shaped by social pressures across individual, group and societal levels.

Sources:
- https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-orgpsych-120920-054654
- https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-soc-030222-013736

### Whistleblower channel failure / retaliation

Current whistleblowing scholarship emphasizes that formal protections and internal channels can fail, and that whistleblowers may face retaliation, public naming, blacklisting or other serious consequences. Official channels can in some cases become part of the retaliation mechanism.

Sources:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/perspectives-on-whistleblowing/whistleblowing-research-recent-developments/DDA5A13C9E8C96A1400B9CE10A10710F
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/abs/regulators-of-last-resort/when-official-channels-fail-whistleblowers/39FA9516A2AB7C0B533C12589CACD67D

## 2. Boundary

Material scopes/actors:
- regulated firm;
- employees/safety staff with incident knowledge;
- executives/classifiers;
- regulator;
- product/service users exposed to safety risk;
- competitors affected by disclosure/launch delay asymmetry;
- whistleblowers and their households/careers;
- downstream investigators/enforcement bodies.

Boundary caution:
- the firm is not one epistemic actor;
- internal classifiers and employees may hold conflicting incentives/information;
- regulator and reporting channel are not equivalent to the people who operate them;
- affected public users may have no direct access to the internal evidence.

`ORGANIZATION != SINGLE APERTURE`

## 3. Action field

Nominal firm options:
- report;
- do not report;
- classify narrowly below threshold;
- seek clarification;
- investigate internally;
- pause launch voluntarily.

Actual affordances are incentive-shaped.

Early reporting can impose:
- investigation cost;
- delay;
- competitor advantage;
- internal blame/status consequences.

Employee reporting can impose:
- career risk;
- retaliation;
- social exclusion;
- legal/financial burden.

Therefore:

`REPORTING CHANNEL EXISTS != SAFE REPORTING AFFORDANCE`
`NO LITERAL FALSE STATEMENT != NON-GAMED CLASSIFICATION`

## 4. Viability type check

Use `viability` only for declared system/safety/governance objectives, not as intrinsic regulator or corporate normativity.

Relevant declared constraints might include:
- serious safety signals reach an independent enough decision point before exposure hardens;
- firms retain practical capacity to comply;
- reporting burden does not systematically suppress useful information;
- regulator retains capacity to audit/reclassify/escalate;
- anti-gaming adaptation occurs before thresholds become ritualized loopholes.

## 5. Capability / correction capacity

A formal duty to report is not enough. The regime needs capability to make reporting consequential.

Required capacities include:
- independent reclassification/audit;
- sampling below thresholds;
- investigation capacity;
- credible escalation/enforcement;
- protected employee reporting;
- retaliation response/support;
- ability to revise thresholds/indicators;
- evidence preservation before internal records or careers are altered.

This directly tests the commons-pass seam:

`COMMITMENT / RESERVE CAN BUILD FUTURE CORRECTION CAPACITY`.

Here the relevant reserves are not only money. They can be:
- inspector capacity;
- protected reporting infrastructure;
- independent technical expertise;
- legal/support capacity for reporters;
- pre-authorized review triggers;
- audit bandwidth.

A regime that has a right to investigate but no capacity to investigate before the clock closes has formal authority without effective correction capability.

## 6. Scale / authority topology

Firm level:
- detect and preserve incident evidence;
- conduct bounded internal assessment;
- make required initial report;
- implement immediate safety containment within its legitimate competence.

Regulator level:
- define/reporting regime;
- independently classify/reclassify where necessary;
- inspect/audit;
- escalate enforcement;
- revise gaming-prone thresholds/rules.

Independent/protected channel:
- receive information that cannot safely traverse management classification;
- preserve reporter identity/protection where applicable;
- route evidence to an actor able to act before retaliation or safety exposure hardens.

External review/legal level:
- challenge regulator/firm misconduct;
- protect procedural/legal rights;
- adjudicate disputes the regulator cannot legitimately decide for itself.

Important authority correction:

A firm classifier can possess **de facto epistemic gate power** without holding legitimate authority to determine the regulator's final scope.

This fits the existing authority-function decomposition:

`SENSE / CLASSIFY / ROUTE` can control whether `AUTHORIZE / INVESTIGATE / ENFORCE` ever activates.

No new authority primitive is needed, but upstream information functions must not be treated as politically/ethically neutral plumbing.

## 7. Strategic participants / mechanism robustness

This case validates the scaffold's existing warning:

`DISCLOSURE ROUTE != TRUTHFUL / COMPLETE DISCLOSURE`
`CHECKABLE != NON-GAMEABLE`

The main failure is mechanism design, not absence of a form.

A published threshold changes the environment. Firms may optimize classifications/transactions around it. The regulator must therefore watch not only reported incidents but the distribution around thresholds and other evidence of strategic bunching/avoidance.

`INTERVENTION CHANGES OBSERVATION` applies strongly here.

The threshold is simultaneously:
- a decision rule;
- an incentive;
- a public signal about regulator attention;
- a target for strategic adaptation.

## 8. Participation burden / whistleblower standing

The commons-pass distinction transfers cleanly:

`ACCESS TO PARTICIPATE != PRACTICAL CAPACITY TO PARTICIPATE`.

Here, formal employee voice is not enough if using it predictably threatens livelihood/status.

A post-enforcement appeal is too late for some harms:
- retaliation may already have happened;
- evidence may already be reframed/lost;
- unsafe exposure may have continued;
- classification may have hardened into organizational narrative.

Therefore the correction architecture needs a pre-hardening route, not only appeal after enforcement.

## 9. Persist / commit / release / transform

Do not respond to gaming by simply hiding every rule or replacing judgement with ever-more granular thresholds.

The regime should retain stable commitments:
- duty to report defined safety classes;
- evidence-preservation duties;
- anti-retaliation/protected-route architecture;
- regulator authority to reclassify/audit;
- due process/appeal.

But it should adapt:
- thresholds;
- audit strategy;
- sampling;
- enforcement posture;
- indicators of gaming;
- support/protection mechanisms.

`STABLE PURPOSE / RIGHTS != FROZEN DETECTION MECHANISM`.

## 10. Action

Retain self-reporting, but redesign it so the firm is not the sole effective judge of whether its own incident enters the regulatory system.

Minimum posture:

1. preserve a clear initial firm reporting duty and evidence-preservation requirement;
2. separate firm self-classification from final regulatory classification/reclassification authority;
3. sample/audit some below-threshold events and monitor distributional bunching around published thresholds;
4. treat persistent near-threshold clustering as a trigger for review, not as proof of wrongdoing by itself;
5. preserve a protected route outside the immediate management chain before enforcement/classification hardens;
6. make anti-retaliation response and reporter support part of correction capacity rather than decorative policy;
7. maintain credible escalation capacity for strategic/non-cooperative firms while allowing proportionate lower-reach responses where appropriate;
8. account for firm capacity to comply without converting incapacity into blanket permission to externalize safety risk;
9. periodically re-evaluate threshold/rule design because the intervention changes behavior and evidence;
10. keep post-enforcement appeal, but do not mistake it for an adequate pre-harm correction route;
11. preserve external/legal oversight of regulator discretion and whistleblower rights.

Do not infer a universal safe-harbor, penalty schedule, confidentiality rule or threshold formula from this research scaffold. Those belong to the actual regulatory/domain/legal owner.

## 11. Reversal / modification evidence

Materially change the recommendation if evidence shows:
- below-threshold sampling adds large burden without detecting gaming/omissions;
- an independent route creates serious false-reporting/abuse without manageable screening;
- confidential/preliminary reporting systematically prevents affected users from receiving necessary warnings;
- the regulator lacks capacity to act on additional signals, making added channels performative rather than corrective;
- strategic bunching is explained by benign operational distributions rather than avoidance;
- another domain-owner mechanism achieves the same information quality and reporter protection at lower burden.

## 12. Type failures / demotions

- `ANSWERABILITY` alone is insufficient: a channel can be answerable after the fact while strategically starved of evidence before the decision.
- `PROVENANCE` alone is insufficient: perfectly attributed reports can still be selectively classified or never produced.
- `CONTESTABILITY` after enforcement is insufficient when evidence/retaliation/safety clocks close earlier.
- `TRANSPARENCY` is not monotonic good: transparent thresholds can improve predictability and simultaneously expose an optimization target.

No existing scaffold term becomes wholly ill-typed; the main correction is to keep incentive/mechanism analysis active.

## 13. Missing structure exposed

Again, no new universal primitive is earned.

Three stronger formulations survive the second-domain pressure:

### A. Authority topology must include upstream epistemic gates

The entity that senses/classifies/routes evidence can exercise consequential de facto power even without final decision authority.

`FINAL AUTHORITY != ONLY CONSEQUENTIAL POWER`

This is already representable by the authority-function decomposition and should remain research-level until further transfer.

### B. Participation burden generalizes to disclosure burden

`FORMAL VOICE != SAFE / USABLE VOICE`

This travels from commons meetings to employee/whistleblower reporting and likely further.

### C. Correction capacity requires pre-hardening actuation capacity

A right/channel without investigators, protection, audit bandwidth, expertise or time is not enough.

`ROUTE + NO CAPACITY BEFORE CLOCK CLOSES = FORMAL CORRECTION WITHOUT EFFECTIVE CORRECTION`

This strengthens the existing correction-capacity work rather than creating a new theory layer.
