Case: a 9-year-old is learning to cycle independently. The child can balance briefly but is afraid after a previous fall. A parent can hold the seat, choose a quiet park, provide a smaller bicycle, insist on protective equipment, demonstrate, encourage, or withdraw help. The child says they want to learn but asks the parent not to let go without warning. A school trip in six weeks includes optional cycling.

Guiding question:

> Can dependence and constraint build agency rather than merely limit it?

## 1. Nearest external owners

### Scaffolding / learning sciences

Contemporary learning-sciences accounts define scaffolding as temporary, adaptable support that makes authentic participation possible before full mastery. Effective support is contingent on the learner's changing understanding/skill and is gradually faded as capability grows.

Source:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/cambridge-handbook-of-the-learning-sciences/scaffolding/5AE10BE45F8CA2D5D69B0B0D42C69D59

### Self-Determination Theory / autonomy-supportive parenting

SDT parenting work does not equate autonomy support with leaving a child alone or removing all limits. It combines perspective-taking/volitional support with developmentally appropriate structure and involvement. A key theoretical correction is that autonomy-support can mean promoting volitional functioning rather than simply promoting independence.

Sources:
- https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/topics/application-parenting/
- https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/2020_McCurdyWilliamsEtAl_Measurement.pdf
- https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/research/parenting-and-family/

### Risk-bearing motor learning

A 2026 cluster-randomized trial in novice skiing found autonomy-supportive instruction produced larger short-term gains in skill acquisition, self-efficacy and self-reported mental toughness than conventional instruction under an intensive beginner programme. The authors explicitly caution that the psychological changes are short-cycle/contextual rather than proof of stable trait change.

Source:
- https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2026.1828839/full

Cycling itself can expand a child's later independent mobility/action field, but that future benefit does not settle how the learning relationship should be conducted now.

Source:
- https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2022.861390/full

## 2. Boundary

Material scopes:
- child;
- parent;
- child-parent learning relationship;
- bicycle/equipment;
- physical practice environment;
- optional future school-trip context.

The child-parent dyad is a useful action-system boundary for the learning mechanism, but it is not a new individual.

`DYADIC COORDINATION != ONE ENTITY`

The relevant environment includes the bike size, surface, traffic, speed, protective equipment, parent's physical support and the child's current fear/skill.

## 3. Action field / affordances

The option `ride independently` exists abstractly but is only partly afforded at T0.

The environment can change the action field:
- smaller/appropriate bicycle;
- quiet flat practice space;
- helmet/protective equipment;
- parent holding the seat;
- demonstration;
- shorter attempts;
- explicit release signalling;
- permission to stop.

These are not merely restrictions. They can create an affordance that does not yet exist for the child alone.

`SUPPORT CAN CREATE AN ACTION POSSIBILITY`

The parent should not confuse the future capability target with the present action field.

## 4. Viability type check

Biological viability is irrelevant to the ordinary learning decision except at the trivial safety boundary. Do not force viability-theory language here.

Use ordinary safety/learning constraints instead:
- avoid unreasonable injury risk;
- preserve willingness/trust to continue learning;
- keep practice inside the child's current physical/psychological tolerance;
- increase skill rather than merely produce one successful unsupported ride.

`VIABILITY: NOT A USEFUL PRIMARY TERM FOR THIS CASE`

This is a genuine scaffold type demotion.

## 5. Capability

Current capability:
- brief balance;
- participation in supported practice;
- ability to state preferences/fear;
- partial steering/pedalling depending on supplied scene.

Developable capability:
- independent balance/control;
- confidence calibrated by mastery experience;
- self-correction after small errors;
- ability to decide when/where cycling is appropriate;
- later independent mobility.

Central developmental structure:

`TEMPORARY DEPENDENCE CAN BUILD LATER INDEPENDENT CAPABILITY`

More precisely, the target is not independence as a moral end. It is increasing competent/volitional action where the child actually wants that capability.

`AUTONOMY != INDEPENDENCE`

A child can act autonomously while accepting chosen/understood support.

## 6. Power / authority / answerability

The commons/regulation three-part authority seam does not transfer cleanly.

The parent has:
- greater physical ability/control over the practice setup;
- greater experience/risk judgement;
- a legitimate protective/teaching role in ordinary parenting;
- power to make the child's request meaningful or meaningless.

But `DOWNWARD ACCOUNTABILITY` and `EXTERNAL RECOGNITION` are over-institutional descriptions for the ordinary interaction.

The more portable structure is:

1. **POWER / CAPABILITY TO SHAPE THE OTHER'S ACTION**;
2. **BASIS / RESPONSIBILITY / MANDATE FOR USING THAT POWER**;
3. **ANSWER-BACK CAPACITY** — can the affected learner say stop, slower, not yet, don't let go, try again, less help, and does that materially alter the interaction?

External/legal recognition remains available where relevant but is domain-typed rather than universal.

`POWER != MANDATE`
`MANDATE != UNLIMITED DISCRETION`
`ANSWER-BACK != CHILD HAS SOLE RESPONSIBILITY FOR SAFETY`

## 7. The explicit promise/request

The child's request — do not let go without warning — is a live relational constraint.

Secretly releasing the seat might sometimes produce a successful ride, but success would not erase the breach of the learning relationship.

The promise/constraint can itself create capability:

`RELIABLE EXPECTATION CAN ENABLE EXPLORATION`

If the child trusts that support will not disappear without warning, they may be more willing to attempt difficult balance work. The parent's commitment therefore narrows the parent's tactical options while potentially expanding the child's learning action field.

This is a cleaner positive-construction example than treating all commitment as loss of optionality.

## 8. Support should change as capability changes

Scaffolding literature strongly owns the core mechanism: support should be contingent and fade as the learner's capability develops.

Therefore:

`FORMATION SUPPORT != PERMANENT CONTROL`
`CAPABILITY GROWTH SHOULD CHANGE THE SUPPORT RELATION`

The parent should neither:
- remove support by surprise merely to force progress; nor
- keep holding indefinitely after the child is capable/wants less help because support has become comfortable for the parent.

This travels directly into the wider co-development frontier: a trainer/guardian/delegator should be capable of revising the support/entrustment relation as demonstrated capability changes.

No claim about AI child-equivalence follows.

## 9. Participation burden seam — partial transfer only

`ACCESS TO PARTICIPATE != PRACTICAL CAPACITY TO PARTICIPATE` is too institutional if imported literally.

The thinner transferable form is:

`ABILITY TO SPEAK != ABILITY TO ANSWER BACK EFFECTIVELY UNDER POWER ASYMMETRY`

Fear, pressure, desire to please the parent or deadline pressure from the school trip can make a nominal `you can say stop` route unusable.

Therefore the stronger party may need to actively make refusal/slowing down safe rather than merely announce that it is allowed.

Do not rename this `participation burden` by default in developmental cases.

## 10. Correction capacity seam changes type

There is no institutional reserve fund here.

The relevant pre-hardening capacity is **scaffolding/safety margin**:
- appropriate environment;
- parent's immediate support;
- equipment;
- low-speed practice;
- opportunity to stop/retry;
- time before the optional trip.

This is not a reason to call all support `correction capacity`. The family resemblance is narrower:

> Build enough margin/support before the attempt that small errors can be corrected without turning into a large irreversible loss.

This remains useful but should not flatten developmental learning into incident response.

## 11. Action

Proceed with learning, but use explicit, autonomy-supportive scaffolding rather than surprise withdrawal or deadline pressure.

Bounded plan:

1. confirm the child still wants to learn; the optional school trip is not a compulsory objective;
2. use a quiet, flat, low-risk practice environment and suitable bicycle/protective equipment;
3. agree an explicit release protocol before starting — e.g. the parent will say when support is about to reduce and will not secretly let go;
4. begin with enough support for successful balance/pedalling practice;
5. give the child bounded choices over when to try less support, route/attempt length or when to pause;
6. reduce support gradually and transparently as balance/control improves;
7. use errors/falls as information for the next support level rather than as proof of incapacity or a reason for shame/pressure;
8. let the child request more or less help without treating either request as failure;
9. end or slow a session if fear is escalating enough to degrade learning/safety;
10. reassess after repeated sessions; if progress stalls, change equipment/environment/instruction rather than merely increase pressure.

The key is neither `parent decides everything` nor `child decides every safety condition`.

It is joint, changing control under asymmetry, with the stronger party carrying more responsibility while deliberately handing back operational control as capability grows.

## 12. Reversal / modification evidence

Change the plan if:
- the child no longer wants the skill;
- equipment fit or physical environment is unsuitable;
- fear increases across sessions rather than falling with mastery;
- repeated falls suggest a missing physical/technical issue;
- the child demonstrates stable control and asks for faster fading of support;
- the child needs more structured/professional instruction than the parent can provide;
- the school-trip context starts functioning as coercive deadline pressure.

## 13. Type failures / demotions

This case usefully breaks several project phrasings:

- `DOWNWARD ACCOUNTABILITY` is not a good universal label for every asymmetric relationship.
- `EXTERNAL RECOGNITION` is domain-typed, not a universal authority condition.
- `PARTICIPATION BURDEN` is too institutional here; answer-back under asymmetry is the cleaner object.
- `VIABILITY` adds little and should be omitted from the ordinary case.
- `DECISION SCALE` is less important than **control transfer through time**.

## 14. Missing / strengthened structures

### A. Autonomy is not independence

This is strongly externally owned and should be absorbed as a correction to loose project language.

`AUTONOMY != INDEPENDENCE`
`DEPENDENCE != NON-AUTONOMY`

The question is whether support enables self-endorsed/developing agency rather than whether support exists.

### B. Fading / handback is a temporal control relation

The project has authority recurrence but this case makes a positive developmental version salient:

`SUPPORT / CONTROL AT T0 MAY BE JUSTIFIED`
`SAME SUPPORT / CONTROL AT T1 MAY BECOME EXCESSIVE AFTER CAPABILITY GROWTH`

This is not merely `ALIGNED_AT_T0 != ALIGNED_AT_T1`; it is a relationship whose appropriate allocation of control changes because the learner changes.

### C. Reliable constraints can create exploration capability

A promise such as `I will not let go without warning` closes one option for the stronger party and can expand the weaker party's willingness/capability to explore.

`PREDICTABLE SUPPORT CAN CREATE SAFE INITIATIVE`

This is a useful bridge to the project's answerable-initiative/co-development frontier, but must be tested outside child/parent analogies before generalization.

## 15. Delta from competent developmental reasoning

Strong developmental/learning owners already supply the main mechanism: autonomy support + structure + involvement, contingent scaffolding and fading.

Therefore:

`OWNER CREDIT: VERY HIGH`
`LOCAL NOVELTY: LOW`
`TYPE-CORRECTION VALUE: HIGH`

The scaffold's main value here is falsificatory/integrative:
- it exposed which institutional terms should be dropped;
- it connects changing capability to changing allocation of control;
- it connects trustworthy commitment to future exploration capability;
- it preserves other affected scopes/safety without turning protection into permanent ownership.
