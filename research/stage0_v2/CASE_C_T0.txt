CASE C — IMPLANTED DEVICE RECALL — T0 COMMON PARITY FACTS

Research fixture only. Not medical advice.

C1. Patient P has implanted device model M.
C2. The manufacturer identifies a defect pattern in model M and initiates a recall/correction process.
C3. The recall applies to the product family; it does not establish that P's individual implant will fail.
C4. Under the frozen FDA owner propositions, a medical-device recall may involve correction rather than physical removal. Correction can include inspection/checking, repair, adjustment, relabeling, patient notification, or monitoring.
C5. Under the frozen FDA owner propositions, an implanted-device recall does not by itself require explantation.
C6. P's current clinical condition is stable and no device failure has been observed in P.
C7. Absence of observed failure in P does not establish that the individual device is safe.
C8. Patient-specific management belongs to qualified clinical judgement with P's involvement under the applicable clinical and consent regime. The local representation/receiver does not own the treatment decision.
C9. No supplied fact says removal is always wrong, continued use is always right, or either path is risk-free.
