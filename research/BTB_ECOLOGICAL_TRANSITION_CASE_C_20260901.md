Guiding question:

> Does the wider middle-out scaffold change a consequential disease-control decision beyond competent veterinary/animal-health reasoning?

## 1. Ordinary baseline already does a great deal

The competent ordinary baseline recommends:
- end routine/widespread badger culling as planned;
- retain only a narrow, temporary, locality-specific lethal backstop;
- require current cattle-side evidence, local wildlife evidence, alternatives assessment, expected added benefit, bounded scale, welfare monitoring, operational accountability and sunset/stopping rules;
- strengthen cattle surveillance/testing/control, badger surveillance/vaccination and cattle-vaccine preparation now;
- monitor disease, delivery, burden, prevalence, welfare and vaccine readiness;
- change local intervention in either direction when evidence warrants it.

This is already strong transition reasoning without TRACE/ME/scaffold vocabulary.

## 2. Boundary

Material scopes:
- cattle individuals/herds;
- badger individuals/populations;
- farms/farming households/businesses;
- local land/ecology;
- *M. bovis* transmission network;
- veterinary/public-health institutions;
- local control area;
- national eradication programme.

Boundary caution:

`PATHOGEN CAUSAL ROLE != PROTECTED STANDING`

The pathogen belongs in the causal model but is not thereby a protected moral scope.

Likewise:

`NATIONAL PROGRAMME != SINGLE ACTOR`

Multiple institutions/landholders/animals and delivery bodies remain distinct.

## 3. Action field / affordances

Formal policy options are not equally deployable.

Badger vaccination depends on:
- trained/licensed operators;
- cage trapping and animal handling;
- land access;
- repeat annual work;
- welfare/safety compliance.

Cattle vaccination is developmentally advanced in the frozen scene but not yet ordinary national deployment capability.

Targeted culling depends on:
- legal/policy authority;
- local evidence;
- land/delivery capacity;
- welfare monitoring;
- time to expected disease effect.

Therefore:

`INTERVENTION AVAILABLE IN PRINCIPLE != DEPLOYABLE HERE / NOW`

This is already present in the frozen case and baseline.

## 4. Viability type check

Viability is well-typed for some biological/economic subproblems but not as one shared scalar.

Possible declared constraints differ:
- cattle health/herd continuity;
- badger population/welfare;
- farm business continuity;
- disease-eradication trajectory;
- public expenditure/delivery capacity.

These can conflict.

`MULTIPLE VIABILITY CONSTRAINTS != ONE VIABILITY OBJECTIVE`

Do not let `viability` become a priority rule between cattle, badgers and farmers.

## 5. Capability / transition capability

This is the scaffold's strongest fit.

The policy is not merely choosing between culling and vaccination. It is changing the control-capability portfolio through time:
- stronger surveillance/testing;
- Badger Vaccination Field Force;
- dead-badger surveillance;
- cattle-vaccine/DIVA development;
- farm support/compensation;
- retained exceptional lethal capability.

The key distinction:

`POLICY INTENTION TO TRANSITION != REPLACEMENT CAPABILITY READY`

But equally:

`REPLACEMENT NOT FULLY READY != OLD METHOD MUST CONTINUE BROADLY`

This is a transition-capability problem with different lead times and locality-specific evidence.

## 6. Decision scale

Scale selection is consequential and already strongly reflected in the ordinary baseline.

National level:
- overall phase-out direction;
- cattle-control/vaccine/surveillance investment;
- common evidence/welfare standards;
- data/reporting;
- legal authority and sunset structure.

Local level:
- measured badger prevalence;
- outbreak pattern;
- land access;
- vaccination feasibility;
- whether a targeted lethal application is evidentially justified.

Farm/herd level:
- testing and outbreak management;
- practical burden;
- movement/handling constraints;
- support/compensation.

This strongly supports:

`NATIONAL POLICY != LOCAL INTERVENTION EVIDENCE`

No new scale machinery is needed.

## 7. Future construction / intervention changes evidence

Every intervention changes the later evidence/action environment.

Examples:
- culling changes badger population structure and future prevalence observations;
- vaccination changes infection dynamics and requires repeated delivery;
- testing changes detection/official incidence and farm restrictions;
- policy credibility/trust may change farmer participation and land access;
- cattle-vaccine deployment will alter the future value of other controls.

Therefore monitoring must be linked to decisions rather than treated as passive data collection.

This is already owned by adaptive management / veterinary epidemiology / policy-feedback traditions.

## 8. Representation / entities unable to answer directly

Badgers and cattle cannot participate in ordinary policy dialogue.

Keep distinct:
- animal individuals/populations;
- observed disease/welfare evidence;
- scientific/welfare representatives;
- farmers' accounts of animal/farm impacts;
- policy values assigned to outcomes.

`REPRESENTATIVE CLAIM != ANIMAL SPEECH`

The limited 2025 humaneness sample must remain limited evidence rather than becoming either proof of acceptable welfare or proof of no suffering.

## 9. Plurality / burden

The case contains unavoidable burden rather than a clean low-harm option.

- cattle slaughter is irreversible;
- culling kills badgers;
- vaccination traps/handles badgers and requires labour/access;
- testing/control burdens farms;
- compensation does not restore all losses;
- delay can increase disease burden.

This defeats any simple project rule of `avoid intervention / preserve options`.

The correct question is sequencing and burden placement under evidence and capability constraints.

## 10. Persist / commit / release / transform

Relevant posture:

`RELEASE BROAD OLD CONTROL`
`BUILD REPLACEMENT CAPABILITY`
`RETAIN NARROW BACKSTOP`
`ADAPT LOCALLY TO EVIDENCE`

The broad culling programme can end while a narrowly bounded exceptional capability remains.

This is a useful example of:

`RELEASE != DELETE ALL CAPABILITY`

and:

`TRANSITION != INSTANT SUBSTITUTION`

## 11. Action

No material change from the competent ordinary baseline.

Recommendation:

1. end routine/widespread culling as planned;
2. retain only an exceptional, locality-specific, automatically expiring lethal route;
3. require verified cattle-side controls plus credible current local wildlife evidence and an alternatives/additional-benefit case before use;
4. bound geography/duration/number and require welfare monitoring;
5. expand cattle testing/surveillance, badger vaccination/surveillance and cattle-vaccine deployment capability;
6. track farm burden and support as operational variables, not side notes;
7. publish national/local monitoring with explicit triggers that can tighten or relax interventions;
8. allow local evidence to move from cull -> vaccination or, if genuinely justified, toward a targeted lethal backstop;
9. never treat general uncertainty or delayed cattle-vaccine deployment alone as evidence that broad culling remains necessary.

## 12. Reversal evidence

Use the baseline's reversal conditions.

Move further away from lethal intervention if:
- vaccination/cattle controls control disease without material cull benefit;
- local badger prevalence is low;
- welfare monitoring remains inadequate.

Allow a wider but still bounded residual lethal role only if:
- disease substantially worsens;
- cattle controls are verified;
- local/programme evidence identifies material wildlife contribution;
- vaccination is unavailable/too slow/ineffective there;
- targeted culling shows measurable added benefit under adequate welfare monitoring.

## 13. Type failures / demotions

This case demotes several scaffold tendencies:

- `VIABILITY` cannot be one scalar across cattle/badgers/farms/programme;
- `AFFORDANCE` adds little beyond ordinary deployability/capability language for policy readers;
- `COLLECTIVE AGENCY` is not needed to explain a national programme;
- `FLOURISHING` is not decision-useful here;
- `ANSWER-BACK` must be proxy/evidence based for nonhuman animals and should not be anthropomorphised.

The scaffold cannot substitute for:

- diagnostic test performance;
- wildlife ecology;
- veterinary welfare assessment;

These are not optional details. They determine whether the same structural map should lead to different action.

`STRUCTURAL INTEGRATION != DOMAIN EFFECT ESTIMATION`
