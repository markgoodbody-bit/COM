# Offering or asking for help, without it turning into something else

You do not need any of the rest of this folder to use these questions. There is no vocabulary to learn.

## If you are offering help

**1. What exactly are you offering to do?**
One sentence. If you cannot say it in one, you are offering more than one thing, and the other person cannot agree to some and not others.

**2. What are you *not* offering?**
Name at least one thing. This is the important question and it is the one people skip.

An offer that excludes nothing is a promise of everything, and when it later turns out you did not mean everything, that reads as you letting someone down rather than as a boundary you always had.

**3. When does this end, or when will you both look at it again?**
"Until it's done" is not an end. Pick a date, even a soft one. Help with no end becomes permanent quietly, and nobody ever asks again.

**4. How do they say no — and what actually happens if they do?**
Say the true answer, including when it is "nothing changes." If something *does* change, saying so is not a threat; hiding it is.

If saying no requires your agreement, they cannot say no. They can only apply to say no.

**5. If they think you have got it wrong, who do they tell?**
If the answer is "me", they have no way of disagreeing with you — only a way of telling you they disagree. Name someone else where you can.

## If you are asking for help

The same five, from the other side, plus:

**6. What are you asking for, and what are you not handing over?**
Accepting help with a form is not handing over the decision the form is about. Say which one you meant.

**7. What do you already know, and what do you not know?**
The second list is more useful than the first, and it is the one that makes it possible for someone to tell you that you have asked for the wrong thing.

## If you had to act without asking

Because there was no time, or they were not there.

That is sometimes right. It is **not** voluntary help, and calling it that afterwards is where the damage happens.

Say three things instead:

- **what gave you the standing to act** — and "nothing did, I judged it necessary" is an honest answer;
- **why asking was not possible** — not merely inconvenient;
- **what you now owe** — telling them, undoing what can be undone, and letting them decide whether you do it again.

## The one that matters most

**Someone saying nothing has not agreed.**

Not after a week. Not because the deadline passed. Not because they did not object. If you find yourself counting days until you can treat silence as a yes, the answer you have is no, and what you actually need is to ask again.

## And the honest caveat

Answering these questions well does not make an arrangement fair. Someone who cannot afford to refuse you will still say yes to a perfectly worded offer. These questions make the shape of the thing visible; they do not change who holds the power in it.
