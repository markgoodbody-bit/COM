Case: forty households jointly use a community-owned woodland and hall. Maintenance is deteriorating. Five volunteers currently do most work. Proposed governance introduces dues, elected stewards, published accounts, work-credit alternatives, booking rules, graduated sanctions and a roof reserve. Some residents object that this reduces freedom over a resource they already co-own.

Guiding question:

> Can constraint create durable shared capability without converting collective governance into domination?

## 1. Nearest external owners

This case is heavily externally owned already.

### Commons / Ostrom tradition

Long-running commons research rejects the binary `central command OR atomised private choice`. Robust common-pool institutions often involve:
- clearly defined resource/user boundaries;
- rules fitted to local conditions;
- contribution/provision rules congruent with benefits and conditions;
- participant involvement in modifying operational rules;
- accountable monitoring;
- graduated sanctions;
- low-cost conflict-resolution mechanisms;
- recognised rights to self-organise;
- nested governance where systems cross scales.

The Indiana University Ostrom Workshop still presents these as characteristics of robust CPR institutions rather than a universal recipe.

Relevant sources:
- https://ostromworkshop.indiana.edu/courses-teaching/teaching-tools/ostrom-design/index.html
- Elinor Ostrom, `Coping with Tragedies of the Commons`, Annual Review of Political Science 2 (1999)
- Elinor Ostrom, `A Long Polycentric Journey`, Annual Review of Political Science 13 (2010)

### Modern commons correction: power and equity

A recent Annual Review of Environment and Resources survey treats commons research as a mature field but identifies power and equity as an important continuing frontier, alongside constructing commons in new social settings and stronger causal analysis.

Source:
- https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-environ-112320-113509

### Institutional-choice experiments

Experimental work finds that institutions often improve cooperation once implemented and that endogenously selected institutions can outperform imposed ones, but participants do not always choose them. Institutional cost, residual free-riding and lack of learning opportunities are major barriers. This does not support a universal rule that self-chosen governance always beats external enforcement.

Source:
- https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/experimental-economics/article/choice-of-institutions-to-solve-cooperation-problems-a-survey-of-experimental-research/58268F51A371010FBF6E199AAA53F9C0

### Capability approach

Resources, public goods and formal rights are not equivalent to substantive capability. Whether a person can convert resources into real opportunity depends on personal, social and environmental conversion factors.

Source:
- https://plato.stanford.edu/archives/sum2025/entries/capability-approach/

### Authority decomposition

Recent political-science review work on traditional authorities explicitly separates:
- de facto power;
- downward accountability;
- recognition by the state.

These can vary independently and have different effects. This is directly useful to the project’s wider authority/entrustment frontier.

Source:
- https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-polisci-070723-012434

## 2. Boundary

Material scopes:
- each household/member;
- low-income/time-poor households;
- current heavy volunteers;
- frequent hall users;
- elected stewards;
- woodland/hall as jointly maintained resource system;
- the household association/co-ownership institution;
- future users who inherit maintenance debt or reserves.

Plausible boundary alternatives:
- treat only the forty owners as the governing collective;
- distinguish hall users from woodland users where burdens/benefits differ;
- treat the stewardship body as a delegated actor, not as the collective itself;
- treat the resource + rules + reserve + maintenance capability as a coupled institutional system for some operational questions.

Do not infer a new higher-level individual merely because collective agency exists.

`CO-OWNERSHIP != ONE MIND`
`STEWARDSHIP BODY != COMMUNITY ITSELF`

## 3. Action field

The apparent option `keep current freedom / reject rules` is not neutral.

Under current conditions it also means:
- continued concentration of unpaid maintenance burden on five people;
- declining asset condition;
- no funded roof-replacement capability;
- continuing free-rider incentives;
- likely later emergency decisions under worse clocks.

Conversely, `adopt rules` is not one option. Its practical affordance depends on:
- dues affordability;
- time required for work-credit alternatives;
- whether residents can understand/challenge rules;
- monitoring burden;
- whether stewards have usable but bounded authority;
- whether sanctions distinguish damage/free-riding from inability to contribute;
- whether conflict resolution is cheap enough to use.

`FORMAL WORK-CREDIT OPTION != EQUALLY USABLE CONTRIBUTION ROUTE`

A cash-poor household may be time-rich; another may be cash-poor and time-poor. The conversion conditions matter.

## 4. Viability type check

`Viability` is usable here only as a declared institutional/resource condition, not as intrinsic group normativity.

Candidate declared constraints:
- hall/woodland remain maintainable over the relevant horizon;
- roof/infrastructure failure does not outrun available reserve/repair capacity;
- governance costs do not exclude a material subset of co-owners from substantive use/voice;
- volunteer burden does not remain structurally concentrated on a tiny minority;
- rules remain contestable enough to correct abuse or bad fit.

These are selected governance objectives, not proof of an intrinsic collective self-maintenance norm.

## 5. Capability

Current capability:
- access/use of the hall/woodland;
- some volunteer maintenance;
- informal social coordination.

Missing/weak capability:
- reliable maintenance funding;
- scheduled repair capacity;
- legitimate delegated administration;
- monitoring and conflict resolution;
- burden-sharing that survives volunteer burnout;
- pre-failure roof replacement capacity.

The reserve fund is particularly important.

A present contribution requirement can reduce immediately disposable money/options while creating a future repair capability that does not exist otherwise.

`CURRENT CONTRIBUTION BURDEN CAN CREATE FUTURE CORRECTION CAPACITY`

This is not automatically justified: the burden and rule still require legitimacy, fit and contestability.

## 6. Scale / authority

Different functions should live at different levels.

Household/member level:
- ordinary use;
- contribution choice within legitimate alternatives;
- reporting problems;
- voice/challenge.

Steward level:
- routine bookings;
- published accounting;
- executing pre-authorised maintenance;
- proportionate monitoring/enforcement within a bounded mandate.

Collective/member level:
- changing material dues/rules;
- selecting/removing stewards;
- major reserve use or asset disposition;
- revising sanction policy;
- changing the stewardship mandate.

External/legal level:
- property, safety, charity/company/co-ownership and other applicable law;
- dispute routes that cannot legitimately be closed by local rules.

The cross-domain authority lesson is stronger than a scalar `who decides?` question:

`DE_FACTO_POWER != DOWNWARD_ACCOUNTABILITY != EXTERNAL_RECOGNITION`

A steward may have real operational power without adequate downward accountability. A formally recognised body may lack practical capability. A capable volunteer may lack mandate.

This distinction travels beyond commons governance and is worth preserving in the authority frontier without claiming local invention.

## 7. Plurality / incentives / power

The case is not solved by participation alone.

Recent community-driven-development evidence is a useful warning: devolving public-good choices can deliver goods without producing durable empowerment, and participation itself imposes time/opportunity costs. Exclusive local institutions can remain a problem.

Source:
- https://www.annualreviews.org/content/journals/10.1146/annurev-economics-080217-053339

Therefore inspect:
- who has time to attend meetings;
- whether frequent users dominate rule formation;
- whether the five existing volunteers gain informal veto/status power;
- whether low-income/time-poor households are notionally included but practically absent;
- whether monitoring becomes surveillance/status policing;
- whether sanctions target harmful conduct or poverty/nonconformity.

`VOICE OPPORTUNITY != DOWNWARD ACCOUNTABILITY`
`PARTICIPATION != EMPOWERMENT`

## 8. Persist / commit / release / transform

The relevant posture is COMMIT + ADAPT, not maximum reversibility.

Some commitments must persist long enough to create the good:
- annual funding;
- reserve accumulation;
- maintenance contracts;
- elected terms;
- booking expectations.

But the governance architecture should remain revisable:
- member rule-change route;
- steward removal/re-election;
- accessible conflict resolution;
- published accounts;
- periodic review of dues/work alternatives;
- bounded sanctions;
- external owner/legal routes remain reachable.

Permanent instant reversibility of every contribution/rule would destroy the ability to maintain a shared asset. Permanent unchallengeable commitment would turn stewardship into ownership of the members.

## 9. Action

Adopt the governance package in principle, but only as a bounded self-governance constitution rather than a blank cheque to stewards.

Minimum design:

1. define resource, membership, use and contribution boundaries clearly;
2. fit dues/contribution rules to local conditions and benefits;
3. retain a genuinely usable low-income route, with explicit attention to time poverty rather than assuming work-credit is equivalent to cash relief;
4. elect stewards under a written, limited operational mandate;
5. publish accounts and reserve position;
6. create accountable monitoring tied to resource condition/use, not generalized behavioural surveillance;
7. use graduated sanctions for repeated harmful conduct/free-riding where responsibility is established;
8. keep a rapid low-cost dispute/appeal route that is not controlled solely by the steward whose act is challenged;
9. preserve member authority over material rule/mandate changes;
10. ring-fence a roof/major-maintenance reserve with transparent use triggers;
11. review burden distribution and rule fit periodically;
12. preserve external legal/property/safety authority rather than treating local agreement as sovereign.

Do not freeze a universal voting threshold, sanction schedule or dues formula from the scaffold. Those belong to local/legal/institutional design and evidence.

## 10. Reversal / modification evidence

Materially change the recommendation if evidence shows:
- the resource is not actually threatened by current maintenance under-capacity;
- proposed dues materially exclude co-owners from use or basic household needs;
- work-credit alternatives systematically burden disabled, caring or time-poor households;
- the stewardship body captures decision-making or suppresses challenge;
- sanctions generate more conflict/evasion than cooperation;
- reserve size/triggers are badly mismatched to real asset risks;
- a simpler existing legal/management arrangement performs as well at lower burden;
- households do not share enough of the resource/problem for one governance layer to be appropriate.

## 11. Type failures / demotions

The scaffold mostly survives, but several terms need restraint.

- `VIABILITY` is conditional and governance-designated here; do not anthropomorphise the collective.
- `COLLECTIVE CAPABILITY` is well-typed for maintenance/public-good production; `NEW INDIVIDUAL` is not earned.
- `FLOURISHING` adds little to the immediate decision and should not be forced into the case.
- `EXIT` is weak as the primary correction route because co-ownership, home and belonging make exit costly/non-equivalent.

## 12. Missing structure exposed

No new universal primitive is earned.

But three recurring cross-domain distinctions deserve explicit research attention:

### A. Authority has at least three independently variable conditions

`POWER TO ACT`
`DOWNWARD / AFFECTED-PARTY ACCOUNTABILITY`
`EXTERNAL / LEGAL RECOGNITION`

Do not collapse them into one authority scalar.

### B. Participation burden is itself governance burden

A formally open decision process can systematically exclude those with least time, money, confidence or status.

`ACCESS TO PARTICIPATE != PRACTICAL CAPACITY TO PARTICIPATE`

### C. Commitment can be correction-capacity construction

A reserve, trained steward, maintenance contract or mutual-aid obligation may consume present resources/options specifically to keep future repair reachable before a clock closes.

This connects positive construction to the existing correction-window work without turning all reserves into goods.

## 13. Delta from competent commons reasoning

Most of the substantive governance prescription is already available from strong commons/institutional practice.

Therefore:

`OWNER CREDIT: HIGH`
`LOCAL NOVELTY: LOW`
`CROSS-DOMAIN INTEGRATION VALUE: PLAUSIBLE`

The scaffold's useful delta is mainly integrative:
- connecting formal freedom to substantive capability;
- keeping participation burden visible;
- decomposing authority across power/accountability/recognition;
- treating reserve/commitment as future correction capability;
- carrying the same distinctions into institutions, human-AI delegation and other multi-scale systems.

That is not evidence that TRACE/ME need new fields.
