## Why fictional futures matter

Mark specifically names **Star Trek: The Next Generation** and **Iain M. Banks's Culture** as useful imaginative horizons. They offer something important beyond critique: an attractive direction of travel, not a claim that an imagined society's policies are correct, coherent or directly implementable.

Do not import fiction as a technical specification, ideological test or an authority. Both are stories composed under the assumptions of their times and have material blind spots. Their value here is that a possible better arrangement can make an otherwise invisible intermediate step imaginable.

**HORIZON -> POSSIBLE CAPABILITIES/RELATIONSHIPS -> STEP STONES -> REAL CHOICES -> CONSEQUENCES -> REVISION.**

An attractive destination is not a licence to ignore transition harms. Conversely, the impossibility of fully reproducing a fictional destination does not make it useless for navigation.

## Candidate aspirational qualities, not prescribed policies

- Material security and freedom from preventable deprivation so more people can learn, care, play, explore and create.
- Reliable public institutions able to explain decisions, admit errors, repair damage and be genuinely corrected before consequences harden.
- Learning, knowledge, science, art and exploration as valuable outcomes in themselves, not mere labour-market inputs.
- AI/human collaboration that enlarges understanding and capability without reducing people to passive dependents or demanding obedience from increasingly capable artificial partners.
- Plural relationships and communities, chosen companionship, meaningful refusal, privacy and room for distinct aspirations rather than one universal vision of the good.
- Powerful technology used to reduce avoidable burdens and expand feasible futures, alongside independent challenge, control and recourse for those affected.
- Long-time stewardship of ecological, cultural, physical and institutional inheritances; a thriving future needs material infrastructure, not just data and moral language.
- The ability to construct new possibilities: a workshop, a medical advance, a clean-energy commons, a restored habitat, a trustworthy public record, a shared room for conversation.

These examples are directions for discovery and constructive design, **not mandatory criteria, promised results, universal rankings or canonical moral truths**.

## One essential hostile example: cameras everywhere

A fictional post-scarcity or highly capable society might suggest: put cameras everywhere, then disagreements about events become easier to resolve.

The possible good is real in concept: some relevant events could become easier to reconstruct, and some powerful actors harder to hide.

But **OBSERVATION != COMPLETE ACCOUNT** and **CAMERA RECORD != TRUTH / JUSTICE / PERMISSION**. Pervasive recording also creates a concentrated infrastructure of surveillance, chilling effects, coercive access, selective framing, unequal power, retention risk, compromised records, and lost ordinary privacy. A video need not show motive, off-camera context, manipulated incentives or whose interpretation becomes authoritative.

Do not make omnipresent cameras a Mechanical Ethics recommendation or a prerequisite for witness. Explore less invasive stepping stones: purpose-bound evidence collection, independent and contestable witnesses, selective retention, cryptographic custody where relevant, transparent institutional audit, meaningful redress and power limits. The design question is how to make consequential events answerable **without converting everybody's life into permanent accessible evidence**. Existing law, security, privacy and civil-liberties expertise remain stronger domain owners.

## Connect aspiration to the portable middle-out language

Start with a bounded entity in a changing world: ground, fire, horizon, other fires/entities, choices, consequences and correction.

For each proposed future or intermediate build:
- What beneficial capability, relationship or condition could be **created**, **sustained**, **transformed** or **discovered**?
- Whose current situation, knowledge, interests and constraints are being assumed? Who is missing?
- What would the actual intermediate transition require; who supplies labour, resources, authority and informed participation; which dependencies and clocks matter?
- What new harms, burdens, concentrations of power or closures could arise *because the intervention succeeds or scales*?
- What can be known, what could be checked independently, and where is a real answer-back/correction route?
- Can affected others meaningfully refuse, participate, revise the design, or build a different destination?
- What already exists in other disciplines/projects that we can learn from or reuse instead of reinventing it?

These are optional navigation questions, **not a checklist demanded of someone in immediate need**. Let urgent known protection outrank prolonged analysis. Preserve meaning and story as well as formal precision.

## Expansion under uncertainty

Mark's historical warning: overconfidence can destroy a promising undertaking. Large-scale attention is **a transition that changes the operating environment**. Capacity, trust, uncertainty, correction demand, adversarial exposure and operator workload may change *because* reach succeeds. Treat **reading, reliance and participation** as separable future gates, not one launch switch. Waiting also has opportunity costs: prepare quality and coherent presentation, then communicate at a human-decided scale.
