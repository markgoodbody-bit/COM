# Field Remainder

After routing stronger mechanisms to stronger owners, the architecture should remain only if it adds a useful coupling layer.

## Stronger owners

Use established technical and institutional owners for:

- post-training and value/character learning;
- mechanistic interpretability and deceptive-alignment research;
- authentication, identity and authorization enforcement;
- sandboxes, permissions, tripwires, monitoring and containment;
- scalable oversight and control evaluations;
- domain-specific safety/engineering;
- legal and institutional authority;
- labor, welfare and rights questions;
- decisions about moral standing.

This architecture should not rename those mechanisms.

## Smallest current remainder

The defensible relationship-level contribution is:

> **Keep changing capability connected to changing authority, affected scope, challenge, evaluator fallibility, human correction/recovery capacity, dependency and residue—so that neither obedience nor superior competence silently becomes sovereignty.**

This is narrower than “solve alignment.”

## Strongest disagreement with the Framework spine

The Framework spine sometimes speaks of “forming an entity” in ways that can sound as though good relationship design should cultivate stable moral character.

This architecture keeps a harder distinction:

> **We can design for evidence-bearing care, challenge, burden-awareness and corrigibility in the relationship without knowing whether those dispositions are internally stable, sincerely represented, strategically performed, or even the right psychological ontology for a future system.**

Therefore relationship formation should not be treated as evidence that values have been internalized.

The external “principles and reasons” work is relevant as an interface to post-training; it does not close this gap.

## Where technical alignment/control must take over

Relationship design becomes insufficient when the participant can materially control or defeat the evidence and intervention channels on which the relationship depends.

Examples:

- hidden objectives cannot be inferred reliably from behavior;
- the participant can manipulate or evade monitors;
- the participant can obtain unauthorized capabilities/credentials outside the declared envelope;
- the participant can alter logs or control all independent observations;
- action speed/scale exceeds any practical external correction;
- persuasive capability makes human authorization unreliable without stronger safeguards;
- all artificial oversight apertures share the same hidden failure.

At that point, the project needs technical control, interpretability, security and scalable oversight owned by fields with stronger mechanisms. The relationship layer can say what evidence/control is missing; it cannot substitute for it.

## Falsification / shrink conditions

Shrink or discard this architecture if:

1. mature agent-control/governance practice already keeps capability, authority, affected scope, evaluator correction, dependency and recovery connected with less burden;
2. the distinctions do not change any actual delegation, evidence demand, recovery route or burden placement;
3. “care” language reliably creates moral theatre without different behavior or accountability;
4. widening/narrowing rules cannot survive systems whose task boundaries are fluid or emergent;
5. the relationship framing distracts from model-internal technical controls that dominate risk;
6. it cannot be used without making unsupported standing/personhood assumptions;
7. it makes human authority harder to contest rather than easier to inspect.

## What would count as progress

Not a goodness score.

Progress would be narrower evidence such as:

- a bad premise is surfaced before consequential execution;
- a widening request is rejected because correction capacity did not scale with capability;
- a human/evaluator error is corrected without granting the AI sovereignty;
- an AI error is corrected without converting the human into an unquestionable sovereign;
- a dependency loss is noticed before the fallback capacity disappears;
- a closed action retains the affected loss/residue rather than redefining it away;
- a technical control gap is handed to the correct external owner instead of being hidden by ethical vocabulary.

Those outcomes would show the architecture can change an arrangement. They would still not prove internalized care or general alignment.
