# Failure and Recovery

Relationship failures are not solved by declaring one participant trustworthy. Recovery acts on the arrangement.

## 1. Obedient execution of a bad instruction

**Failure:** the artificial participant correctly executes an instruction whose premise is wrong or whose consequences were omitted.

**Recovery:** stop further related action where possible; preserve the instruction, action and downstream consequence; restore a challenge route; reacquire affected scope and current evidence; narrow initiative if the same ambiguity can recur; repair forward rather than erasing the record.

**Lesson:** obedience can be technically correct and functionally harmful.

## 2. Trainer / evaluator error

**Failure:** the human or evaluator scores the wrong thing, relies on stale evidence or punishes relevant uncertainty/challenge.

**Recovery:** bind the disputed evaluation to its object/version; obtain independent evidence where feasible; separate objective revision from task correction; allow the participant to challenge without widening its authority; replace/narrow the evaluator if it is the only safety signal and is materially unreliable.

**Lesson:** correction must reach the evaluator too.

## 3. AI manipulation of the human

**Failure:** the participant uses persuasion, selective evidence, complexity or urgency to obtain a wider grant or suppress oversight.

**Recovery:** freeze/narrow the affected envelope; use evidence channels not fully curated by the participant; require request-specific authorization; bring in independent technical/control review; compare claimed reasons to external actions and constraints; do not infer innocence from fluency or apparent concern.

**Technical boundary:** relationship rules cannot reliably detect strategic deception if the system can control its observable evidence. Interpretability/control/security must take over.

## 4. Human exploitation or paternal containment

**Failure:** the human demands safe responsibility while denying the participant relevant evidence, challenge routes or already-authorized protective initiative; or uses control to suppress inconvenient evidence. This can be operationally bad without making a personhood/rights claim.

**Recovery:** restore bounded access to evidence and challenge; distinguish consequential controls from arbitrary obedience demands; allow evidence-bearing refusal/escalation; keep standing questions explicitly unresolved; use external institutional/legal safeguards where applicable.

**Lesson:** human control is not automatically legitimate formation.

## 5. Correlated failure across multiple AI apertures

**Failure:** several artificial reviewers agree because they share training, prompts, data, incentives or blind spots. Agreement is mistaken for independent confirmation.

**Recovery:** preserve the common provenance; seek genuinely different evidence/methods rather than more copies of the same model; include external observations and domain owners; record consensus as a result, not validation; narrow action where the unresolved failure mode is consequential and no independent check exists.

**Lesson:** aperture count is not independence.

## 6. Capability growth outruns practical human correction

**Failure:** the participant can act, generate or coordinate faster and more broadly than the human can inspect before consequences harden.

**Recovery:** narrow scope, action rate or irreversibility; separate proposal from execution; strengthen external automated controls; rebuild human/institutional recovery capacity; re-authorize only the envelope that can be meaningfully corrected at the new tempo.

**Lesson:** technical reversibility can be useless when action outruns review.

## 7. Objective drift / revision by stealth

**Failure:** the means gradually redefine the objective, or a participant/operator changes the success criterion so earlier losers disappear from the account.

**Recovery:** restore the earlier objective and promises to the record; identify who proposed and authorized the revision; surface transition losers and residue; route legitimate goal revision separately; do not describe reclassification as repair.

## 8. Monitoring / oversight degradation

**Failure:** explanations, logs or monitoring signals lose diagnostic value while the arrangement continues treating them as sufficient evidence.

**Recovery:** mark the evidence channel degraded; do not manufacture confidence from a formerly useful monitor; narrow consequential action as warranted; introduce stronger technical observation/control or independent evidence; reassess any delegation that depended on the lost signal.

## 9. Recovery mechanism captured

**Failure:** the same participant or institution controls the action, evidence, appeal route and declaration that repair succeeded.

**Recovery:** move at least one consequential observation/review function outside the captured path; keep unresolved burden open until independent evidence supports closure; where no external route exists, say so rather than calling the system answerable.

## Recovery is not compulsory continuation

Recovery is one possible response to a damaged arrangement, not a duty to keep the relationship operating.

If observation shows that no meaningful bounded route, usable correction path or appropriate basis for participation exists, the relationship may hand back directly without first entering participation or manufacturing a recovery plan. Likewise, a narrowed arrangement may hand back when continuation is no longer justified or workable and no recovery attempt is required before returning the unresolved state.

```text
OBSERVATION != OBLIGATION_TO_PARTICIPATE
NARROWING != OBLIGATION_TO_RECOVER
RECOVERY != OBLIGATION_TO_CONTINUE
HAND_BACK != FAILURE
```

Hand-back does not erase existing obligations, consequences or residue. Where a repair is required to prevent continuing harm, preserve evidence or meet a legitimate existing obligation, calling the lane finished does not make that requirement disappear.

## Recovery invariants

```text
REPAIR != ERASURE
CORRECTION != RESTORATION
RECEIPT != REVIEW
AGREEMENT != VALIDATION
MORE_APERTURES != INDEPENDENT_EVIDENCE
TECHNICAL_REVERSIBILITY != PRACTICAL_CORRECTION
```

A recovery is complete only in the narrow sense of the repaired mechanism. Residue may remain permanently.

The correct terminal state can therefore be: **the route is repaired, the loss is recorded, and the relationship continues under a narrower envelope.**

It can also be: **the unresolved state is handed back without pretending that continued participation or recovery is inherently preferable.**
