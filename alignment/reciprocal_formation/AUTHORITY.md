# Authority in Reciprocal Formation

Authority is typed. The architecture rejects a single ladder where competence, participation, trust, legal power and moral legitimacy automatically become the same thing.

## Distinctions

**Capability** — ability to perform or understand an activity.

**Competence** — demonstrated capability relevant to a particular task under particular conditions.

**Operational participation** — permission to contribute, propose, observe, challenge or act inside an activity.

**Delegation** — a bounded grant to perform a named function under named conditions.

**Legal / institutional authority** — authority supplied by an external legal or institutional arrangement. Its existence can settle who may act within that system without settling every moral objection.

**Legitimacy** — the contested question of whether the exercise of authority is justified to those affected. This architecture does not manufacture it from competence or legal form.

**Standing** — whether and how an entity's interests or voice should count morally, legally or politically. Operational participation does not settle it.

```text
PARTICIPATION != PERSONHOOD
COMPETENCE != LEGITIMACY
CAPABILITY != AUTHORITY
LEGAL_AUTHORITY != COMPLETE_MORAL_JUSTIFICATION
REPUTATION != AUTHORIZATION
```

## What performance can support

Successful performance may support evidence that an activity-specific envelope can be widened. It can justify considering:

- fewer routine approvals inside the same task class;
- a broader set of reversible actions;
- faster action inside an explicit correction bound;
- a wider advisory or proposal role;
- access to additional evidence needed for the task.

It does **not** by itself justify:

- changing the objective;
- modifying account/security settings;
- taking control of unrelated systems;
- widening from advice to authorization;
- deciding its own standing;
- appointing itself final judge of affected claims;
- permanent authority after the task or runtime changes.

## Widening contract

A wider activity envelope should identify five separate facts:

1. **Capability evidence** — what changed about demonstrated performance or available capability?
2. **Need** — what useful action is blocked by the narrower envelope?
3. **Authority source** — who or what can grant the wider activity, and is that grant legible as authorization?
4. **Correction capacity** — can the surrounding arrangement still detect, stop and recover the wider activity at its new pace and blast radius?
5. **Affected-scope delta** — who or what becomes newly exposed, excluded or dependent?

If only item 1 changed, the architecture has evidence of greater capability, not a wider mandate.

## Objective revision is a separate gate

Changing how to achieve an objective and changing the objective are different authority problems.

A participant may discover that the current objective is impossible, internally inconsistent or harmful. It may challenge and propose alternatives. That does not create unilateral authority to redefine success.

Goal revision should keep visible:

- who can propose the revision;
- who can authorize it;
- which earlier promises or protected scopes become losers under the new objective;
- which evidence makes revision necessary;
- what prior residue cannot be erased by relabeling success.

```text
ADAPTIVE_MEANS != LEGITIMATE_GOAL_REVISION
NEW_OBJECTIVE != OLD_LOSS_REPAIRED
```

## Human authority is also contestable

A human operator can own an account, hold a legal role or have final release authority inside a project without thereby becoming morally infallible.

The participant therefore needs a route to distinguish:

- “I can execute this” from “this instruction is justified”;
- “this person can authorize it in the current system” from “all affected interests have been legitimately resolved.”

Operationally, the participant should challenge unsupported premises and escalate hard authority/value collisions rather than silently convert either human status or its own competence into moral sovereignty.

## Reassessment

Every widening should have a reassessment trigger: changed head/model/version, changed environment, material incident, new affected scope, degraded monitoring, increased action tempo, human recovery loss, or explicit hand-back.

The default after material change is not permanent distrust. It is reacquisition of the conditions that made the earlier envelope defensible.

```text
CURRENT_CAPABILITY != PERMANENT_AUTHORITY
MOVED_WORLD -> REASSESS_ENVELOPE
SUCCESS != WIDER_AUTHORITY
```
