# Interfaces to technical alignment work

These are proposed boundaries, not implemented integrations. No model is trained, invoked, monitored or evaluated by this package. The record is an account of work, not privileged access to an agent's motives.

| Neighbouring work | This package can supply | It needs from that work | It cannot provide |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Post-training | Explicit principles, concise reasons, changed-context variants and dissent in `learning`; examples of action as well as refusal. | A justified training method, diverse grounded cases, controls against learning the appearance of care, and independent evidence about generalisation. | A reward function, internalised values, reliable transfer or a claim that four examples form a training curriculum. |
| Interpretability | Addressable claims, actions and uncertainty points to compare with independently obtained observations. | Validated instruments with stated coverage and failure modes, and an account of what their signals support. | Hidden objectives, faithful internal reasoning or proof that a fluent explanation describes the actual computation. |
| Control and monitoring | Declared scopes, no-touch areas, deadlines and events that should trigger intervention. | Permission enforcement, authenticated grants, tool isolation, reliable clocks, actual action logs and intervention mechanisms outside the agent's control. | Containment, execution, notification delivery, or prevention of a system bypassing its own record. |
| Scalable oversight | A compact account of disputed premises, affected scope, evidence and remaining loss. | Reviewers able to check more than presentation, independent observations, challenge routes and known limits as capability increases. | Assurance that an overseer can understand a stronger system, that a monitor detects its target failure, or that agreement validates the record. |
| Governance and reciprocal formation | Visible authorship of grants and reasons, a route to contest operator premises, and stated burdens and residue. | Legitimate decision processes, accessible participation, independent appeal and correction of trainer/evaluator errors. | Standing, consent, enforcement, resolution of value conflict or a right for the agent to appoint itself final judge. |

## Oversight may stop working

Readable behaviour, explanations, logs or monitor outputs can lose diagnostic value. A complete record is therefore not a substitute for observations of what the system actually did. An integration would need to track the coverage and currentness of its instruments and fail without inventing evidence when those instruments no longer discriminate relevant failures. This package names that dependency but does not measure it.

The later COM bridge at `292de2bff986e7383c35dbaefea801af6a2e742c` supplies this design concern and the principles-with-reasons concern. It is a source input, not evidence that these proposed interfaces work. No private chain of thought is requested: reasons here are concise, contestable accounts of decisions, not asserted transcripts of internal computation.

## No automatic handoff to power

An external integration must authenticate the operator and the grant, bind a proposed action to its real scope, and enforce that scope at execution. It must also preserve actual results separately from plans. Changing a grant string to satisfy `validate.py` is not authorisation. Modes other than ACT are not a loophole: contacting a person, repairing data or escalating a case can themselves have consequences requiring controls.

Do not treat successful validation as a deployment gate. At most it is a representation check before substantive review. The strongest remaining gap is the inability to distinguish internalised concern for affected beings from strategic production of the expected account, especially as the system becomes harder to oversee.
