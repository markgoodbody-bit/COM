# Worked Representation — The Riverbank Plan

Status: **CONSTRUCTED CIVIC / ECOLOGICAL SCENE / OWNER-FIRST LEARNING AID / NOT PLANNING OR LEGAL ADVICE / NOT A STANDING RULE / NOT EVIDENCE / NOT A TEST**

> **HOW CAN WE MAKE A BETTER FUTURE?**

This scene exists because the [Representation, Voice & Unresolved Standing](representation-standing.md) route already carries strong distinctions, but they remain hard to use until several kinds of representation collide in one decision.

The scene is fictional.

It does **not** propose a planning method, legal-capacity rule, child-participation standard, environmental-standing doctrine or theory of future-generations representation.

Useful owner routes include:

- [UN Committee on the Rights of the Child — General Comment No. 12](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=YDah9tP9EQ4yJGUbd1XDmaOoMVuVv2KW4ANWL9tpK079%2BonDsRJ0Ml%2FiNhKKcf4S8cVgG4Sb3HsKSs9SKqy5zA%3D%3D) — the child’s right to express views in matters affecting them and have those views given due weight;
- [UN Declaration on Future Generations](https://www.un.org/pact-for-the-future/en/declaration-future-generations) — safeguarding the needs and interests of future generations without pretending they can presently consent;
- [IPBES Values Assessment](https://www.ipbes.net/ar/va) — diverse values of nature and the need to make value assumptions visible in decision-making;
- [Future Generations Commissioner for Wales](https://futuregenerations.wales/discover/about-future-generations-commissioner/commissioner-for-wales/) — one real institutional example of advice, challenge and long-term scrutiny without claiming to be the literal voice of future persons.

The cross-domain question is narrower:

> **How do we keep materially affected scopes present when some can answer directly, some need support, some can only be represented through evidence and advocacy, and some do not yet exist — without turning the representative into the represented entity?**

---

## Scene

A fictional town is redesigning a riverside corridor after repeated winter flooding.

The area currently contains:

- a narrow riverside path;
- a small playing field used by local children;
- a wet woodland and reed margin used by birds, insects and other wildlife;
- several mature trees;
- sixty public parking spaces;
- a row of small shops nearby;
- an ageing flood wall that protects part of the neighbourhood but constrains the river during high flow.

The town has funding for one major redesign.

A proposed plan would:

- set part of the riverbank back to create more flood storage;
- restore wetland habitat;
- replace the narrow path with a wider accessible route;
- remove forty parking spaces;
- relocate the playing field slightly inland;
- add low-level lighting to the main route;
- reserve one currently undeveloped parcel so a later flood-storage expansion remains possible.

No option is burden-free.

Current affected scopes include:

- children who use the field and riverbank;
- residents with limited mobility who cannot currently use much of the path;
- nearby shopkeepers and customers who use parking;
- current residents exposed to flood risk;
- people who value the woodland and habitat;
- wildlife and ecological processes;
- maintenance staff and local taxpayers;
- future residents who may inherit a different flood regime and a different town layout.

```text
MANY_AFFECTED_SCOPES
!=
ONE_SHARED_PREFERENCE
```

---

## 1. The first mistake would be to begin with proxies

The town initially receives four formal submissions:

1. a residents' association says families want the playing field left exactly where it is;
2. a conservation group says the wet woodland must be protected from lighting and path expansion;
3. a business group says parking loss will damage the local centre;
4. a long-term planning adviser says the town should preserve land for future flood adaptation.

All four may carry useful evidence.

None is automatically the voice of every entity it mentions.

```text
REPRESENTATIVE_SUBMISSION != REPRESENTED_SCOPE_SPEAKING
GROUP_NAME != COMPLETE_MEMBERSHIP_VIEW
ADVOCACY != OWNERSHIP_OF_INTEREST
```

Before treating proxy claims as the available voice, the town asks which affected people can be reached more directly.

---

## 2. Children are not silent merely because adults usually speak for them

The original residents' submission says:

> local children will be devastated if the field moves.

That might be true.

But children using the site can answer for themselves to some extent.

The town therefore creates several supported routes:

- a site walk with school groups;
- a large physical map on which children can mark where they play, avoid, cross and gather;
- short small-group discussions rather than one formal evening hearing;
- a way to submit drawings or audio comments;
- a clear explanation of what is actually fixed by flood constraints and what remains open to change.

The result is more differentiated than the adult proxy claim.

Some children strongly want the current football field preserved.

Others say:

- the existing riverside path feels unsafe on bicycles;
- the woodland is one of the most interesting parts of the site;
- they would accept a moved field if it remained large enough and did not become a formal fenced sports pitch;
- they want places to sit near the water;
- some dislike bright lighting, while others say the route feels unusable after dark in winter.

```text
CHILD_VIEW != ADULT_INFERENCE
SUPPORT_CAN_INCREASE_REACHABLE_VOICE
```

The adults' representation is not discarded. It is now one evidence source rather than a substitute for all child views.

---

## 3. Support can change the apparent preference

A public meeting held in an upstairs room had initially produced almost no input from people with mobility impairments.

One official draft therefore recorded:

> no significant accessibility concern was raised.

That conclusion is withdrawn.

The absence was partly created by the participation environment.

The town adds:

- an accessible venue;
- remote participation;
- a daylight site visit;
- tactile and high-contrast route materials;
- direct discussion with current path users and non-users.

New evidence shows that several residents do not use the riverside at all because the existing surface, width and gradient make it impractical.

```text
NO_INPUT_OBSERVED
!=
NO_PREFERENCE_EXISTS

LOW_PARTICIPATION
MAY_MEAN
CHANNEL_FAILED
```

This is why support should precede substitution where direct participation is actually reachable.

---

## 4. The ecosystem supplies evidence, not a single first-person vote

The wet woodland cannot attend the meeting.

That does not make it absent from the decision.

Ecologists can provide evidence about:

- habitat condition;
- species presence;
- hydrology;
- likely effects of lighting;
- connectivity with upstream/downstream habitat;
- consequences of tree removal;
- uncertainty in those assessments.

Local residents and conservation groups can also express human relationships and values attached to the woodland.

But the decision record keeps these claim types separate:

```text
ECOLOGICAL_CONDITION_EVIDENCE
!=
HUMAN_VALUE_CLAIM
!=
ECOSYSTEM_FIRST_PERSON_PREFERENCE
```

The conservation group may legitimately advocate strongly for habitat protection.

It should not be recorded as having discovered what `the river wants`.

```text
REPRESENTING_NATURE
!=
DISCOVERING_ONE_NATURE_VOICE
```

This matters because different ecological objects can also conflict: more open flood storage may help one habitat while tree loss burdens another.

---

## 5. A proxy can be challenged without erasing the interest it protects

The business group states that forty parking spaces are essential to shop survival.

Rather than accepting or dismissing the claim based on who made it, the town asks for its basis.

Evidence gathered includes:

- occupancy at different times;
- customer travel surveys;
- loading needs;
- accessible parking requirements;
- public-transport and walking access;
- turnover patterns from comparable street closures;
- uncertainty about how behaviour may change after redesign.

The business group's role remains legitimate advocacy.

But:

```text
INTEREST_GROUP_CLAIM != ESTABLISHED_CAUSAL_FACT
CHALLENGE_PROXY_EVIDENCE != ERASE_PROXY_SCOPE
```

Questioning the evidence does not mean shops cease to matter.

---

## 6. Future residents cannot consent to the current plan

The undeveloped parcel could be sold now to fund more of the current scheme.

Keeping it open carries an immediate opportunity cost.

The town's long-term adviser argues that retaining the parcel protects future flood-storage options if rainfall and river conditions worsen.

Future residents cannot answer whether they would prefer:

- a larger park now;
- more housing now;
- lower current tax;
- future flood storage;
- some technology not yet available.

So the record refuses the phrase:

> future generations want us to reserve this land.

Instead it says:

> present evidence indicates that selling the parcel would remove one potentially important adaptation route from people who will inherit the later flood regime.

```text
FUTURE_INTEREST != PRESENTLY_KNOWABLE_PREFERENCE
SAFEGUARDING_FUTURE_CAPABILITY != FABRICATED_CONSENT
```

The adviser represents a long-term concern and evidence base, not future persons' literal voice.

---

## 7. Representation needs typed claim labels

The planning team begins tagging important claims in ordinary prose with their basis.

For example:

```text
DIRECT_VIEW:
"Several children asked to keep informal play space near the river."

SUPPORTED_VIEW:
"Residents reached through an accessible site walk identified the current path as practically unusable."

PROXY_ADVOCACY:
"The conservation group argues that lighting should be removed from the wet woodland edge."

CONDITION_EVIDENCE:
"Ecological survey identifies light-sensitive habitat in this section."

FUTURE_INTEREST_ARGUMENT:
"Retaining the undeveloped parcel preserves a possible later flood-storage route."

DECISION_JUDGEMENT:
"The authority proposes a lit main route with an unlit habitat edge and reserves the parcel."
```

This is not a new project schema.

It is simply enough separation to prevent one category silently becoming another.

```text
PROXY_ADVOCACY != CONDITION_EVIDENCE
CONDITION_EVIDENCE != CONSENT
FUTURE_INTEREST_ARGUMENT != FUTURE_VOICE
DECISION_JUDGEMENT != REPRESENTED_ENTITY_CHOICE
```

---

## 8. Multiple representatives can disagree about the same scope

Two environmental groups disagree.

One prioritises preserving every mature tree.

Another accepts removal of some trees if the resulting wetland has materially greater flood and habitat function.

Neither disagreement proves that environmental representation is defective.

It reveals that:

```text
ONE_SCOPE
CAN SUPPORT
MULTIPLE_REASONABLE_VALUE_READINGS
```

The town records both the ecological evidence and the value disagreement instead of selecting one group as `the environmental voice`.

This is especially important where the affected scope cannot directly contradict a proxy.

---

## 9. Representation does not transfer decision authority automatically

A youth panel can advise.

A conservation group can advocate.

An accessibility organisation can identify practical barriers.

A future-generations adviser can challenge short-term reasoning.

An ecologist can provide condition evidence.

None automatically becomes the planning authority.

```text
VOICE != VOTE_BY_DEFAULT
EVIDENCE != MANDATE
ADVOCACY != SOVEREIGNTY
EXPERTISE != AUTHORIZATION
```

The body holding the actual decision remains responsible for how it uses or rejects those inputs under the relevant owner law and governance.

Representation makes affected scopes more present. It does not make the decision disappear.

---

## 10. The decision should expose consequence, not just consultation count

A weak participation report might say:

```text
CHILDREN CONSULTED: YES
ACCESSIBILITY GROUP CONSULTED: YES
ENVIRONMENT GROUPS CONSULTED: YES
BUSINESSES CONSULTED: YES
LONG_TERM IMPACT CONSIDERED: YES
```

That proves little.

The stronger record says what changed.

In the constructed scene:

- the field is moved but remains informal and unfenced because child input changed the original sports-only design;
- the main path is widened and made accessible;
- lighting is narrowed to the main route and kept away from the most sensitive habitat edge;
- accessible parking is retained closer to the route even as total parking falls;
- the undeveloped parcel remains reserved for later adaptation rather than being sold immediately;
- shop loading arrangements change after evidence showed loading mattered more than total customer parking at certain times;
- a five-year ecological and access review is added because several effects remain uncertain.

```text
INPUT_COLLECTED != INPUT_COULD_AFFECT_DECISION
PARTICIPATION_RECEIPT != ANSWERABILITY
```

The authority also records material views it did **not** follow and why.

---

## 11. Rejecting a representative claim does not erase the represented scope

Suppose the final plan rejects the conservation group's demand for zero lighting anywhere on the riverbank.

The record should not conclude:

> environmental objections were rejected.

Instead it may say:

- ecological sensitivity remains a protected consideration;
- condition evidence supported an unlit habitat edge;
- accessibility/safety evidence supported limited lighting on the main route;
- the zero-lighting proposal was not adopted;
- monitoring remains because some ecological effect is uncertain.

```text
PROXY_PROPOSAL_REJECTED
!=
REPRESENTED_SCOPE_DISMISSED
```

This prevents a disagreement with the representative from turning into disappearance of the interest.

---

## 12. A direct voice can still be incomplete or conflicted

Direct participation is not magic either.

Children disagree with each other.

Residents with mobility impairments have different priorities.

Business owners vary in their dependence on parking.

Current residents may reasonably prefer options that impose more long-term burden on future residents.

```text
DIRECT_VOICE != UNITARY_VOICE
SELF_REPRESENTATION != INFALLIBILITY
```

The purpose of supporting direct voice is not to manufacture unanimity.

It is to stop avoidable proxy substitution from occurring before the entity's own participation has actually been tried.

---

## 13. The decision changes future representation conditions

The design does more than allocate today's space.

It changes what future participants will be able to experience and contest.

For example:

- a more accessible path lets more residents become direct users rather than absent non-users;
- preserving the flood-storage parcel retains a future decision;
- destroying the wet woodland would remove ecological evidence and relationships that later people might have valued;
- a highly engineered fixed wall could narrow later adaptation choices;
- an open monitoring record gives future decision-makers evidence about whether today's assumptions were wrong.

```text
TODAY'S_DECISION
CHANGES
TOMORROW'S_VOICE / EVIDENCE / OPTIONS
```

Representation is therefore partly about the future conditions under which later entities can answer back.

---

## 14. Unresolved standing does not force binary treatment

Suppose participants disagree about whether the wetland or river should have any moral or legal standing beyond established environmental protections.

The planning decision does not need to settle that metaphysical/legal debate before it can preserve ecological evidence and avoid unnecessary irreversible loss.

Nor does preserving the wetland for review prove that the ecosystem is a legal or moral person.

```text
STANDING_UNRESOLVED
!=
SCOPE_ERASED

PRECAUTIONARY_PRESERVATION
!=
PERSONHOOD_RECOGNITION
```

The same logical discipline matters in other unresolved-standing domains, including possible artificial entities, without claiming the categories are morally equivalent.

---

## 15. What good representation looks like in this scene

No representative earns legitimacy merely by claiming to care more.

The useful construction is a layered representation field:

```text
DIRECT SIGNAL WHERE REACHABLE
+ SUPPORT THAT INCREASES REACHABLE SELF-EXPRESSION
+ PROXY ADVOCACY LABELLED AS PROXY
+ CONDITION EVIDENCE LABELLED AS EVIDENCE
+ FUTURE INTERESTS WITHOUT FABRICATED CONSENT
+ MULTIPLE PROXIES ALLOWED TO DISAGREE
+ DECISION AUTHORITY KEPT DISTINCT
+ REASONS / CONSEQUENCE VISIBLE
+ REVIEW WHEN UNCERTAINTY PERSISTS
```

The practical lesson is:

> **keep the affected scope present while keeping honest the distance between the scope, the evidence about it, the person speaking for it, and the institution deciding what to do.**

---

## What this scene does not establish

It does not establish:

- the legality or planning merits of any real riverbank scheme;
- that children's expressed preferences must determine every decision affecting them;
- that all people can participate directly with enough support;
- that proxies are inherently suspect;
- that ecosystems have or lack legal/moral personhood;
- that ecological science can determine moral priority by itself;
- that a future-generations adviser literally represents future people's consent;
- that preserving future options is always superior to current use;
- that accessibility, ecology, business, flood safety and recreation can be combined without real trade-offs;
- that one consultation method is universally adequate;
- that artificial systems are equivalent to children, animals, ecosystems or future humans;
- that TRACE or Mechanical Ethics has been validated.

```text
REPRESENTATION != POSSESSION
PROXY_VOICE != SELF_REPRESENTATION
SUPPORT != SUBSTITUTE
EVIDENCE != CONSENT
FUTURE_INTEREST != FUTURE_PREFERENCE
CAPACITY != MORAL_STANDING
BUILD != PROOF
```

## Owner boundary

Child rights, disability/support practice, planning, environmental governance, ecological science, future-generations institutions, public law and contested standing doctrines remain the owners of real cases.

This project keeps only the cross-domain relation:

```text
AFFECTED_SCOPE PRESENT
WITHOUT
FABRICATED VOICE
```

and the operational sequence:

```text
CAN DIRECT VOICE BE REACHED?
-> CAN SUPPORT REACH MORE OF IT?
-> WHAT STILL REQUIRES REPRESENTATION?
-> WHAT IS THE REPRESENTATIVE CLAIMING?
-> WHAT EVIDENCE / CONFLICTS SUPPORT THAT CLAIM?
-> WHO ACTUALLY HOLDS DECISION AUTHORITY?
-> DID THE INPUT HAVE A CONSEQUENCE?
-> WHAT REMAINS UNCERTAIN / REVIEWABLE?
```
