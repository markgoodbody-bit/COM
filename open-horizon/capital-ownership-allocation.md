# Capital, Ownership & Allocation

Status: **OPEN HORIZON LEARNING ROUTE / OWNER-FIRST SYNTHESIS / NOT CANON / NOT TRACE OR ME CHANGE / NOT FINANCIAL OR LEGAL ADVICE**  
Parent map: [Open Horizon](README.md)  
Live exploration ledger: [COM #74](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/COM/issues/74)

> **HOW CAN WE MAKE A BETTER FUTURE?**

This page carries forward one part of the Open Horizon that becomes easy to miss when financing is treated as something that happens after the ethical or institutional design is complete:

> capital allocation is part of causal architecture. It changes which projects become physically possible, which commitments can be sustained, who can wait, who can absorb failure, who receives upside and which future actions remain reachable.

That does **not** mean money morally owns the future, that investors are presumptively exploitative, or that the project has a general theory of ownership or distributive justice.

This is a learning route into existing owner-first exploration. It does not promote `CAPITAL_ALLOCATION`, `RISK_BEARING`, ownership structure or any financing model into TRACE or Mechanical Ethics.

The most developed dedicated Horizon pass is [Capital, ownership and control: what does money actually entitle you to?](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/COM/issues/74#issuecomment-5633605464). Detailed sources, field cases and later corrections remain in the live ledger. Later supported owner evidence wins if it changes this summary.

## What the project has learned so far

### Capital allocation changes future action space

Some worthwhile futures require physical plant, working capital, long-term finance, reserves, skills, infrastructure or demand commitments before they can become real.

So financing is not merely a later distribution question. It can determine which options ever become usable.

```text
CAPITAL_ALLOCATION -> FUTURE_ACTION_SPACE
FINANCE_STRUCTURE -> REACHABLE_ACTION_SPACE
FORMAL_PLAN != FINANCEABLE_CAPABILITY
```

This complements the Positive Construction route: capability creation may require durable commitments, and capital is one of the mechanisms through which those commitments become material.

But:

```text
CAPITAL_ALLOCATION -> FUTURE_ACTION_SPACE
!=
CAPITAL_OWNER -> MORAL_OWNERSHIP_OF_FUTURE
```

### Capital supplied is not the same as risk borne

A project described as privately financed may also depend on public guarantees, grants, public land, infrastructure, workers, local communities, customers, future maintenance obligations or other parties carrying loss that does not appear on the investor's balance sheet.

The Horizon therefore preserves:

```text
CAPITAL_PROVIDED != RISK_BORNE
NOMINAL_PRIVATE_FINANCE != FULL_PRIVATE_RISK
RESIDUAL_FINANCIAL_CLAIM != TOTAL_SOCIAL_RESIDUAL
```

The practical question is not simply who wrote the cheque. It is which risks each participant can influence, absorb, diversify, exit, transfer or leave behind for someone else.

### Risk-bearing can justify protection and return without creating sovereignty

Real financial exposure can justify bounded protections relevant to that exposure: information rights, covenants, board representation, consent over actions that materially change the risk, contractual return, or other domain-appropriate safeguards.

It does not automatically justify control over unrelated purposes or affected-party interests.

```text
FINANCIAL_PROTECTION_RIGHT != GENERAL_MISSION_CONTROL
RISK_BEARING != SOVEREIGNTY
PROTECT_CAPITAL != OWN_THE_PURPOSE
```

This is not an argument that investors should never hold control. In some stages or structures, significant control may be an explicit part of the bargain required to make capital available.

The Horizon's stronger requirement is to make the bargain visible: what risk the control protects, how long it lasts, what domains it reaches, what changes when the risk profile changes, and who else carries consequences that the capital contract does not fully price.

### Public support is also part of the risk-and-value stack

Public guarantees, subsidy, land, infrastructure, regulation or de-risking can make an otherwise unfinanceable project possible.

When public action absorbs material downside, the public contribution should not disappear from the story when returns and control are allocated.

```text
SOCIALISED_RISK != ZERO_PUBLIC_CONTRIBUTION
PUBLIC_DOWNSIDE_SUPPORT -> PUBLIC_CLAIM_ON_TERMS / OUTCOMES
```

The appropriate public claim is contextual. It might involve pricing, performance conditions, transparency, clawback, public or community benefit, ownership, stronger review, or no additional claim where the existing bargain is already justified.

The project does not provide a formula for selecting among them.

### Contractual risk transfer is not always causal risk transfer

A contract can assign a risk to a party that cannot actually absorb it when the bad state arrives.

```text
CONTRACTUAL_RISK_TRANSFER != CAUSAL_RISK_TRANSFER
```

If a project company becomes insolvent under the event, or a supplier exits, the physical/social consequence may still fall on the public, workers, customers or place.

Owner disciplines in infrastructure, insurance and finance already have richer tools for modelling these questions. Use them.

### Capital can exit faster than communities, workers and infrastructure

Different participants often live on different clocks.

```text
CAPITAL_EXIT_TIME != COMMUNITY_EXIT_TIME
CAPITAL_EXIT_TIME != INFRASTRUCTURE_LIFETIME
```

An investor may sell after a few years while land commitments, local dependence, maintenance, environmental consequences or workforce effects persist for decades.

That makes transfer/change-of-control terms consequential rather than administrative detail. The question becomes what obligations, capabilities, mission commitments and answerability routes survive ownership change.

### Ownership and control can be deliberately separated

Cooperative, employee-owned, community-benefit, trust and other institutional forms demonstrate that governance rights do not have to track financial contribution in one universal way.

That proves design space exists; it does not prove those forms are generally superior.

```text
CAPITAL_WEIGHTED_VOTING != ECONOMIC_NECESSITY
OWNERSHIP != VOICE
VOICE != OWNERSHIP
```

The same separation matters in AI-related economic change:

```text
PRODUCTIVITY_GAIN != WORKER_INCOME_GAIN
TIME_SAVED != TIME_RETURNED_TO_WORKER
AI_ACCESS != SHARE_OF_AI_CAPITAL_RETURN
TECHNOLOGY_DIFFUSION != INCOME_DIFFUSION
```

Creating value, receiving value, owning productive assets and having consequential voice are different relationships. Institutions can couple them in different ways.

### Democratic or mission control has financing costs

The inverse warning matters too.

If outside capital is asked to bear substantial residual risk while having little ability to protect itself from later decisions, it may demand a higher return, stronger covenants, different security or simply decline to invest.

```text
DEMOCRATIC_CONTROL_HAS_FINANCING_COSTS
MISSION_CONSTRAINT_CAN_CHANGE_COST_OF_CAPITAL
```

Those costs are not proof that democratic or mission control is wrong. They are real constraints that should be surfaced rather than moralised away.

A constructive design may respond with patient/public capital, guarantees, lower leverage, phased ownership, smaller scale, slower development, different return structures or a different project altogether.

### Capital structure is governance structure

Debt service, covenants, liquidity needs, investor time horizons and exit expectations shape which actions remain reachable even before a formal governance vote occurs.

High leverage can reduce resilience and experimentation. Short-horizon equity can make sale/growth timing dominate long-payback capability. Grant dependence can bend activity toward funding categories.

```text
CAPITAL_STRUCTURE_IS_GOVERNANCE_STRUCTURE
FINANCIAL_OBLIGATION -> FUTURE_CONSTRAINT
```

This does not imply financiers secretly control everything. It means financial architecture is one of the mechanisms that shapes practical agency and future possibility.

## The central tension

The constructive object currently visible is:

```text
BUILD ENOUGH FINANCIAL CAPABILITY TO MAKE THE FUTURE REAL
+
KEEP THE RESULTING CLAIMS / CONTROL ANSWERABLE TO THE FULL RISK AND CONSEQUENCE STACK
```

Neither half is sufficient alone.

- capital-starved projects can leave worthwhile futures permanently theoretical;
- hidden public or community risk can make private-return stories misleading;
- investor protection can become mission sovereignty if its scope is not bounded;
- mission purity can make projects unfinanceable if its real costs are denied;
- local/community ownership can still exclude, capture or pressure those unable to invest;
- liquid ownership can detach control from long-lived affected places and obligations;
- public subsidy can build private asset value without building durable public capability;
- efficient finance can still produce unjust distribution, and fair distribution can still fund an incapable system.

A useful middle-out question is therefore:

> **What future capability is the financing making possible; who actually supplies capital, bears downside, receives upside and holds control; which protections correspond to which risks; which consequences remain outside the financial contract; how do those relationships change over time; and who can answer back when the bargain stops serving its stated purpose?**

That is a question, not a decision rule.

## What not to promote from this page

Do **not** infer:

- `CAPITAL_ALLOCATION` is a TRACE primitive;
- private capital is generally illegitimate;
- public finance is generally superior;
- risk-bearing creates sovereignty;
- community ownership proves community consent;
- employee ownership is a universal answer to AI distribution;
- one-member-one-vote is universally correct;
- financial return is evidence of social value;
- public subsidy always deserves public equity;
- mission control should always override investor protection;
- investor protection should always override mission or affected-party claims;
- a project checklist can settle ownership, fiduciary, tax, securities, insolvency or public-law questions;
- this page is financial or legal advice;
- this page is evidence that TRACE or Mechanical Ethics works.

```text
OWNER_KNOWLEDGE != PROJECT_OWNERSHIP
FINANCIAL_MODEL != MORAL_CLEARANCE
RISK_BEARING != SOVEREIGNTY
PROTECT_CAPITAL != OWN_THE_PURPOSE
DESCRIPTION != PERMISSION
BUILD != PROOF
```
