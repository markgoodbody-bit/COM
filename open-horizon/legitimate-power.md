# Legitimate Power

Status: **OPEN HORIZON LEARNING ROUTE / OWNER-FIRST SYNTHESIS / NOT CANON / NOT TRACE OR ME CHANGE / NOT AN AUTHORITY CLEARANCE RULE**  
Parent map: [Open Horizon](README.md)  
Live exploration ledger: [COM #74](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/COM/issues/74)

> **HOW CAN WE MAKE A BETTER FUTURE?**

This page carries forward one part of the Open Horizon that is easy to miss when the project concentrates mainly on constraining power:

> a better future also needs institutions, relationships and systems that can legitimately acquire enough real power and capability to do useful things together.

That is not the same as saying that power is good, that competence creates legitimacy, or that a project vocabulary can certify who ought to rule.

This is a learning route into existing owner-first exploration. It is not a new project framework and does not promote `LEGITIMATE_POWER`, `AUTHORITY_TOPOLOGY`, entrustment or any other term into TRACE or Mechanical Ethics.

Detailed owner work, field cases, corrections and source links remain in [COM #74](https://github.com/markgoodbody-bit/COM/issues/74). Later supported ledger state wins if it changes this summary.

## What the project has learned so far

### Constraint is not the whole theory of power

The project has strong language for authority creep, asymmetric power, answerability, contestability and correction. Open Horizon work found the opposite failure mode too:

an institution can be carefully constrained and still be unable to do what affected people legitimately need.

```text
POWER_NEEDS_CONSTRAINT != POWER_SHOULD_NOT_EXIST
LEGITIMATE_MANDATE != IMPLEMENTATION_CAPACITY
ACCOUNTABILITY_ASSIGNED != RESOURCES / SKILLS / DATA / AUTHORITY_ALIGNED
```

State-capacity and public-administration owners already cover much of this territory. The project should learn from them rather than treating institutional competence as a locally invented concept.

A useful positive question is therefore not only:

> who may act?

but also:

> can the authorised system actually coordinate the resources, expertise, information and implementation needed at the relevant scale and clocks?

Capability without purpose and answerability can increase harmful reach. Mandate without capability can become performative government.

### Authority is not one scalar quantity

Owner-rich work across infrastructure control, incident command, mutual aid and agent authorization broke a simple `more consequence -> more central authority` model.

Different functions can legitimately live in different places:

```text
SENSE / OBSERVE
INTERPRET / PLAN
AUTHORIZE
ACTUATE
COORDINATE
ASSURE / AUDIT
REVOKE / RECOVER
```

These functions may be co-located, but should not be assumed to be.

The stronger question is:

> **For this transition, where should sensing, planning, authorization, actuation, coordination, assurance and revocation live — and which must remain independently answerable to another?**

```text
WHO_SHOULD_ACT? IS OFTEN UNDER_SPECIFIED
COORDINATION_AUTHORITY != INCIDENT_COMMAND
MODEL_CAPABILITY != AUTHORIZATION
TASK_PLAN != AUTHORITY_GRANT
AUTHORITY_GRANTED != AUTHORITY_STILL_VALID
```

Higher consequence can require stronger boundaries, verification and correction without implying that every function moves upward to one sovereign centre.

Sometimes fast local actuation matters. Sometimes cross-boundary coordination belongs higher. Sometimes local refusal or resource retention protects risks the centre cannot see. Sometimes authorization should remain outside the acting intelligence entirely.

### Participation and mandate can be layered rather than collapsed

Deliberative-democracy owners show that legitimate collective decision-making need not compress every form of participation into sovereignty.

Institutionalised deliberation can give affected citizens real routes to raise issues, examine evidence and trade-offs, deliberate with elected representatives and receive formal follow-up while legal decision authority remains where constitutional arrangements place it.

Useful distinctions from the Horizon:

```text
PARTICIPATION != MANDATE
RANDOM_SELECTION != SOVEREIGNTY
REPRESENTATIVE_DELIBERATION != REFERENDUM
RECOMMENDATION != FORMAL_DECISION
DUTY_TO_RESPOND != DUTY_TO_IMPLEMENT
ELECTED_AUTHORITY != ONLY_SOURCE_OF_LEGITIMATE_EPISTEMIC_INPUT
VOICE_ROUTE_EXISTS != CONSEQUENCE_FOLLOWS
```

The positive lesson is **layered legitimacy**: sensing lived experience, learning evidence, deliberating, recommending, authorising, implementing and reviewing can be legitimate functions without belonging to the same participant or institution.

This does not establish a universally correct democratic design. Deliberative institutions can still exclude, stall, be ignored or reproduce power imbalances.

### Power can be held for a purpose without being owned by the power-holder

Fiduciary and trustee traditions provide a strong existing language for delegated power.

A trustee can exercise substantial control over assets without those assets becoming theirs to use for personal purposes. The discretion is constrained by purpose, governing rules, prudence, conflicts duties, beneficiary interests and reviewable decision standards.

The useful portable distinctions are:

```text
CONTROL != OWNERSHIP
DECISION_POWER != PERSONAL_BENEFIT_ENTITLEMENT
DELEGATED_POWER != UNBOUNDED_DISCRETION
IMPARTIALITY != IDENTICAL_TREATMENT
```

This is stronger than simply saying `power must be accountable`. It shows an institutional form in which real discretion can exist while the power-holder remains answerable to a purpose and to others whose interests the role exists to serve.

Do not universalise fiduciary law into every domain. The legal owners remain the authority for actual trustee and fiduciary duties.

### Useful delegation can generate legitimate practical power

Some shared projects require specialised expertise, infrastructure, coordination or execution capacity that every affected participant cannot and need not personally exercise.

Delegation can therefore be constructive rather than merely a loss of autonomy.

```text
DELEGATION != SURRENDER
SPECIALISATION != SOVEREIGNTY
COMMUNITY_OWNERSHIP != COMMUNITY_CONTROL
OFFER_TO_PARTICIPATE != CAPACITY_TO_PARTICIPATE
```

The Horizon's community-energy work exposed a useful question:

> the important issue is not simply whether delegation occurred, but whether the relationship continues to earn the delegation.

That can depend on purpose, competence, transparency, conflicts, affected-party routes, ability to challenge, performance, correction and whether the delegated scope still matches the problem.

There is no project formula that turns those factors into legitimate authority.

### Entrustment can grow, narrow and recover

Medical education, professional revalidation, aviation currency and security authorization all resist the idea that authority is a permanent property of identity.

Existing owner systems distinguish evidence of competence from the decision to entrust a specific activity, and often preserve routes for supervision, remediation, renewal or renewed evidence.

```text
TRUSTWORTHINESS_EVIDENCE != ENTRUSTMENT_DECISION
ENTRUSTMENT_FOR_ACTIVITY_A != ENTRUSTMENT_FOR_ACTIVITY_B
CERTIFIED_ONCE != CURRENTLY_ENTRUSTABLE_FOR_EVERY_OPERATION
LOST_CURRENCY != PERMANENT_DISQUALIFICATION
KNOWN_IDENTITY != PERMANENT_TRUST
ENTRUSTMENT != STANDING_PRIVILEGE
```

This matters for growing human and artificial participants because it creates a third path between permanent subordination and unbounded autonomy.

But serious hostile questions remain:

- who owns the entrustment criteria?
- whose risk counts?
- can the participant inspect and answer the evidence?
- can success be gamed?
- does authority decay fairly?
- is there a route back after failure?

```text
COMPETENCE != SOVEREIGNTY
ENTRUSTMENT != PERSONHOOD
DELEGATED_AUTHORITY != MORAL_STANDING
SUPERVISOR_APPROVAL != AFFECTED_PARTY_CONSENT
```

The project should not use operational entrustment as a back door for settling personhood, rights or political standing.

### Local responsibility and wider coordination can coexist

Mutual-aid and emergency-response owners show why `centralise or decentralise` is often the wrong question.

A local service can retain responsibility for the incident while national structures coordinate scarce specialist resources, situational awareness and mutual aid. Donor areas can also retain legitimate reasons not to release assets when doing so would create unacceptable local risk.

```text
NATIONAL_NEED != AUTOMATIC_LOCAL_DISPOSSESSION
LOCAL_RESPONSIBILITY != LOCAL_SELF_SUFFICIENCY
MUTUAL_AID != UNCONDITIONAL_RESOURCE_TRANSFER
```

This exposes several legitimate obligations at once rather than pretending one scale sees the whole system.

## The central tension

The constructive complement currently visible is:

```text
CONSTRAIN POWER SO IT REMAINS ANSWERABLE
+
BUILD / DELEGATE ENOUGH CAPABILITY TO DO WHAT MATTERS
```

Neither half is sufficient alone.

- constrained but incapable institutions can abandon affected people;
- capable but unanswerable institutions can dominate them;
- distributed participation without a route to consequential decision can become theatre;
- central coordination without local knowledge can dispossess or mis-handle the system;
- permanent fragmentation can make timely action impossible;
- entrustment without challenge can become paternal gatekeeping;
- revocation without rehabilitation can harden one failure into permanent exclusion.

A useful middle-out question is therefore:

> **What real work must be done, which authority functions does it require, who can legitimately hold each function, what capability makes that authority usable, who is affected by its exercise, who can answer or challenge it, and how can the allocation change when evidence, risk or capability changes?**

That is a question, not a decision rule.

## What not to promote from this page

Do **not** infer:

- `LEGITIMATE_POWER` is a TRACE primitive;
- `AUTHORITY_TOPOLOGY` is an accepted project term or universal architecture;
- functional separation is always safer;
- centralisation or decentralisation is generally superior;
- deliberative processes automatically create legitimate mandates;
- community ownership proves community control or consent;
- professional entrustment models settle AI standing or rights;
- competence creates sovereignty;
- fiduciary language travels unchanged outside its legal/institutional domains;
- a mandate is adequate merely because it is procedurally valid;
- capability excuses missing answerability;
- the project can certify legitimate authority through a checklist;
- this page is evidence that TRACE or Mechanical Ethics works.

```text
OWNER_KNOWLEDGE != PROJECT_OWNERSHIP
USEFUL_INTEGRATION != NEW_PRIMITIVE
COMPETENCE != LEGITIMACY
DESCRIPTION != AUTHORITY_GRANT
BUILD != PROOF
```
