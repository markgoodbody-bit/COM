# Worked Scarce Allocation — Four Generators

Status: **CONSTRUCTED SCENE / OWNER-FIRST LEARNING AID / NOT EMERGENCY, ENERGY, HEALTH OR FINANCIAL ADVICE / NOT A UNIVERSAL ALLOCATION RULE / NOT EVIDENCE / NOT A TEST**

> **HOW CAN WE MAKE A BETTER FUTURE?**

This scene carries forward one Open Horizon result that becomes consequential only when a shared capability is insufficient for all valid claims:

> **scarcity makes the allocation rule itself consequential power.**

The scene is fictional. It does not propose an electricity code, emergency doctrine, triage rule or distributive theory.

Mature owners already use different allocation constitutions for different purposes. Useful neighbouring examples include:

- the UK [Electricity Supply Emergency Code](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/electricity-supply-emergency-code/electricity-supply-emergency-code), which combines equitable sharing among non-protected users with pre-designated Protected Sites and ongoing review;
- WHO's [2025 national allocation guidance for pandemic medical countermeasures](https://www.who.int/publications/i/item/9789240117884), which combines equity, reciprocity, efficiency, procedural fairness, evidence, governance, monitoring and contingency planning;
- FEMA/NIMS resource coordination, where simultaneous incidents competing for limited critical resources create a distinct prioritisation and allocation problem.

These owners do not imply one common priority rule. They demonstrate the opposite: allocation logic depends on the purpose of the resource, relevant standing, evidence, law, clocks and institutional context.

```text
SCARCITY != LICENSE_FOR_IMPROVISED_POWER
FIRST_COME != NEUTRAL
PRICE != NEED
PRIOR_CONTRACT != UNIVERSAL_PRIORITY
TECHNICAL_NECESSITY != JUST_DISTRIBUTION
```

---

## Scene

A fictional region operates a shared resilience pool of **four large mobile generators**.

They are maintained jointly by several public bodies and infrastructure operators because no participant can economically keep enough spare generation for every rare contingency.

Normal-time agreements include:

- annual contributions to maintenance;
- several commercial reservation contracts for planned outages;
- a list of sites considered especially consequential during emergencies;
- a coordination centre that can dispatch the units;
- an emergency clause allowing normal reservations to be reconsidered during a formally declared regional emergency.

After a severe storm, seven requests arrive within ninety minutes.

### Claim A — community hospital

Its installed backup generators are running, but a fuel-delivery route is blocked. Current fuel is estimated to last eight hours. Several clinical services depend on electricity, but the hospital is not yet without power.

### Claim B — drinking-water pumping station

Grid power is lost. Stored water gives the network approximately twelve hours before pressure begins to fall materially in two districts. The pumping station can run on one mobile generator.

### Claim C — residential care home

The building has forty residents, including people dependent on powered medical and mobility equipment. Its small backup supply has failed. Temporary relocation is possible but would take several hours and carries its own risks.

### Claim D — telecom exchange

Battery reserve is estimated at four hours. Loss would reduce communications coverage used by residents and some emergency responders, though alternate networks cover part of the area.

### Claim E — cold-storage warehouse

A food distributor has a normal-time reservation contract for one of the mobile generators during a planned maintenance period beginning today. Without power, a large refrigerated stock may be lost. The company has already paid for the reservation.

### Claim F — chemical plant

The plant is shut down, but one cooling/circulation system must continue for approximately ten hours to avoid material equipment damage and a possible later hazardous condition. The site was historically classified as a protected industrial dependency, but its process changed six months ago and the classification has not been reviewed since.

### Claim G — dialysis clinic

A new clinic opened four months ago and is **not** on the old protected-site list. It has patients booked through the day, limited battery support for equipment shutdown, and arrangements to transfer some patients elsewhere. Transfer capacity is uncertain.

There are four generators and seven plausible claims.

No amount of better inventory changes that fact.

```text
CAPACITY_EXISTS
AND
CAPACITY_IS_INSUFFICIENT
```

The system has crossed from a capability problem into an allocation problem.

---

## 1. A request is not an entitlement

Each claimant has a real reason to ask.

The coordination centre therefore does **not** begin by classifying some requests as selfish and others as morally pure.

It records:

```text
REQUEST
-> CLAIM BASIS
-> CURRENT EVIDENCE
-> CLOCK
-> ALTERNATIVES / SUBSTITUTES
-> CONSEQUENCE IF UNSERVED
```

A valid request still does not establish priority over every other valid request.

```text
RESOURCE_REQUEST != RESOURCE_ENTITLEMENT
VALID_NEED != HIGHEST_PRIORITY_BY_DEFINITION
```

Scarcity requires a rule for comparing claims that were never comparable while enough capacity existed.

---

## 2. First-come would convert network position into priority

The warehouse submitted its request first because its planned reservation was already in the dispatch system.

The care home called twenty-three minutes later.

A literal queue would therefore serve the warehouse before the care home.

That is procedurally simple, but it is not neutral.

It rewards:

- prior system access;
- faster information;
- staffing able to submit early;
- an existing contractual relationship;
- proximity to the coordination mechanism.

```text
EARLIER_REQUEST != GREATER_CONSEQUENCE
FIRST_COME != ABSENCE_OF_VALUE_JUDGEMENT
```

A first-come rule may still be appropriate in some contexts. The scene only refuses to disguise it as value-free.

---

## 3. Prior reservation creates a real claim, but not necessarily the controlling claim

The warehouse has paid for reserved capacity.

That contract matters.

Ignoring it casually would undermine the normal-time system that funds and coordinates the shared pool.

But the emergency clause explicitly anticipated a state in which reservation may be pre-empted.

So the relevant distinction is:

```text
CONTRACTUAL_CLAIM = REAL
BUT
CONTRACTUAL_CLAIM != UNIVERSAL_EMERGENCY_PRIORITY
```

If the warehouse is displaced, the decision record must preserve what follows:

- compensation or contractual remedy;
- food loss and downstream supply consequences;
- whether an alternate generator can be sourced commercially;
- whether future participants will still trust the reservation system.

Pre-emption is not moral cleansing. It transfers burden.

---

## 4. Pre-designated protection is useful precisely because scarcity is too late for first design

The region maintains a protected-site list so that every emergency does not begin from zero.

This creates speed.

It also creates risk.

The chemical plant is on the list because of its older process.

The dialysis clinic is absent because it did not exist when the list was last reviewed.

```text
PREDECLARED_PRIORITY -> FAST_ACTION_CAPABILITY
AND
STALE_PREDECLARED_PRIORITY -> MISALLOCATION_RISK
```

The coordinator therefore treats protected status as strong prior evidence, not as an untouchable badge.

Currentness is checked.

The chemical plant must explain what hazardous consequence still exists under its new process and how soon it becomes material.

The dialysis clinic's absence from the list is not read as evidence that its patients have lower standing.

```text
NOT_ON_LIST != NOT_IMPORTANT
ON_LIST != CURRENTLY_QUALIFIED_BY_MAGIC
```

---

## 5. The allocation objective must be named

The shared pool cannot allocate coherently if each decision-maker silently uses a different objective.

The fictional emergency plan therefore states a bounded objective hierarchy for this pool:

1. prevent imminent serious loss of life or large irreversible harm where the generator materially changes that risk;
2. preserve infrastructure whose failure would rapidly create wider life-safety or essential-service loss;
3. preserve time-sensitive capacity needed for continuing response;
4. where claims are materially similar, distribute burden rather than privilege access by money, timing or institutional status alone;
5. preserve contractual and economic losses as real residue even when they do not control emergency priority.

This is a constructed rule for the scene, **not** a recommendation for real jurisdictions.

Its purpose is to make the value choice visible.

```text
ALLOCATION_OBJECTIVE_DECLARED
!=
ALLOCATION_OBJECTIVE_PROVEN_CORRECT
```

Different owner systems could legitimately use different criteria.

---

## 6. Expected benefit matters only if the resource actually changes the outcome

The hospital is medically consequential, but it still has eight hours of fuel.

The care home has already lost its backup.

The water station has twelve hours of stored-water margin.

The telecom exchange has four hours of battery reserve.

These clocks matter because a generator allocated **now** may produce very different marginal benefit.

```text
HIGH_SIGNIFICANCE_SITE != HIGHEST_MARGINAL_BENEFIT_AT_THIS_MINUTE
```

The hospital may need a generator urgently later without needing one first.

The allocation plan can therefore sequence rather than simply rank:

```text
WHO NEEDS CAPACITY NOW?
+
WHO MUST HAVE CAPACITY RESERVED FOR A KNOWN LATER CLOCK?
```

Scarcity management is temporal as well as distributive.

---

## 7. One generator can sometimes protect more than one claimant through sequencing

The water pumping station needs continuous generation for a four-hour refill cycle, after which stored-water margin materially improves.

The hospital needs a unit only if replacement fuel cannot arrive before its own threshold.

The system therefore considers a staged allocation rather than treating each generator as permanently attached to the first claimant.

For example:

```text
GENERATOR 1 -> CARE HOME NOW
GENERATOR 2 -> TELECOM EXCHANGE NOW
GENERATOR 3 -> WATER PUMPING FOR BOUNDED REFILL CYCLE
GENERATOR 4 -> CHEMICAL / DIALYSIS / HOSPITAL DECISION DEPENDING ON VERIFIED CLOCKS

WATER CYCLE COMPLETES
-> GENERATOR 3 CAN MOVE IF SAFE / PRACTICABLE
```

The exact assignments are illustrative, not operational advice.

The portable point is:

```text
SCARCE_UNIT != PERMANENT_ONE-CLAIMANT_OWNERSHIP
SEQUENCING_CAN_EXPAND_EFFECTIVE_CAPACITY
```

But moving equipment also consumes transport time and creates failure risk. Reallocation must therefore be physically real, not spreadsheet optimism.

---

## 8. Alternatives change priority without making the underlying need unreal

The dialysis clinic can transfer some patients.

The care home can theoretically evacuate.

The warehouse may rent commercial generation.

The telecom exchange has partial coverage from another network.

The hospital may receive fuel by a different route.

These alternatives matter.

But an alternative is not binary.

The system asks:

- how much capacity does the alternative actually provide?
- on what clock?
- with what burden and risk?
- who carries that burden?
- is it confirmed or merely plausible?

```text
ALTERNATIVE_EXISTS != ALTERNATIVE_IS_USABLE
TRANSFER_POSSIBLE != TRANSFER_COST_ZERO
```

A claimant is not erased because another route exists.

---

## 9. The losing claim remains in the account

Suppose the warehouse loses its reservation and a significant quantity of food is spoiled.

That loss does not become ethically clean merely because a life-safety claim prevailed.

The account keeps:

- the displaced contract;
- the value destroyed;
- workers or customers affected;
- possible compensation;
- evidence for whether the pool should be larger next time;
- whether cold-storage resilience should be improved outside the shared pool.

```text
LOWER_PRIORITY != ZERO_HARM
PRIORITY_DECISION != REPAIR_OF_LOSER
```

This is especially important because repeated allocation against the same claimant can create systematic burden even when each individual decision is defensible.

---

## 10. Equal sharing can be fair in one layer and disastrous in another

One proposal is to give every claimant the same number of generator-hours.

That treats claims symmetrically.

But if a care home's critical equipment fails after one hour while a warehouse loses only part of its stock after six, identical shares may not produce equal protection.

Conversely, always allocating to whichever claimant can demonstrate the largest quantified loss can privilege actors best able to monetise consequence.

```text
EQUAL_RESOURCE_SHARE != EQUAL_PROTECTION
MONETISABLE_LOSS != TOTAL_SIGNIFICANCE
```

Equality remains relevant. The question is equality of what, among whom, under which constraints.

---

## 11. A paid market can allocate efficiently without settling public emergency priority

A commercial market offers a fifth generator at a very high emergency price.

The warehouse can afford it immediately.

The care home cannot.

If the warehouse buys it, that may improve the whole allocation picture by removing one claim from the shared pool.

But the market outcome does not establish that the warehouse deserved the shared generator more.

```text
ABILITY_TO_PAY_CAN_CREATE_ADDITIONAL_CAPACITY
AND
ABILITY_TO_PAY != GENERAL_STANDING_RULE
```

Markets can be part of the capability architecture without becoming the moral constitution for every scarce shared resource.

---

## 12. The rule itself needs authority and challenge

The dispatcher should not invent priority ad hoc because they happen to control the keys.

The scene separates:

```text
RULE-MAKING AUTHORITY
RULE-APPLICATION AUTHORITY
CLAIM EVIDENCE
PHYSICAL DISPATCH
REVIEW / REVISION
```

During the acute period, dispatch may need to move quickly.

But material disputes can still be recorded:

- the chemical plant challenges the interpretation of its current process risk;
- the dialysis clinic challenges the stale protected-site list;
- the warehouse invokes its contractual remedy;
- the care home disputes an assumption that evacuation is an adequate substitute.

```text
FAST_DECISION != UNRECORDED_DECISION
CONTEST_ROUTE != AUTOMATIC_STAY
```

Not every challenge can halt urgent allocation. It can still create a correction route and later accountability.

---

## 13. Priority classifications must have a maintenance clock

The stale chemical-plant classification and missing dialysis clinic expose a governance defect before the storm ever happened.

A resilient allocation system therefore needs ordinary-time work:

- periodic eligibility review;
- route for new sites to apply;
- evidence updates;
- removal when a condition no longer applies;
- change history;
- emergency override rules with explicit reasons;
- monitoring of who repeatedly receives or loses capacity.

```text
PRIORITY_LIST != TIMELESS_TRUTH
PRECOMMITMENT_WITHOUT_REVISION -> RIGIDITY
NO_PRECOMMITMENT -> PANIC / CAPTURE / DELAY
```

The point of precommitment is to buy decision speed without freezing an obsolete world model.

---

## 14. Scarcity can reveal that the pool itself is too small

After the event, the region should not limit review to whether dispatchers followed the priority rule.

It also asks:

- were four generators enough for foreseeable correlated demand?
- did contractual reservations consume too much shared reserve?
- which claimants should hold their own minimum resilience rather than relying on mutual aid?
- can more capacity be pooled economically?
- would geographically distributed smaller units reduce transport delay?
- did the emergency rule repeatedly externalise burden onto the same organisations?

```text
GOOD_ALLOCATION_OF_TOO_LITTLE_CAPACITY
!=
ADEQUATE_SYSTEM_DESIGN
```

Allocation governance cannot substitute for capacity construction.

Capacity construction cannot substitute for allocation governance.

---

## 15. What has actually been constructed in the scene

The object is not a priority score.

It is an **answerable allocation constitution**:

```text
RESOURCE PURPOSE
+ CLAIMANT / STANDING ROUTES
+ PREDECLARED PRIORITY PRINCIPLES
+ CURRENT EVIDENCE
+ CLOCKS / MARGINAL BENEFIT
+ ALTERNATIVES
+ RESERVATION / PRE-EMPTION RULES
+ DECISION AUTHORITY
+ PHYSICAL DISPATCH
+ CONTEST / REVIEW
+ LOSER BURDEN / RESIDUE
+ PRIORITY-LIST MAINTENANCE
+ CAPACITY-ADEQUACY REVIEW
```

No single ordering formula follows.

The cross-domain lesson is smaller:

> **when not every valid claim can be served, somebody or some mechanism is deciding which correction windows remain open. That allocation power should be visible, pre-thought where possible, current enough to respond to the real event, and answerable for the burdens it creates.**

---

## What this scene does not establish

It does not establish:

- how any real generator pool, grid, hospital system or emergency service should allocate resources;
- that life safety is always the only legitimate priority;
- that prior contracts should always be overridden or always honored;
- that protected-site lists are superior to case-by-case judgement;
- that first-come allocation is never appropriate;
- that markets are illegitimate under scarcity;
- that equal sharing is generally fair or unfair;
- that one mathematical score can compare heterogeneous harms;
- that every loser is owed compensation;
- that a challenge must suspend an urgent allocation decision;
- that the project can determine moral standing through an allocation checklist;
- that TRACE or Mechanical Ethics has been validated.

```text
ALLOCATION_RULE != MORAL_ORACLE
PRIORITY != STANDING
PRICE != NEED
CONTRACT != SOVEREIGNTY
LOWER_PRIORITY != ZERO_HARM
BUILD != PROOF
```

## Owner boundary

Emergency resource management, electricity-system operation, healthcare allocation ethics, public law, contract, market design, infrastructure resilience and domain-specific operators remain primary owners of real allocation decisions.

This project keeps only the cross-domain relation:

```text
SHARED CAPACITY
-> CORRELATED DEMAND
-> VALID CLAIMS > AVAILABLE CAPACITY
-> ALLOCATION RULE BECOMES CONSEQUENT POWER
-> DECISION + RESIDUE
-> REVIEW BOTH RULE AND CAPACITY
```
