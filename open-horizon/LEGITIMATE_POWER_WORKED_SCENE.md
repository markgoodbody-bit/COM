# Worked Legitimate Power — The Smoke Line

Status: **CONSTRUCTED MULTI-AGENCY SCENE / OWNER-FIRST LEARNING AID / NOT EMERGENCY DOCTRINE / NOT LEGAL OR OPERATIONAL ADVICE / NOT EVIDENCE / NOT A TEST**

> **HOW CAN WE MAKE A BETTER FUTURE?**

This scene exists because the [Legitimate Power](legitimate-power.md) route is already strong on distinctions but comparatively abstract about how several legitimate forms of authority can operate together under time pressure.

It does **not** propose an emergency-management framework.

Mature emergency-response owners already carry the operational doctrine. Useful current routes include:

- [JESIP principles for joint working](https://www.jesip.org.uk/joint-doctrine/principles-for-joint-working/) — co-location, communication, co-ordination, joint understanding of risk and shared situational awareness;
- [UK Cabinet Office — Emergency response and recovery](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/emergency-response-and-recovery) — subsidiarity, integration, continuity and the distinction between single-agency command/control and multi-agency coordination;
- [US NIMS resource management](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/a-z/nims/managing-resources.html) — pre-planned resource management, mutual aid, tracking, demobilisation and replenishment.

The roles below are therefore deliberately fictionalised. They are not a statement of the precise legal powers of any real UK responder.

The scene asks a narrower cross-domain question:

> **How can real authority become temporarily faster and more concentrated where necessary without turning coordination, competence or emergency into sovereignty?**

---

## Scene

At 14:12 on a hot weekday, a fire starts at an industrial storage site beside a river and a major road.

Within twenty minutes:

- fire crews report several burning chemical containers;
- smoke begins moving toward two residential districts;
- a hospital three kilometres away is assessing whether outside-air intakes may be affected;
- runoff threatens a watercourse;
- one access road used by emergency vehicles is becoming congested;
- several schools are still open;
- the incident lies close to the boundary between two local response areas.

No single organisation owns the whole problem.

A constructed response ecology contains:

- **Fire & Rescue Service A**, holding incident-scene firefighting command within its remit;
- **Fire & Rescue Service B**, a neighbouring service with specialist hazardous-material resources and its own local cover obligations;
- **Police**, with its own powers and operational responsibilities for traffic, cordons and public order;
- **Ambulance / health responders**, responsible for casualty and health-system response within their remit;
- **Local Authority North** and **Local Authority South**, responsible for several local support/recovery functions;
- **environmental specialists**, supplying pollution and runoff evidence;
- **the hospital**, operating its own clinical and estate-continuity decisions;
- a **multi-agency tactical coordination group**, created to align the response;
- a later **strategic coordination group** if the incident persists or widens.

The scene is constructed so that no one actor can simply issue commands to every other actor.

```text
MULTIPLE AUTHORITIES != NO AUTHORITY
COORDINATION != SOVEREIGN COMMAND
SPECIALIST CAPABILITY != GENERAL MANDATE
```

---

## 1. Ordinary authority is already plural

At 14:20, Fire A does not need a cross-agency vote before moving crews away from an immediately dangerous tank.

Police do not need Fire A to become the legal owner of every road-management decision.

The hospital does not surrender clinical decision-making because the fire service knows more about the plume source.

Each organisation has existing responsibilities, competence, procedures and authority.

The joint problem begins because those separate legitimate actions now interact.

For example:

- closing one road can obstruct ambulance routing;
- moving a fire appliance can change the safety of an evacuation corridor;
- a hospital decision to seal an intake can affect internal temperature and clinical operations;
- a local-authority shelter plan may depend on whether the plume moves north or south;
- calling every neighbouring specialist unit may leave another area dangerously uncovered.

So:

```text
VALID_SINGLE_AGENCY_ACTION
CAN STILL
CREATE_MULTI_AGENCY_CONSEQUENCE
```

The purpose of coordination is not to erase agency authority. It is to make interdependence governable.

---

## 2. The authority functions are deliberately not one thing

The response separates several functions that are often compressed into the question `who is in charge?`

### Sense / observe

Evidence arrives from:

- crews at the site;
- weather and plume modelling;
- environmental monitoring;
- police traffic observations;
- hospital engineering/clinical reports;
- public emergency calls;
- neighbouring services' resource status.

### Interpret / plan

Each agency interprets evidence through its own expertise. The tactical coordination group combines those views into a joint operating picture and identifies cross-agency conflicts.

### Authorize

Authority for a particular act remains with the actor or institution that legitimately holds it unless a valid exceptional arrangement says otherwise.

### Actuate

Different organisations execute their own actions: firefighting, traffic control, hospital measures, shelter preparation, environmental containment and so on.

### Coordinate

The tactical group aligns priorities, sequencing and shared resource needs.

### Assure / audit

Decision logs, command records, later environmental and health evidence, internal review and external legal/accountability routes can examine what happened.

### Revoke / recover / hand back

Temporary arrangements can end, narrowed powers can return to ordinary structures, mutual-aid resources can be demobilised and recovery governance can replace response governance.

```text
SENSE != AUTHORIZE
PLAN != GRANT
AUTHORIZE != ACTUATE
COORDINATE != OWN
AUDIT != COMMAND
```

This is not a claim that these functions must always be separated. It is a refusal to assume they are naturally one authority.

## 3. The first emergency switch is earned by a concrete coordination failure

At 14:34, two things happen together:

1. smoke modelling indicates the plume may cross the second local boundary within forty minutes;
2. the hospital reports that a change in wind direction would require a decision on air-handling measures within roughly twenty minutes.

The ordinary bilateral calls are now too slow and fragmented.

A pre-planned **fast coordination regime** is activated.

In the scene, activation requires a named trigger condition and a recorded declaration:

```text
TRIGGER:
CROSS_BOUNDARY_HAZARD
+
MULTI_AGENCY_DECISION_CLOCK < ORDINARY_COORDINATION_TIME
```

The trigger does not prove that every proposed emergency action is justified.

It establishes only that the **coordination mode** needs to change.

```text
EMERGENCY_EXISTS != UNBOUNDED_POWER_EXISTS
TRIGGER_VALID != EVERY_ACTION_VALID
```

---

## 4. Fast coordination changes tempo, not ownership of every function

The tactical group now meets continuously rather than on a normal periodic rhythm.

Its bounded fast-regime purpose is to:

- maintain one current multi-agency situation picture;
- agree a small number of shared objectives;
- surface resource conflicts immediately;
- sequence actions whose effects cross organisational boundaries;
- identify decisions that require escalation;
- keep agency limits visible.

For the next hour the shared objectives are:

1. prevent responder/public exposure to the highest-confidence hazard zone;
2. prevent the hospital from losing safe operating conditions;
3. maintain emergency access;
4. contain runoff where safely practicable;
5. establish whether wider shelter/evacuation measures are needed before the plume clock closes.

These objectives can align agencies without making the tactical chair sovereign over them.

```text
SHARED_OBJECTIVE != SHARED_LEGAL_PERSON
FAST_COORDINATION != COMMAND_OF_ALL_COMMANDERS
```

---

## 5. A lead role can exist without swallowing neighbouring authority

Fire A is agreed as the **lead operational organisation for the immediate hazard source** because it holds the relevant scene expertise and current incident command.

That lead matters.

It lets other actors know where the best source-hazard picture sits and avoids a vacuum where nobody integrates the immediate hazard.

But the role has explicit limits.

It does not automatically give Fire A authority to:

- decide hospital clinical priorities;
- compel neighbouring Fire B to release resources below its own safe minimum cover;
- set local-authority recovery policy;
- determine every police action;
- permanently define the acceptable public-risk threshold after the fast regime ends.

```text
LEAD_ORGANISATION != UNIVERSAL_COMMAND
EXPERTISE_CENTRE != SOVEREIGNTY
```

The scene therefore keeps `lead` typed to a problem/function rather than to the whole world.

---

## 6. Mutual aid is a negotiated capability, not dispossession

Fire A requests Fire B's specialist hazardous-material unit.

Fire B has two such capabilities available on paper.

One is already covering an unrelated industrial district where another high-risk site is operating.

Fire B therefore offers:

- one specialist team now;
- an additional equipment cache;
- a second team only if local standby cover can be restored from a third service.

The tactical group cannot honestly record this as `Fire B refuses to cooperate`.

It is a real system constraint.

```text
NATIONAL_OR_REGIONAL_NEED != AUTOMATIC_LOCAL_DISPOSSESSION
RESOURCE_EXISTS != RESOURCE_CAN_BE_RELEASED_WITHOUT_NEW_RISK
MUTUAL_AID != UNCONDITIONAL_TRANSFER
```

A better coordination system makes the donor's retained risk visible instead of treating central demand as morally cleansing.

---

## 7. The exceptional regime needs a scope envelope

The fast regime records what it is for and what it is not for.

Constructed envelope:

```text
PURPOSE:
coordinate immediate cross-boundary life-safety / continuity actions

START:
14:34

REASSESS:
every 30 minutes or on material hazard change

ENDS WHEN:
ordinary coordination can meet the active decision clocks
OR strategic arrangements supersede it

DOES NOT CREATE:
new statutory powers
permanent resource claims
blanket override of agency safety limits
permanent public-policy authority
```

The time field matters because exceptional coordination can otherwise persist after the condition that justified it has disappeared.

```text
EXCEPTIONAL_MODE_WITHOUT_EXIT
->
AUTHORITY_RATCHET_RISK
```

---

## 8. The switch itself must be answerable

At 14:51, new monitoring suggests the plume may be less toxic than the initial worst-case estimate.

That is not a reason to pretend the earlier trigger was fraudulent.

Nor is it a reason to keep the fast regime unchanged simply because activating it was once reasonable.

The group asks:

- what evidence triggered the mode?
- what has changed?
- which active actions still depend on the original assumption?
- are any actions now disproportionate?
- do the current clocks still justify the exceptional tempo?

```text
EARLIER_TRIGGER_JUSTIFIED
DOES_NOT_IMPLY
CURRENT_TRIGGER_STILL_SATISFIED
```

The emergency switch therefore has its own correction route.

---

## 9. Urgency does not erase dissent

The hospital disagrees with a proposed broad shelter instruction because its clinical advisers believe the wording could cause vulnerable patients to self-evacuate unnecessarily.

The tactical group does not resolve this by declaring the hospital `non-cooperative`.

Instead it distinguishes:

```text
SHARED_HAZARD_OBJECTIVE
!=
IDENTICAL_PREFERRED_ACTION
```

The disagreement is recorded and the public message is revised so the hospital can preserve its internal clinical instruction while the wider community receives the agreed protective action.

Dissent can therefore improve the joint act without giving every participant an individual veto over all system action.

```text
DISSENT != VETO_BY_DEFAULT
COORDINATION != FORCED_CONVERGENCE
```

---

## 10. A fast regime can still escalate a decision it should not own

At 15:06, the plume forecast worsens again.

A potential evacuation of one neighbourhood would create substantial transport, care-home, school and hospital consequences.

The tactical group has enough evidence to say:

> this decision clock is now real.

It does **not** infer:

> therefore this group automatically owns every authority needed to order and implement the whole evacuation.

Instead it routes the decision through the relevant established authorities while continuing preparatory work that is already within scope.

```text
ABILITY_TO_IDENTIFY_DECISION
!=
AUTHORITY_TO_MAKE_EVERY_PART_OF_IT
```

This preserves speed without making urgency a permission generator.

---

## 11. Strategic coordination adds scale without replacing the incident

The event persists into the evening and begins affecting transport, schools, healthcare continuity and environmental recovery across both local areas.

A strategic coordination layer is established.

Its role is different from direct scene command.

It can address questions such as:

- cross-area strategic priorities;
- sustained resource pressure;
- public communication strategy;
- longer-term health/environment monitoring;
- transition from response to recovery;
- unresolved conflicts that exceed the tactical layer's mandate.

But the strategic layer should not pretend that greater hierarchy automatically produces better local perception.

```text
HIGHER_SCALE != BETTER_VIEW_OF_EVERY_LOCAL_FACT
LOCAL_KNOWLEDGE != LOCAL_SELF_SUFFICIENCY
```

The useful relation is layered rather than winner-takes-all.

---

## 12. Recovery should not inherit emergency authority silently

By the next morning the fire is contained.

The most acute plume risk has passed, but the response leaves:

- displaced residents;
- contaminated runoff questions;
- closed roads;
- business disruption;
- possible health follow-up;
- school closures;
- equipment and staffing debt across several agencies.

The fast response regime is **not** simply relabelled `recovery` and kept alive indefinitely.

A transition record says which emergency arrangements end, which obligations persist and which recovery structures now take custody.

For example:

```text
FAST_TACTICAL_CELL -> CLOSED
FIRE_SCENE_COMMAND -> NARROWED / CONTINUES WHERE NEEDED
MUTUAL_AID_TEAMS -> DEMOBILISED / RESTOCKED
ENVIRONMENTAL_MONITORING -> CONTINUES
HEALTH_FOLLOW_UP -> TRANSFERRED TO RELEVANT OWNER
COMMUNITY_RECOVERY -> BROADER PARTICIPATION / DIFFERENT GOVERNANCE
```

This is a hand-back, not abandonment.

```text
EMERGENCY_AUTHORITY_EXPIRED != CONSEQUENCES_ERASED
HAND_BACK != NO_SURVIVING_DUTY
```

---

## 13. Later scrutiny is not an insult to emergency judgement

Some emergency choices must be made before perfect information exists.

That makes later scrutiny more important, not less.

Useful questions after the event include:

- was the fast regime triggered on the evidence claimed?
- were agency limits correctly represented?
- did donor services retain enough local cover?
- did any actor use coordination to obtain powers outside the declared scope?
- did public communication suppress material disagreement?
- did the regime stand down when the exceptional clock ended?
- which burdens were exported to residents, staff, neighbouring areas or the environment?
- what needs repair, compensation, redesign or preserved dissent?

```text
REVIEW != CLAIM_THAT_REAL_TIME_CERTAINTY_WAS_POSSIBLE
EMERGENCY_JUDGEMENT != IMMUNITY_FROM_REVIEW
```

---

## 14. What legitimate power looks like in this scene

The scene does not produce legitimacy from one property.

Not from:

- expertise alone;
- election alone;
- urgency alone;
- hierarchy alone;
- voluntary consent alone;
- successful outcome alone.

Instead it shows a relationship among:

```text
PURPOSE
+ EXISTING AUTHORITY SOURCES
+ CAPABILITY
+ FUNCTIONAL SCOPE
+ SUBSIDIARITY
+ CROSS-BOUNDARY COORDINATION
+ VISIBLE LIMITS
+ CHALLENGE / DISSENT
+ TIME / TRIGGERS
+ ASSURANCE
+ HAND_BACK
```

No project checklist can certify the real-world legitimacy of those arrangements.

The practical lesson is narrower:

> **Real power can be made more usable without being made ownerless or unlimited when the system keeps clear which function is being entrusted, for what purpose, under which trigger, with whose capability, against which limits, and how the exceptional arrangement ends.**

---

## What this scene does not establish

It does not establish:

- the exact legal powers of any UK emergency service or public authority;
- that one particular organisation should lead a real chemical incident;
- that distributed authority is always superior to unified command;
- that temporary emergency authority is justified whenever coordination is inconvenient;
- that a declared trigger is truthful merely because it is recorded;
- that all affected people can or should participate directly in urgent operational decisions;
- that later review repairs irreversible harm;
- that mutual aid must always be given;
- that an AI system should receive emergency authority;
- that competence, speed or correct prediction creates legitimacy;
- that TRACE or Mechanical Ethics has been validated.

```text
CONSTRUCTED_SCENE != DOCTRINE
DESCRIPTION != AUTHORITY_GRANT
COMPETENCE != LEGITIMACY
EMERGENCY != MORAL_CLEARANCE
BUILD != PROOF
```

## Owner boundary

Emergency management, incident command, public law, civil-contingencies, professional command systems and domain-specific operational doctrine remain the owners of actual emergency authority.

This project keeps only the cross-domain construction:

```text
NORMAL DISTRIBUTED AUTHORITY
-> MATERIAL CLOCK / COORDINATION FAILURE
-> BOUNDED FAST REGIME
-> ACTION UNDER NAMED LIMITS
-> REASSESS / ESCALATE / NARROW
-> EXPLICIT HAND_BACK
-> SURVIVING DUTIES / REVIEW
```

and the guard:

```text
POWER_CAN_GROW_FOR_A_PURPOSE
WITHOUT
POWER_BECOMING_PROPERTY_OF_THE_POWER_HOLDER
```
