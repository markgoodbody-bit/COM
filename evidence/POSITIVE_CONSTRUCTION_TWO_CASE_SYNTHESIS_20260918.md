
## Case differences that must remain visible

### Sieve / riddle

- earlier condition: extinction classification / loss of active practice;
- positive transition: renewed making and current training;
- key coupling: reported advice/tuition and person-to-person learning;
- carrier: embodied/tacit skill plus tools/practice;
- continuation depends on living training, practice, market and future makers;
- current continuation is real but fragile;
- attention is not equivalent to transmission.

### Burnt Oak

- earlier condition: no equivalent community-linked solar installation on this council asset;
- positive transition: physical energy asset completed;
- key couplings: site authority, operator demand, community-benefit organisation, technical delivery and funding;
- carrier: physical infrastructure plus contracts/governance/maintenance;
- some benefits are expected rather than yet observed;
- future community-share participation is planned, not already realised;
- multi-party delivery is observed; joint-only necessity is **not** established.

## What the comparison adds

The comparison supports a positive-side distinction already latent in released ME/TRACE:

```text
FUTURE KEPT OPEN
!=
FUTURE CONSTRUCTED
```

A future can become materially more reachable because capabilities, practices, infrastructure, relationships or maintenance arrangements are created or restored.

But the construction claim must name the transition and enabling relations. It cannot be inferred merely because a desirable end-state exists.

```text
DESIRABLE_OUTCOME_DESCRIBED != CONSTRUCTION_PATH_ESTABLISHED
PROJECT_COMPLETED != CONTINUATION_SECURE
COOPERATION_OBSERVED != JOINT_ONLY_NECESSITY_PROVED
BENEFIT_EXPECTED != BENEFIT_REALISED
MORE_OPTIONS != BETTER
```

## Portable construction view

A bounded positive-construction reading may ask:

1. What capability, practice, relationship or material state exists later that was absent, degraded or unreachable earlier?
2. What evidence establishes the transition rather than merely the before/after labels?
3. Which actors/resources/permissions/skills were actually involved?
4. Which relations enabled the transition, and which are only inferred?
5. What maintenance or continuation work is needed for the later state to persist?
6. Which benefits are observed, which are projected, and meaningful to whom?
7. What dependencies, asymmetric control, refusal/exit or burden remain?
8. What counterfactual is **not** established—for example, that cooperation was necessary, that no alternative path existed, or that the outcome is universally good?

This view is expressible in existing state/transition/coupling/capability/burden/time/future-path structures.

## Falsification

Kill or shrink the derived view if:
- it cannot distinguish realised transition from promotional projection;
- it upgrades observed cooperation into a joint-only counterfactual;
- it treats maintenance as postscript rather than part of the constructed future;
- it equates construction with moral improvement;
- ordinary domain language carries the same cross-domain relation with materially less burden;
- a third materially different positive case cannot be represented without adding ad hoc semantics.
