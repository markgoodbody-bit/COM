## 1. Anna Atkins — *Photographs of British Algae: Cyanotype Impressions*

Authoritative Met records checked 9 September 2026:
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/286656
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/291498

The Met identifies Anna Atkins (British, 1799–1871), *Photographs of British Algae: Cyanotype Impressions*, 1843–53 / ca.1853, cyanotype, and marks the imagery Public Domain with a Download Image route. The Met describes the album as the first book photographically printed and illustrated: Atkins placed algae specimens directly on sensitized paper and exposed them to sunlight, combining systematic botanical recording with visually deliberate arrangement.

Why preserve this candidate for later attack:
- painting/textile -> early photographic/cyanotype process;
- another woman creator, but not because a demographic checkbox is needed;
- the work sits naturally at a boundary between observation, evidence-making, scientific classification, material process and visual encounter;
- it could let PSFH make room for an act of looking/recording without forcing a crisis or moral lesson onto the work.

Risks:
- easy to turn the work into a didactic illustration of `EVIDENCE` or `OBSERVATION`; reject that treatment;
- the album is a larger body, so a future candidate must choose one plate or an album-level encounter honestly rather than pretend one image is the whole work;
- project interpretation must not overwrite Atkins's scientific/historical purpose.

No asset acquisition/build authorised by this note.

## 2. Utagawa Hiroshige — *View of the Asakusa Kinryuzan Temple from the Azuma Bridge in the Rain*

Authoritative Met record checked 9 September 2026:
- https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/45321

The Met identifies Utagawa Hiroshige (Japanese, Edo/Tokyo 1797–1858), the work ca.1840, Edo period, woodblock print in ink and colour on paper, Japan, and marks it Public Domain with Download Image/Open Access availability.

Why preserve this candidate for later attack:
- historic European/American painting + African American quilt -> Japanese woodblock print;
- a narrow vertical format would demand its own presentation rather than another wide-image template;
- weather, bridge, city/temple and movement are present in the work, but PSFH must not collapse them into a ready-made lesson about uncertainty, infrastructure or transition;
- the work may earn a place simply because it creates a different human visual rhythm and way of holding place/time.

Risks:
- rain/bridge imagery is extremely easy for this project to over-symbolise;
- choosing Hiroshige because his work visually resembles project vocabulary would be a curatorial failure;
- any future use should begin with maker/work/source and museum context, then ask whether a project response is needed at all.

No asset acquisition/build authorised by this note.

## Selection discipline

After Powers/Vermeer, do not automatically promote either candidate. Ask:
1. Does this work add an encounter the current site genuinely lacks?
2. Can it stand before PSFH explains it?
3. Is provenance/custody honest and portable?
4. Does its presentation need a different form rather than cloning Homer/Powers?
5. Would deleting the project response make the page stronger? If yes, delete it.
