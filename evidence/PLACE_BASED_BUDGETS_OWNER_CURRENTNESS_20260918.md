## Why this matters to the project

A tempting project claim is that institutions often organise around files, departments or funding silos rather than the lived situation of the person crossing them.

The current Place Based Budgets programme already owns that diagnosis in explicit public-service-reform language.

The programme says responsibilities and funding can be split across organisations even when serving the same people, creating fragmented support, duplication and late intervention.

It is testing whether local partners can pool budgets, align governance and redesign services around a defined cohort/challenge and shared outcomes.

## Owner-native structure already present

The programme requires or expects pilot areas to:
- map current services, funding and decision-making;
- identify duplication, fragmentation, delayed intervention and barriers;
- agree outcomes;
- engage service users and service actors;
- redesign services around people / defined cohorts rather than departmental structures;
- establish pooled or aligned budgets;
- align governance and accountability;
- capture implementation and governance learning;
- maintain barrier registers / learning logs;
- develop local theories of change and before/after system descriptions;
- contribute comparable evidence to a shared evaluation framework.

The programme explicitly distinguishes proof of concept from mature outcome evidence. Its first evaluation phase is intended to test feasibility/operability and early signals, not definitive impact attribution.
