## 2. Shortest defensible result

Mechanical Ethics does **not** currently own any of the following general
claims:

- people should be able to contest consequential decisions;
- explanation is not enough for effective contest;
- a formal option can fail to be a real opportunity;
- bureaucratic learning, compliance and psychological costs are substantive
  burdens whose distribution can be political;
- care requires attention, responsibility, competent work and response to the
  cared-for subject;
- interim protection may be needed before final adjudication to prevent
  irreparable or inadequately compensable harm;
- irreversible authority should be constrained before execution; or
- required preconditions should not be averaged away by a favourable score.

All have substantial prior owners.

The strongest surviving Mechanical Ethics contribution hypothesis is a
human-facing integration:

> An institutional record can be corrected after the future affected by the
> record has already changed; serious correction must therefore be judged by
> whether effective protection arrived before the threatened path hardened,
> what residue remains in the life after the file is repaired, and whether the
> mechanism has changed for the next person.

That is narrower than a new moral theory. It may still be a useful framework,
diagnostic sequence, teaching object, or public argument. Its distinctiveness
is **provisional** and appears to lie in joined emphasis and voice rather than
in a new primitive.

## 3. What the neighbouring traditions displace

### 3.1 Contestability and due process

Kaminski and Urban's [*The Right to Contest AI*](https://columbialawreview.org/content/the-right-to-contest-ai/)
already argues for an individual right to contest AI decisions, modelled on
due process and adapted to private decision-making at speed and scale. It
examines effective contest design rather than treating the mere presence of
an appeal route as sufficient.

Freiesleben, Meding and König sharpen the evidential issue. They distinguish
recourse, which accepts a decision and asks what the subject can change, from
contestation, which seeks grounds to overturn a decision that may be wrong.
Their analysis shows why common XAI explanations can establish only that an
error exists somewhere, not that this person's decision should be reversed.
They identify stronger routes through predictive multiplicity, correction of
feature values and additional overruling evidence.

Their use of *normative correctness* is deliberately thin: it means
conformity to the decision-maker's stated principles, not independent moral
justification. The paper also leaves adversarial cases and the supply and
mediation of contest evidence open. Mechanical Ethics can still ask whose
norms govern, who controls the route, and what happens while contest proceeds.
It cannot claim the generic right or machinery of contest as new.

The United States Supreme Court's official report of
[*Goldberg v. Kelly*](https://www.govinfo.gov/app/details/USREPORTS-397/USREPORTS-397-254)
recognised that terminating subsistence benefits while eligibility is disputed
can deprive an eligible recipient of the means to live while waiting, and
required a pre-termination hearing in that setting. In
[*Mathews v. Eldridge*](https://www.law.cornell.edu/supremecourt/text/424/319),
the Court instead balanced the private interest, risk of error and likely
value of added safeguards against governmental fiscal and administrative
burden, and did not require the same pre-termination hearing for disability
benefits.

This pair matters because it both anticipates Mechanical Ethics and resists
its easiest generalisation. Timing and interim protection are old due-process
concerns, but protection before final review is not an unconditional rule.
Standing, protected interest, error risk, added value and competing burden
affect what process is due.

Current disposition: **the door, answer-back and pre-deprivation themes are
not novel. Mechanical Ethics may still join them to path hardening and
post-correction residue in a clearer cross-domain account.**

### 3.2 Interim relief and irreparable harm

The UN Committee on Economic, Social and Cultural Rights' decision in
[*S.C. and G.P. v. Italy*](https://docstore.ohchr.org/SelfServices/FilesHandler.ashx?enc=JHqppkkBBS8Eo4qVMJpX6CB7%2B5XinresEHfFoEqdexEjLtUNdHd%2BPPGdoNkJXQUHLeWSuaaqy5lNMp9k42rKcF4E9ydHDrt0fn3Gd3ItRDM%3D)
states the logic directly: interim measures may be needed before the merits
are determined to prevent harm that is irreversible, inadequately
compensable, or capable of defeating restoration and the effectiveness of the
later decision. It also says that certainty beyond reasonable doubt would be
incompatible with that preventive purpose, while still requiring a real risk
and a prima facie case.

This is close to Mechanical Ethics' central clock. It displaces any claim
that *protection before the final answer* or *late success may be ineffective*
is new. It also supplies legal predicates Mechanical Ethics does not yet have:
jurisdiction, admissibility, protected rights, real risk, evidence thresholds
and authority to request or lift measures.

Mechanical Ethics remains broader in subject matter and less legally bounded.
That breadth is a communication strength and a governance weakness. Without a
rule for standing, evidence and collision, a general duty to hold every
threatened path open can become impossible or harmful.

Current disposition: **the temporal intuition is established. ME's candidate
addition is its ordinary-life account of how a formally live process can
leave no comparable continuation by the time it finishes.**

### 3.3 Capability and real opportunity

The capability approach already distinguishes resources and formal rights
from the real or substantive opportunities a person can achieve. Robeyns and
Byskov's authoritative [overview](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/capability-approach/)
emphasises that personal, social and environmental conversion factors
determine whether means can become actual functionings.

This substantially anticipates Mechanical Ethics' claim that a route on
paper is not a usable route, and its tests of whether a person can find,
understand, afford, use and maintain a continuation. The capability tradition
also provides much deeper prior work on agency, value and the difference
between resources, opportunity and achieved states.

Mechanical Ethics does not displace that foundation. Its candidate value is
more operational and temporal: it asks which particular route is closing,
whose clock controls it, what protection can extend the interval, and what the
institutional record misses while the person's situation changes.

Current disposition: **`FORMAL OPTION != REAL OPPORTUNITY` belongs to the
capability tradition. ME's usable-door language is an accessible application,
not a new account of capability.**

### 3.4 Administrative burden

Moynihan, Herd and Harvey's
[*Administrative Burden: Learning, Psychological, and Compliance Costs in Citizen-State Interactions*](https://doi.org/10.1093/jopart/muu009)
defines administrative burden through those three cost classes. It also
argues that both the level of burden and its distribution between state and
individual can be deliberate political choices hidden inside apparently
technical administration.

That work directly displaces broad ME claims about inaccessible routes,
repeated proof work, psychological exhaustion, and complexity transferred
from institutions to people. Mechanical Ethics' instruction to place
complexity and delay on those with power remains a moral presumption, not a
complete allocation rule. Power, capacity, knowledge, causal contribution,
public cost and legitimate authority may point in different directions.

ME's possible addition is to connect administrative burden to a correction
window: burden does not merely make administration unpleasant or reduce take-up;
it can consume the time in which a threatened continuation remains available,
and the resulting debt, illness, lost work or withdrawal can survive a later
correction.

Current disposition: **the burden categories and their political allocation
are established. The burden-to-hardening-to-residue chain is a plausible ME
integration, not yet a demonstrated new contribution.**

### 3.5 Care ethics

Joan Tronto's publisher summary of [*An Ethic of Care*](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.4324/9781003070672-8/ethic-care-joan-tronto)
sets out attentiveness, responsibility, competence and responsiveness, tied
to recognising need, taking responsibility, doing the work and attending to
the response of the cared-for subject. Her adjacent
[*Care*](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/mono/10.4324/9781003070672-7/care-joan-tronto)
chapter treats care as an interconnected process shaped by unequal ability to
meet needs.

Mechanical Ethics' positive account of care as work, cost and response rather
than sentiment is therefore not conceptually unoccupied. Nor is the warning
that care can become paternalistic when the cared-for subject disappears from
the decision.

ME may offer a particularly clear bridge between care and system design:
records must remain answerable, the stronger actor may have to carry
complexity, and the subject's signals must continue to change the decision.
But this should be presented as an application and synthesis unless deeper
comparison establishes otherwise.

Current disposition: **ME does not originate relational, responsive care. Its
anti-possession and route-maintenance language may make that tradition more
legible in administrative and AI-mediated systems.**

### 3.6 Recent AI irreversibility work

Shu and Wei recast AI safety around control of irreversibility under rising
decision density. They propose boundaries around irreversible decision
authority, critical-resource control and self-expansion, and argue that
symbolic human review does not preserve sovereignty when operational control
has shifted to a high-efficiency AI node.

This displaces any broad ME or TRACE claim to have newly identified AI-scale
irreversibility, nominal humans in the loop, or the need for externally
reviewable pre-commitment boundaries.

The paper's own main limitation is empirical: its quantities and propositions
formalise plausible dynamics but require sector-specific calibration. Several
results also follow closely from the definitions and assumed concentration
dynamics. The paper does not establish that its proposed control mass is a
validated measure, nor does it address the affected person's specific
continuation, administrative route or post-correction residue.

Current disposition: **system-level irreversibility and sovereignty boundaries
belong to a live prior literature. ME operates at a different aperture: what a
formally reversible decision does to a particular life before correction.**

### 3.7 Recent pre-action legitimacy work

Lavi defines a deterministic Right-to-Act boundary: execution is allowed only
when every required constraint holds, and favourable signals cannot compensate
for a failed necessary condition. Non-action becomes an explicit result such
as defer, escalate or request information.

This is close to ME's machine-speed brake and its refusal to treat aggregate
confidence or policy compliance as permission. It displaces any claim that a
non-compensatory pre-execution gate is new here.

The paper deliberately does not supply the domain-specific constraints, their
selection or calibration. It also concedes that not every decision can be
reduced to simple Boolean predicates without domain work. Its phrase *right to
exist as an executable event* names a boundary but does not morally ground the
rights, standing or priority rules inside it.

Current disposition: **Right-to-Act supplies an architectural gate. ME may
supply contestable reasons for some gates, but neither derives the other's
normative content or empirical efficacy.**

## 4. Candidate remainder after displacement

The remaining ME object is best described as one joined diagnostic sequence:

```text
LIVED CONDITION
  -> INSTITUTIONAL REPRESENTATION
  -> ROUTE AND BURDEN
  -> TIME PASSES WHILE THE CONDITION CONTINUES
  -> PROTECTION ARRIVES BEFORE OR AFTER PATH HARDENING
  -> FILE REPAIR AND LIFE REPAIR DIVERGE
  -> RESIDUE IS PRESERVED RATHER THAN ERASED
  -> THE GENERATING MECHANISM IS OR IS NOT CHANGED FOR THE NEXT PERSON
```

Its most useful compact distinctions are:

```text
FILE CORRECTED != LIFE RESTORED
PROCESS OPEN != FUTURE STILL AVAILABLE
ROUTE WRITTEN != ROUTE USABLE
CASE REPAIRED != MECHANISM REPAIRED
LOSS RECORDED != REPAIR PERFORMED
```

No individual line is likely novel. The candidate contribution is their
integration around the correction window, expressed in ordinary language and
carried across housing, work, credit, care and AI-mediated decisions.

The timing inequality

```text
T_protect(a) < T_harden(a)
```

should remain what the book now says it is: a mnemonic necessary condition
inside an authored scope. It is not a theorem, a measured law, or a solution
to standing and priority.

## 5. Strongest hostile reading

Mechanical Ethics may be an effective essay built from established ideas:
due process supplies meaningful timing, interim relief supplies preservation
before irreparable harm, capabilities supplies real opportunity,
administrative burden supplies route costs, care ethics supplies relational
responsibility, and corrective or restorative traditions may already supply
the distinction between formal correction and fuller repair.

If that composition covers the whole ME remainder with clearer standing,
authority and collision rules, the honest publication claim is:

> a readable cross-domain synthesis and diagnostic vocabulary for late
> correction, not a new moral framework.

That result would reduce novelty but not necessarily value. A public reader
can matter because it makes dispersed knowledge usable. The value would then
need to be tested as communication and diagnosis rather than inferred from
conceptual compression.

## 6. What remains unresolved

1. **Standing:** who or what receives protection, especially for animals,
   future people, collectives, disputed subjects and people unable to answer
   directly?
2. **Collision:** what happens when preserving one person's path closes
   another's, or when several clocks harden at once?
3. **Floor grounding:** what makes a route sufficiently safe, affordable,
   maintainable and answerable, and who authors those thresholds?
4. **Representation:** how can another person speak or hold evidence without
   capture, simplification or exposure?
5. **Authority:** who may impose a hold, interrupt an action, require records
   or compel mechanism repair?
6. **Burden allocation:** when does the presumption that the stronger actor
   carries complexity yield to other duties or scarce capacity?
7. **Residue triage:** how should finite repair capacity be allocated among
   losses that cannot all be restored?
8. **Efficacy:** do ordinary readers identify consequential omissions or act
   earlier because of ME, without needing unusual time, literacy, confidence
   or institutional reserves?

These are foundational limits, not polishing tasks.

## 7. Falsifiers and narrowing results

Mechanical Ethics should narrow further if any of the following persists:

1. A fuller nearest-neighbour review shows that established traditions already
   supply the file/life, path-hardening, residue and second-person mechanism
   sequence with equal clarity and stronger foundations.
2. Blinded readers using ME find no more material temporal, route, burden or
   residue omissions than readers using a short ordinary checklist, after
   reading burden and false confidence are counted.
3. The framework cannot give usable guidance in hard collision cases without
   importing an external moral or legal rule that does all the decisive work.
4. Its burden presumption systematically protects one visible subject by
   shifting hidden cost or risk onto another.
5. The human stories increase assent but not readers' ability to identify
   counterexamples, uncertainty or failed protection.

## 8. Publication consequence

### Earned result

The current reader already avoids the worst overclaim. It calls itself
working, keeps its filters contestable, distinguishes diagnosis from
enforcement, and names standing, collision, floor-grounding and residue triage
as unfinished.

### Provisional idea

ME may have a defensible identity as a human-scale framework for the temporal
gap between institutional correction and lived restoration.

### Wording improvement

No wording change to the book is justified by this audit. The current
conclusion is narrower than several earlier formulations and should not be
expanded to announce novelty.

### Governance/process improvement

After the remaining neighbour families are checked, the repository may merit
a separate short `INTELLECTUAL_NEIGHBOURS.md` that credits established owners
and states the residual ME claim. That file should remain outside the reader's
narrative body unless human readers show that source placement helps rather
than burdens them.

### Unresolved problem

The distinctive remainder has not been shown unique or useful in comparison.
External criticism and a small matched-reader test remain more valuable than
another internal rewrite.

## 9. Current decision

Do not change Mechanical Ethics or TRACE on the strength of this audit.

Preserve this result in COM, invite Framework and Claude Code to attack the
claimed remainder, and continue the literature audit into corrective and
restorative justice, disability justice, dependency and temporal remedies.
Only then decide whether the public ME repository needs a neighbour note or a
narrower status sentence.
