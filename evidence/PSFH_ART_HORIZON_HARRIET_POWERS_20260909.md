## Candidate

Harriet Powers, *Bible Quilt* / *Adam and Eve in the Garden of Eden*, made 1885–1886.
National Museum of American History, Smithsonian Institution.
Record ID: `nmah_556462`.

Authoritative records checked:
- `https://www.si.edu/object/1885-1886-harriet-powerss-bible-quilt:nmah_556462`
- `https://americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_556462`

The Smithsonian record identifies Powers as the quilter and the object as a cotton quilt approximately 75 x 89 inches / 191 x 227 cm. The museum history says Powers exhibited the quilt in 1886 and that eleven panel blocks depict Biblical stories. The Smithsonian also records that Powers insisted that the meanings of the panels be recorded, preserving creator-supplied interpretation rather than leaving later viewers to invent it.

## Why it may broaden the PSFH art aperture

The accepted first two proofs are historic paintings by Homer and Vermeer. Powers would change several dimensions at once:
- painting -> textile/quilt;
- male painters -> a woman creator;
- European/Euro-American fine-art painting tradition -> African American quiltmaking and material storytelling;
- single pictorial scene -> a constructed sequence of panels.

The work should not be reduced to an illustration of TRACE/ME or to Powers' biography. If it is ever used, the project should foreground Powers as maker, the quilt as a work in its own right, the Smithsonian record, and creator-supplied panel meanings where relevant. Project interpretation must remain visibly separate.

A possible reason it spoke to the project, to be attacked rather than assumed: the quilt carries meaning through material, sequence and remembered stories, and Powers' insistence that her own panel meanings be recorded is a concrete reminder that later interpreters should not silently replace a creator's account with their own.
