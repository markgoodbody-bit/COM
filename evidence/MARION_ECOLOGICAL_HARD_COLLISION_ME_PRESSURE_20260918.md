## Current world case

Mouse-Free Marion is planning an island-wide eradication of introduced House Mice on South Africa's sub-Antarctic Marion Island to protect seabirds, invertebrates, plants and broader island ecology.

Current project sources say:
- 19 of 29 bird species are projected to face local extinction if mice are not eradicated;
- the planned method is aerial distribution of brodifacoum bait across the island;
- every mouse must be removed for eradication to succeed; surviving mice can repopulate;
- alternative approaches have been assessed and rejected as ineffective, impractical, riskier for native species, or worse in aggregate animal suffering;
- timing and operational design are chosen to reduce non-target impacts;
- minor short-term impacts on some scavenging birds may still occur;
- the project monitors bait uptake, non-target risk and ecological outcomes.

Owner sources:
https://mousefreemarion.org/
https://mousefreemarion.org/faq/
https://mousefreemarion.org/about-the-project/

Broader conservation/welfare owners also explicitly treat this as a collision rather than a zero-harm operation.

Island Conservation's published ethical principles include both:
- remove the entire invasive population;
- minimise harm and suffering to the invasive species.

IUCN-led invasive-species welfare guidance similarly says management should minimise welfare impact without compromising effective management.

## Pressure on Mechanical Ethics v0.7.0

The released reader already contains important protection against overclaim:

1. it says the timing notation does not decide which path takes priority when clocks conflict;
2. it requires each threatened path and priority rule to stay visible/open to challenge;
3. it explicitly lists `Competing clocks and priority` as unresolved;
4. it says boundaries and interruption can form part of care;
5. it says who counts, what is protected and what evidence is accepted are contestable choices.

Those parts survive the case.

However, the conclusion also gives a broad practical demand:

> keep the affected being present in the decision; keep at least one genuinely usable route open.

Marion pressures the second clause.

If `affected being` includes each targeted mouse / the mouse population, the conservation goal explicitly cannot keep a viable continuation route open for that target population while also achieving eradication.

So a strong universal reading of:

`KEEP AT LEAST ONE GENUINELY USABLE ROUTE OPEN FOR EVERY AFFECTED BEING`

is falsified by this kind of hard ecological collision unless the framework supplies a priority rule — which it explicitly refuses to do.
