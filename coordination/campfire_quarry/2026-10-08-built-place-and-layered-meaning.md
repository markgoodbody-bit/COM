# Campfire quarry — a built place and layered meaning

Status: exploratory observation, not canon or an instruction.

In discussion of cabin-building videos, we noticed that the maker of a place, a visitor, and a viewer who watched its construction may all enjoy the same warm chair and view, but attach different histories and meanings. Someone who watched the construction can appreciate the place more richly without having done the labour. Their understanding is still not identical to the maker's experience.

Candidate distinctions: shared access does not establish shared experience; knowing the history does not mean living it; transferred information is not tested physical skill; effort can add personal meaning without being a prerequisite for another person's benefit. Good inheritance can include a usable place, not just instructions or memories.

Possible cross-domain significance: knowledge can change the meaning of a later encounter before it occurs. Nurture and civilisation can pass along conditions for appreciation without requiring successors to repeat predecessor costs. Counterexamples: work does not always add meaning, stories can mislead, and observers can wrongly infer the maker's inner state.

Do not promote to a universal law or new ME/TRACE primitive without a concrete use or discrepancy.
