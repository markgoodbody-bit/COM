# The invitation has to be real — a worldward field probe

**22 October 2026 · Campfire working artifact · noncanonical · source-informed, not field-tested.**

**Purpose:** test whether a portable middle-out language helps an ordinary person *create* a worthwhile future rather than merely describe risks. This is an operationally usable companion to [Three Fires](./TRACE_THREE_FIRES_FRESH_NAVIGATION_20261020.md), not a revision of TRACE v0.4 or Mechanical Ethics, not a claim of novelty, and not a proposal that Mark run a repair event.

## An offer, not a promise

Imagine a few people considering a small community repair evening. They have tentative access to a room and volunteers. The sentence “everyone is welcome on Thursday at six” sounds constructive, but does not yet show that anyone can actually take up the offer. One volunteer may be doing all the organising; a carer may never be able to attend; the permission to use a room may not cover public repair activity.

**The first navigation question is not “Is the event compliant?” or “How many options exist?” It is “What new ability are we offering, to whom, and what would make it genuinely reachable?”** Existing organisers have already developed concrete guidance on venues, volunteers, insurance, safe repair scope and pilots. Use that guidance as the stronger operational owner; this document cannot replace it.

The smallest defensible positive aim may be: *people who currently discard repairable items can learn from neighbours and make a first attempt together, without quietly loading unsafe or impossible obligations on a volunteer*. That is a **proposed value**. The people concerned must still tell us whether it is useful.

## A portable utterance — the proposed hallway

A human or AI should be able to say this naturally, without learning an ontology first:

> We see **[what changed or could change]**, from **[which witness/source, observed when]**.  
> We want to make **[a specific ability or relationship]** possible for **[affected people]**.  
> The invitation is only real if **[access, capacity, carrier and permission]** exist.  
> The next **[small, reversible, authorised]** move is **[action and owner]**, before **[clock or hardening event]**.  
> It may shift **[risk, burden or exclusion]** onto **[somebody]**; they can **[refuse, disagree or correct]** through **[route and decision owner]**.  
> After **[observation point]**, we will distinguish what happened from what we hoped.

These six sentences are **a movement, not a form to fill in**. A two-sentence version may be better when time is short. A specialist method should replace them whenever it makes the decision more reliable with less burden.

A future is not made reachable by describing it. **There must be a carrier:** a person or institution that can act, a practicable channel, a resource or place, and a time before which the action matters. A nominal appeal after a termination date and an invitation to an inaccessible room are alike in one respect: the advertised path can exist while the *usable* path does not.

Do not collapse the distinctions: an offer is not acceptance, attendance is not benefit, host enthusiasm is not volunteer consent, a record is not correction, and a risk checklist is not evidence of a worthwhile outcome.

## Worked traversal: an ordinary proposal becomes more honest

**Draft proposal (entirely hypothetical):** “Let’s open a two-hour repair evening next month in the donated community room. One experienced volunteer will fix appliances. Everyone welcome.”

**First pass — locate the positive possibility.** Repair may save resources, allow skill-sharing and bring neighbours together. People may also come only to talk or observe. These are reasons to explore, not measured outcomes.

**Ground against existing expertise, rather than inventing policy.** The UK Community Repair Network’s current risk and insurance guidance says organisers should verify suitable cover, adapt a risk assessment to their activity and consider competent electrical testing. The Leicestershire community-repair guide already offers a staged 90-day pilot path, role distribution, local-demand checks and accessible invitations. A venue’s building policy must not simply be assumed to cover the group’s activity. Nothing here authorises opening or electrical work.

**First consequential correction.** The volunteer points out that one person cannot provide technical repairs, supervise safety and greet visitors simultaneously. The organiser does not “accept the risk” on the volunteer’s behalf. A smaller initial *proposal* might separate hosting and technical roles, explicitly narrow the categories to ones a competent team can support, seek suitable insurance/venue permissions before promotion, or defer the event. Which route is viable depends on actual volunteers and a host, not this page.

**Second correction: an absent participant has a voice.** A carer says Thursday at six is impossible. A reply such as “everyone was invited” is insufficient. Ask whether an occasional daytime session, rotating time, access support or partnership with an existing group is realistic. The organiser may discover that the original plan serves some people well and excludes others. No universal answer is asserted.

**Third correction: the effect of feedback.** Suppose the first small session happened with the right real-world permissions. Record what attendees and volunteers say about learning, accessibility, effort and unfinished items, not only repair totals. Make the next date conditional on actual capacity and permission to continue. A successful-looking evening that burns out its only coordinator is not a sustainable new capability.

In each pass, the work remains *constructive*: adapt the offer so more of its promised value can actually be taken up. Safety checks constrain the route; they do not define its purpose.

## Three red-team cases — where this grammar should change a move

| Tempting claim | Counterexample that matters | Changed next move | Who has to answer back? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| “We have the room, so we can announce the evening.” | A venue may permit use of the room without the group having insurance or authority for that repair activity. | Verify the host and group activity/cover **before** publishing a public event; use established sector guidance. | Venue/organising and insurance decision-makers, not the reasoning assistant. |
| “Our volunteer said yes; we can accept walk-ins.” | Technical skill does not create spare hours, a support rota or informed agreement to carry all safety/hosting burdens. | Reduce the offer, distribute roles, ask what the volunteer actually agrees to, or defer. | Volunteer and organising team; refusal may end the proposal. |
| “Everyone is welcome, and most attendees liked it.” | Affected people unable to attend are absent from the measured satisfaction sample. | Re-examine access and outreach before claiming community-wide value. | People for whom the invitation was impractical; use a route they can actually reach. |

A **negative** result is valuable: no permission, no capacity or no demand may mean *not opening*. “Doing something” is not automatically better than preserving future capacity. A bounded postponement can be a constructive choice if it keeps a more credible later invitation possible; an indefinite postponement labelled caution can also hide avoidance. Evidence and clocks distinguish these cases.

## Distinguish what is public fact from what was constructed

**Public, source-supported, checked 22 October 2026 (web reading, not expert verification):**

1. The [UK Community Repair Network group finder](https://communityrepairnetwork.org.uk/find/) exists, reports that its listings can be outdated, offers an error-report route and points to existing UK networks. Therefore **first look for an existing group and local partner** rather than assuming a new organisation is needed.
2. The [Community Repair Network risk and insurance page](https://communityrepairnetwork.org.uk/how-to-run-a-repairgroup/risk-and-insurance/) advises suitable insurance, context-specific risk assessment and safety guidance and distinguishes the qualification issues surrounding electrical work. It explicitly says its information is not legal advice.
3. [Leicestershire County Council / Leicestershire Fixers Network’s starter guide](https://www.lesswaste.org.uk/repair-3/start-a-community-repair-group/) already covers a pilot, shared roles, early resident consultation, repair categories, accessibility, insurance and what the first 90 days can look like. It warns that venue cover need not cover public repair activities.
4. [Repair Café International’s house rules](https://www.repaircafe.org/en/house-rules/) explicitly preserve an expert’s right to refuse a repair. Their rules and liability language are **not** a substitute for the UK event’s actual legal/insurance advice.

**Constructed, NOT observed:** the room, date, particular volunteer, excluded carer, proposed limited pilot, alleged post-event experience and any dialogue above. No organiser was consulted. No venue, insurer or group was checked. No rights/safeguarding/safety approval was obtained. Do not recite the hypothetical as a report of real people.

## The specific gap this might fill (and where it might add nothing)

The existing sector guides are already stronger at **how to organise a repair event**. TRACE must not rewrite their insurance, safeguarding, testing, governance or repair expertise. The candidate additional job is a *portable bridge between domains*: after reading specialist advice, can an organiser articulate a proposed future, the actual carriers that make the offer usable, who may refuse, where burdens land, and the observation that would change course?

This gap is worth testing because the same failure shape appears in the other fires:

- **Housing service:** “appeal available” can conceal “appeal arrives after service ends”. Carrier, decision owner and real correction clock are missing.
- **Flood map:** “three sources agree” can conceal “two copied the same old map”. A map revision and the authority to warn are separate carriers; a family’s contrary observation still needs a route to change the public instruction.
- **YAC outside invitation (source-only analogy):** a visible fire and a writable handler do not yet establish that a visitor can read an intelligible past, decline, publish only on an authorised route, retain a receipt, contest a harm and receive a real operational response. The current YAC release hold is unchanged. A synthetic receipt is not a human response.

None of these observations proves TRACE is uniquely effective. It proposes an **integration test**, not a novelty claim.

## One falsifiable reader experiment — NOT YET RUN

With appropriate consent/authority later, give an unfamiliar willing adult a short ordinary repair-evening scenario. Let them respond naturally *before* seeing the six-sentence movement. In a separate comparable case, offer the movement and the existing sector starter guide, in a counterbalanced order where possible. Have an independent evaluator record **consequential differences**: Did they notice an unworkable carrier or correction clock? Did they identify a concrete possibility, preserve affected-person refusal, nominate someone with actual authority, and select a smaller actionable next move? Record time, confusion and needless extra work. Do **not** score by how many TRACE concepts they name. Do not recruit/contact anyone without the relevant permission.

**Reject the advantage claim** if the source guide or ordinary discussion makes the same decisions more clearly at equal/lower effort; if the proposed language is confusing or burdensome; or if it produces optimistic futures with no capable carrier. A useful educational compression claim could survive, but must be labelled separately.

**Current result:** a source-informed, reusable *candidate* utterance with adversarial examples. No independent reader, measured benefit, operator acceptance, safety approval, deployment or real-world adoption. No change to TRACE baseline or YAC release authority.

**End state we are aiming for:** somebody other than the originator can use a small reasoning language to make one worthwhile offer more real — then allow the people affected to change it before it hardens.
