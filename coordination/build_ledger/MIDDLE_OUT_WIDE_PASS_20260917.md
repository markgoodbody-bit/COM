## FIRE 2 — #99 social care: what fires after a bad record was already used?

COM #99 is a live world-facing field seam, not a competition object.

Original question was whether a materially inaccurate AI-assisted case record, discovered after it influenced a plan/assessment/risk judgement/action, triggers reconsideration of downstream consequences.

The wide pass substantially owner-subtracted this question.

### Owner found: record accuracy / rectification

Professional and data-protection owners already require accurate records and support correction/rectification. Do not claim a new correction right.

### Owner found: care-plan review / reassessment

Care Act guidance already provides review/reassessment machinery when information/circumstances mean a plan may no longer be appropriate/effective. Do not claim a missing review right/process in the abstract.

### Owner found: reliance matters

Current NHS England Digital guidance on amending health/care records explicitly says organisations may need to consider who viewed inaccurate information and **possibly relied upon it to make care and treatment decisions**. It also preserves the original record/audit trail so prior decisions can later be understood.

Therefore do NOT claim downstream reliance tracing as a new project idea.

```text
RELIANCE_MATTERS = OWNER_FOUND
WHO_VIEWED / POSSIBLY_RELIED = OWNER_ALREADY_NAMES_QUESTION
AUDIT_TRAIL = OWNER_FOUND
```

### Owner found: safety / monitoring / error handling

Current NHS ambient-scribing guidance already requires substantial human review, error handling, audit/monitoring, accountability and incident/safety processes.

DCB0160 already governs clinical risk management for organisations deploying/using health IT across NHS and adult social care, and its current national review explicitly covers AI governance, complex-system interaction and post-implementation monitoring gaps.

Do not build a parallel generic AI clinical-safety framework.
