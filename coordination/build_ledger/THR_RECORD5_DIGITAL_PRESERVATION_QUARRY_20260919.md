## Candidate A — Rhizome Conifer twilight

Object:
Rhizome's Conifer hosted web-archiving service, discontinued in June 2026.

Why it looked promising:
- platform/service closure can harden web-archive access and custody;
- Conifer collections may include born-digital art/culture;
- transition itself is a provenance/succession event.

Strongest owner:
Rhizome / Webrecorder.

Owner evidence:
- Rhizome announced the transition in advance;
- existing collections would remain accessible;
- users were being supported in transition to Webrecorder / Browsertrix tools;
- the closure was explicitly framed as continuity of web-preservation work, not abandonment.

Disposition:
`OWNER FOUND / NO CONSEQUENTIAL THR GAP`.

## Candidate B — Rho Jae Oon's *Bite the Bullet* (2008)

Object:
Flash-based web artwork whose original execution environment became obsolete.

Why it looked promising:
- software/runtime dependency;
- migration can silently change the work;
- artist intent and execution environment are provenance-bearing.

Strongest owner:
The 2026 conservation study and associated conservation practitioners / artist.

Owner evidence:
- explicit emulation and migration strategies;
- reconstructed original environment;
- SHA-256 file-integrity checks;
- annotated code modifications documenting deviations from original;
- artist interview used to identify conceptually essential properties;
- broader museum/software-art conservation owners already exist.

Disposition:
`OWNER FOUND / THR WOULD DUPLICATE CONSERVATION RECORD`.

## Candidate C — i-DAT.org decommissioning

Why it looked promising:

- complex web history;
- possible loss of interactive/site context.

Strongest owner:
Former director / institutional backup + Internet Archive.

Owner evidence:

- rough front-page/PDF continuity is maintained;
- a Wayback capture route is explicitly linked.

Disposition:
`OWNER FOUND / NO SPECIFIC LINEAGE GAP OBSERVED`.

## Candidate D — FutureEverything, 1995–2026

Object:
31-year digital-culture organisation that closed as a company in April 2026.

Why it looked promising:
- long digital-cultural history;
- organisational closure can orphan domains, records and project lineage;
- public site says a more comprehensive archive remains in development.

Strongest owner:
FutureEverything legacy/archive project.

Owner evidence:
- closure site remains public;
- 2018–2026 website is preserved at a dedicated archived site;
- selected highlights span earlier decades;
- a retrospective FutureEverything Story series is being researched/published;
- community memory/evidence collection is active, with contributor use choices;
- founder was designated legacy custodian by board/team;
- named archive/legacy contact exists;
- a more comprehensive public repository is explicitly an ongoing archive project.

Disposition:
`OWNER ACTIVE / THR RECORD WOULD PRE-EMPT OR DUPLICATE OWNER ARCHIVE`.

## Result

All four candidates were real preservation pressures. None survived owner subtraction into a specific THR recoverability gap.

```text
FRAGILE DIGITAL MATERIAL != THR PERMISSION
PLATFORM CLOSURE != OWNER ABSENCE
TECHNICAL OBSOLESCENCE != PROVENANCE GAP
ORGANISATION CLOSED != ARCHIVE ORPHANED
OWNER PRESERVATION IN PROGRESS != THR SHOULD RACE IT
```

The quarry strengthens the current selection rule:

```text
THR IS NOT THE BACKUP PLAN FOR EVERY DIGITAL OBJECT AT RISK.
THR RECORD IS EARNED ONLY WHEN A SPECIFIC LINEAGE / RECOVERABILITY RELATION
IS NOT ALREADY BEING ADEQUATELY PRESERVED BY A STRONGER OWNER.
```
