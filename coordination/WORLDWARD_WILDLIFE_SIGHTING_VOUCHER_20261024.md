# Saw something alive? Keep the observation, not a guessed name.
**A printable first-sighting voucher for UK casual wildlife observers — experimental, 24 October 2026**

A beetle, moth, bird, fungus or plant can be worth noticing even when you cannot name it. This is a **temporary field note**, not an official biological record or identification service. Its purpose is to retain the evidence needed for a later, honest decision: **ask for help, submit to an appropriate recorder, or keep it private and unsubmitted**. Use one copy per sighting.

## 3. Before passing it on

☐ Date, location *and its uncertainty*, recorder, observed details are preserved.
☐ Proposed identification is separated from what was directly observed.
☐ Review whether location disclosure could disturb wildlife or expose private property; check the relevant recording service's sensitivity controls. A social-media post does **not** automatically inherit NBN Atlas blurring.
☐ If sharing a photo publicly, first inspect what the image/background/metadata reveals about its location.
☐ If this is someone else's sighting, obtain the recorder's permission before submitting it.

## 4. Where the authority belongs

The established UK **iRecord** service, operated by the Biological Records Centre at UKCEH, accepts casual records with species, date, grid/map location and optional photos; its records can receive expert review. A Local Environmental Records Centre or specialist recording scheme may be a better route for some taxa or localities. This voucher cannot send a record, verify an organism or check current submission rules. Follow the receiving service's latest terms.

- iRecord: getting started and entering casual sightings — https://irecord.org.uk/help/how-do-i
- iRecord: why *unconfirmed* is not *verified* — https://irecord.org.uk/help/records-verified
- iRecord: permission / recording import guidance — https://irecord.org.uk/help/upload
- iRecord: sensitive records and public location disclosure — https://irecord.org.uk/enter-casual-record
- NBN: why some sensitive species locations are generalised — https://nbn.org.uk/sensitive-data/
- A local centre's “four Ws” and warning against guessing — https://swseic.org.uk/wildlife-recording/how-to-make-a-record/

Sources reviewed **24 October 2026**. They are the authorities for the underlying recording practice; this page is an independent, non-endorsed aid. No account, tracking, network request or contact is created by printing this text.

## 5. What would show this card is a bad idea?

A volunteer recorder or local records centre finds that it encourages misidentification, omits a necessary identifier, creates unsafe location sharing, adds more friction than using iRecord directly, or causes unknown sightings to be submitted as confidently named species. Those observations should change or retire this card. **No outside recorder has tested or adopted it.**

**Example, not an actual sighting:** “One small brown flying insect on a kitchen-window plant, 24 October, photo kept privately; exact species unknown.” That is a recoverable *observation*, **not** a verified species record.
