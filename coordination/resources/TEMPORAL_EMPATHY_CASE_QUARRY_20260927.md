## Selection discipline

A useful packet should:
- be source-grounded;
- contain a consequential temporal problem without using project vocabulary;
- be understandable by a competent non-specialist;
- support at least one stronger-owner route;
- leave room for a baseline reader to notice the issue without the card;
- avoid requiring diagnosis of a living person;
- not depend on private information.

The eventual reader packet must contain only the source facts needed for analysis, not the project interpretation below.

## Candidate A — uncertain scheduling / represented certainty

Source:
Bowler, Bach & Pschetz (CHI 2022), *Exploring uncertainty in digital scheduling, and the wider implications of unrepresented temporalities in HCI*.

Owner surface:
https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-uncertainty-in-digital-scheduling-and-the-wider-implica/

Public source facts:
- digital calendars/social-planning tools leave little space to communicate uncertainty about time, place or ability to attend;
- rigid/strictly paced schedules can particularly affect people with health conditions or caring responsibilities;
- Haze was a speculative uncertainty-based scheduling interface;
- two qualitative studies involved 21 participants.

Why useful:
- clean uncertainty / predictability stratum;
- direct temporal-design owner;
- can test whether a reader treats uncertainty as unreliability/noncompliance or as a representational/design issue.

Risk:
- the abstract itself foregrounds the temporal problem strongly, so the teaching card may have little room to add value.
- better as a positive-control / owner case than as the hardest comparison case.

Disposition:
**KEEP / POSITIVE CONTROL CANDIDATE.**

## Candidate B — remembering when / cue burden under concurrent work

Source:
Gilbert et al. review plus later time-based reminder experiment.

Owner surfaces:
- https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02139-4
- https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-025-01708-x

Public source facts:
- people use external reminders for delayed intentions;
- reminders can improve fulfilment of delayed intentions;
- reminder use is affected by memory load, interruption and metacognitive beliefs;
- a later experiment had participants remember delayed keypresses while also performing an ongoing two-back task;
- reminder use improved accuracy and varied with delay length / clock visibility.

Why useful:
- isolates `remember-to-act` from `know-what-to-do`;
- tests whether the reader sees cue/support as capability support rather than decision control;
- exposes interaction between delayed intention and ongoing workload.

Risk:
- laboratory task is low-stakes and artificial;
- may test cognitive-mechanism recognition better than institutional temporal empathy.

Disposition:
**KEEP / MECHANISM CONTROL CANDIDATE.**

## Candidate C — workload-capacity imbalance / intervention adds work

Sources:
- May, Montori & Mair, *We need minimally disruptive medicine*;
- Cumulative Complexity / treatment-burden literature.

Owner surfaces:
- https://www.bmj.com/content/339/bmj.b2803
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5602768/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7434057/

Public source facts:
- treatment/self-care imposes work on patients;
- workload includes healthcare tasks plus ordinary life demands;
- capacity includes time, energy, abilities and resources;
- the same care workload can be manageable or overwhelming depending on context;
- treatment intensification can sometimes worsen workload-capacity imbalance;
- minimally disruptive medicine aims to reduce unnecessary treatment workload while supporting capacity.

Why useful:
- strongest owner found for much of the spinning-plates intuition;
- tests whether a reader notices work created by the remedy itself;
- provides a direct falsifier for project-original workload/capacity language.

Risk:
- broad framework source rather than one bounded event.
- final reader packet should use a concrete public case/study vignette from this literature, not just the model description.

Disposition:
**KEEP / MUST FIND CONCRETE VIGNETTE BEFORE FREEZE.**

## Candidate D — flexibility versus affected-person schedule control

Source:
Kelly et al., *Changing Workplaces to Reduce Work-Family Conflict: Schedule Control in a White-Collar Organization*.

Owner surface:
https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3094103/

Public source facts:
- schedule control concerns employee latitude over timing, hours and location;
- the authors distinguish employee schedule control from forms of employer flexibility such as contingent work or just-in-time staffing;
- a system can therefore be "flexible" from the employer perspective while being unpredictable from the employee perspective.

Why useful:
- tests `FLEXIBLE SYSTEM != AFFECTED PERSON CONTROLS FLEXIBILITY`;
- distinct from deadline/hardening;
- routes to sociology-of-time / work-family literature.

Risk:
- white-collar workplace context may make the answer relatively obvious if the packet uses the authors' definition directly.
- final packet should use neutral intervention facts rather than the interpretive conclusion.

Disposition:
**KEEP / STRONG CONTROL-ASYMMETRY CANDIDATE.**

## Candidate E — institutional care gap / harm continues while route is unresolved

Source:
Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman, 9 July 2026:
*Gaps in NHS dentistry and confusion about care left patients in unnecessary pain*.

Owner surface:
https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/news-and-blog/news/gaps-nhs-dentistry-and-confusion-about-care-left-patients-unnecessary-pain

Public source facts:
- one woman waited eight years for treatment for broken teeth after falling between local dental and hospital services;
- another patient remained in severe pain for over a year after faulty root-canal work and missing appropriate follow-up;
- the Ombudsman presents the cases as illustrating gaps/confusion in responsibility.

Why useful:
- real public service case;
- delay is not empty time: pain / condition continues while responsibility is unresolved;
- tests whether reader notices ownership/route ambiguity and harm accumulation.

Risk:
- source is a high-level press summary; final packet may need the underlying report if enough detail is not publicly accessible.
- health case requires discipline against diagnosis or speculation.

Disposition:
**KEEP / STRONG INSTITUTIONAL-DELAY CANDIDATE.**

## Candidate F — delayed plan / delayed appeal / lost education

Source:
Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman, London Borough of Bromley (22 016 360), decision 17 May 2023.

Owner surface:
https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/education/special-educational-needs/22-016-360

Public source facts:
- the council took too long to produce an Education Health and Care Plan;
- it did not properly consider alternative education;
- the delay postponed the parent's appeal right;
- the child missed education;
- the Ombudsman recommended apology/payment and staff training/guidance.

Why useful:
- clean hardening / lost-route case;
- delay affects both substantive provision and access to a downstream correction route;
- later apology/payment does not literally restore missed education.

Risk:
- education / disability context requires care not to infer clinical needs beyond the source.
- baseline readers may already identify delay easily; the card's possible delta is whether it sees delayed appeal and non-restorable trajectory separately.

Disposition:
**KEEP / STRONG HARDENING CASE.**

## Candidate G — unequal waiting burden

Source:
Holt & Vinopal (2023), *Examining inequality in the time cost of waiting*, Nature Human Behaviour.

Owner surface:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s41562-023-01524-w

Public source facts:
- study uses nationally representative American Time Use Survey data;
- lower-income people were more likely to wait and waited longer when using basic services;
- the authors estimate a minimum annual waiting-time gap;
- measured income differences were not explained by several observed demographic/time-use covariates;
- high-income Black people experienced higher average wait times than high-income non-Black peers in the reported analysis.

Why useful:
- shows waiting as a distributive burden rather than just subjective inconvenience;
- tests whether reader notices imposed duration and unequal acceleration.

Risk:
- population study rather than bounded individual case;
- political/social interpretation can outrun what the study establishes;
- use as owner/population control, not as proof of mechanism in another case.

Disposition:
**KEEP / DISTRIBUTIVE-TIME CONTROL CANDIDATE.**

## Candidate H — complaint system with serial waiting stages

Source:
Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman, current waiting times (updated June 2026).

Owner surface:
https://www.lgo.org.uk/make-a-complaint/current-waiting-times

Public source facts:
- the service publicly reports separate waiting stages;
- at the June 2026 update, most complaints could wait up to 3 weeks at intake, up to 6 months at assessment and up to 5 months at investigation;
- waiting starts again at each stage;
- the Ombudsman says it publishes these times so complainants can make an informed choice about whether to proceed.

Why useful:
- makes institutional waiting explicit and staged;
- clean test for `ROUTE EXISTS != ROUTE RESOLVES BEFORE OTHER CLOCKS`;
- can pair with a separate harmed-case packet without assuming every complainant is harmed by the delay.

Risk:
- no individual outcome;
- could become a generic queueing exercise unless paired with another clock.

Disposition:
**KEEP AS SUPPORTING OWNER/CONTEXT, NOT PRIMARY CASE.**

## Candidate I — one-year administrative response delay

Source:
Parliamentary and Health Service Ombudsman, 21 October 2020:
*Final investigation report of a complaint about UK Visas and Immigration*.

Owner surface:
https://www.ombudsman.org.uk/publications/final-investigation-report-complaint-about-uk-visas-and-immigration

Public source facts:
- a Windrush claimant waited over a year before receiving a response;
- the Ombudsman found failings and recommended compensation for material/emotional distress, apology, and communication/accountability improvement.

Why useful:
- bounded institutional-delay case;
- useful for separating eventual response from burden accumulated during waiting;
- allows comparison with speed-capital / institutional-time literature.

Risk:
- government/immigration context can invite political or historical discussion irrelevant to the timing test;
- only use if packet remains narrowly factual.

Disposition:
**ALTERNATE / NOT FIRST CHOICE.**
