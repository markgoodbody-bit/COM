# Temporal empathy — stronger-owner map and absorption plan

Date: 27 September 2026

Status: **RESEARCH / OWNER MAP / NOT CANON / NO TRACE OR ME PATCH**

Purpose:

Test the Campfire concept currently called `temporal empathy` against prior work in design, HCI, psychology, sociology, public administration, human factors and stress research.

This note asks three questions:

1. What is already owned?
2. What should the project absorb or credit?
3. What cross-domain remainder, if any, is still useful?

## Current project sketch

Campfire currently uses `temporal empathy` to mean roughly:

> represent enough of an affected entity's temporal condition and relevant external clocks to avoid silently substituting the observer's temporal condition for theirs.

The useful live pressures include:
- deadlines / hardening windows;
- competing obligations;
- interruptibility;
- effective correction capacity;
- reminder / cue burden;
- recovery needs;
- uncertainty / unpredictability;
- institutional waiting;
- whether a nominal route can be used in time;
- whether lost time / opportunity is recoverable;
- whether support can restore feasibility without stealing agency.

The provisional spinning-plates image remains a teaching bridge, not a model.

## 1. Exact phrase: `temporal empathy` is already owned

### Pschetz — Temporal Design (2014)

Larissa Pschetz's doctoral work, *Temporal Design: design for a multi-temporal world*, explicitly uses `temporal empathy`.

Owner surface:
https://discovery.dundee.ac.uk/en/studentTheses/temporal-design/

The project challenges dominant uniform/accelerative conceptions of time and makes multiple situated temporalities visible.

Disposition:
**STRONG PRIOR OWNER OF THE TERM + PLURAL-TEMPORALITY DESIGN ORIENTATION.**

### Pschetz & Bastian — Temporal Design (2018)

Larissa Pschetz and Michelle Bastian, *Temporal Design: Rethinking time in design*, Design Studies 56, 169-184.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.destud.2017.10.007

The paper:
- treats time as produced through material, social, economic and political forces;
- challenges narrow uniform/accelerative narratives;
- foregrounds temporal inequalities;
- explicitly promotes temporal empathy.

Disposition:
**PRIMARY INTELLECTUAL NEIGHBOUR.**

Any public project use of `temporal empathy` should credit this lineage rather than presenting the phrase as project-originated.

### Bowler, Bach & Pschetz — Haze (CHI 2022)

*Exploring uncertainty in digital scheduling, and the wider implications of unrepresented temporalities in HCI*

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1145/3491102.3502107

Owner surface:
https://www.research.ed.ac.uk/en/publications/exploring-uncertainty-in-digital-scheduling-and-the-wider-implica/

Key pressure:
digital calendars encode certainty poorly and can marginalise people whose timing is uncertain, including people with health conditions or caring responsibilities.

The Haze studies (two qualitative studies, total n=21) report that uncertainty-oriented scheduling perspectives can challenge moral assumptions around certainty and increase temporal empathy.

This is very close to the project's current concern:

```text
SAME FORMAL CLOCK
!=
SAME PRACTICAL TEMPORAL REACH
```

and:

```text
UNCERTAINTY
!=
NONCOMPLIANCE / UNRELIABILITY BY DEFAULT
```

Disposition:
**DIRECT DESIGN/HCI OWNER + REAL TESTED INTERVENTION.**

## 2. Current digital-system convergence

### Atkinson — Designing for rhythm (2026)

Robert Atkinson, *Designing for rhythm: tempo-setting infrastructures and the temporal conditions of digital life*, AI & Society (2026).

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s00146-026-03229-w

The paper treats digital systems as tempo-setting infrastructures and identifies recurring structures including:
- interruption;
- recovery;
- communication;
- synchronization;
- closure.

Its `temporal empathy` framing treats human pacing as a legitimate design constraint and asks whether system rhythms remain compatible with attention, fatigue, recovery, competing obligations and changing availability.

This is strongly convergent with our present sketch.

However:
- it is a very recent conceptual synthesis;
- the older Pschetz/Bastian lineage remains the stronger prior owner for the term;
- it should be treated as contemporary convergence, not the historical source of the concept.

Disposition:
**CURRENT CONVERGENCE / USEFUL DESIGN VOCABULARY / NOT PROJECT NOVELTY.**

## 3. Time as a distributive resource and power relation

### Goodin — Temporal Justice (2009)

Robert E. Goodin, *Temporal Justice*, Journal of Social Policy.

Owner surface:
https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-social-policy/article/abs/temporal-justice/C19E923FB188E759B9ABA9E4B6823F56

Core:
control over one's time and discretionary time are distributive resources, not merely private scheduling preferences.

Absorb:

```text
CLOCK TIME AVAILABLE
!=
DISCRETIONARY CONTROL OVER TIME
```

### Williams, Masuda & Tallis — Formalizing Time Poverty (2016)

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11205-015-1029-z

Time is a scarce resource allocated to necessities, services, rest and other activities. The literature distinguishes necessary and discretionary time and exposes measurement problems.

### Giurge, Whillans & West — Time Poverty (2020)

*Why time poverty matters for individuals, organisations and nations*, Nature Human Behaviour.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-020-0920-z

Time poverty is linked to lower wellbeing, health and productivity. Material affluence does not imply time affluence.

Absorb:

```text
RESOURCE ADEQUACY
!=
TIME ADEQUACY

MONEY AVAILABLE
!=
CORRECTION TIME AVAILABLE
```

## 4. Institutional delay is itself distributed

### Christ — Institutional time inequality / speed capital (2026)

Stephen R. Christ, *Institutional time inequality: speed capital and the social distribution of waiting*, Theory and Society.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1007/s11186-026-09730-x

This source was already read in the 30 August ME remainder-reduction pass:
`evidence/ME_REMAINDER_REDUCTION_20260830.md`.

It directly owns several broad temporal claims:
- institutions distribute duration;
- access received too late is not equivalent to timely access;
- delay changes trajectories;
- waiting is finite and nonrecoverable;
- resources, representation and procedural literacy can convert into speed.

The 2026 article adds a particularly useful distinction for temporal empathy:

```text
TIME AS SOMETHING I CONTROL
!=
DURATION AN INSTITUTION IMPOSES ON ME
```

and:

```text
SAME EVENTUAL RESULT
!=
SAME TRAJECTORY
```

Project consequence:
do not rediscover this as a new ME theory.

### Holt & Vinopal — inequality in waiting (2023)

*Examining inequality in the time cost of waiting*, Nature Human Behaviour.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-023-01524-w

Using nationally representative US time-diary data, the paper reports unequal service-waiting burdens by income and race.

Useful pressure:
waiting time is not merely subjective inconvenience. It can be empirically distributed unequally.

### Administrative burden

Moynihan, Herd & Harvey (2015), *Administrative Burden: Learning, Psychological, and Compliance Costs in Citizen-State Interactions*.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1093/jopart/muu009

Administrative burden already distinguishes:
- learning costs;
- compliance costs;
- psychological costs.

Time/effort often appear inside learning and compliance burden.

Temporal empathy should therefore not become a vague synonym for administrative burden.

Possible cross-domain addition is only routing:
when burden interacts with closing windows, recovery limits, competing clocks and hardening, point to the stronger burden/time literatures.

## 5. Remembering when to act is itself work

### Prospective memory / intention offloading

Gilbert et al., *Outsourcing Memory to External Tools: A Review of 'Intention Offloading'*, Psychonomic Bulletin & Review (2022/2023).

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02139-4

The review shows that people use calendars, alerts, placed objects and other external tools to remember delayed intentions; intention offloading is effective, measurable and shaped by metacognition.

Particularly useful project correction:

```text
KNOWING WHAT TO DO
!=
REMEMBERING WHEN TO DO IT
```

External reminders can reduce prospective-memory demand without another entity taking over the underlying choice/action.

This sharpens the spinning-plates image:

```text
CORRECTIVE ACTION COST
+
REMEMBER-TO-REVISIT COST
```

Both may matter.

## 6. Workload-capacity balance / Minimally Disruptive Medicine

### Cumulative Complexity Model / treatment burden

A stronger owner exists for one of the central spinning-plates intuitions.

May, Montori & Mair's *Minimally Disruptive Medicine* (BMJ, 2009), and the later Cumulative Complexity Model / treatment-burden literature, treat care as work that competes with the rest of a person's life.

Owner surfaces:
- https://www.bmj.com/content/339/bmj.b2803
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5602768/
- https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7434057/

The model distinguishes:
- **workload**: what the person has to do, including treatment/self-care plus ordinary life demands;
- **capacity**: the abilities/resources available to carry that work;
- dynamic imbalance: when workload exceeds capacity, access, self-care and outcomes can worsen;
- feedback: worsening illness or intensified treatment can increase workload and reduce capacity further.

This is a direct stronger owner of much of the provisional spinning-plates mechanism.

Absorb:

```text
REQUIRED WORK
!=
CAPACITY TO CARRY IT

TREATMENT / CORRECTION ADDED
CAN ITSELF ADD BURDEN

MORE HELP
!=
LESS TOTAL WORK FOR THE AFFECTED ENTITY

NONADHERENCE / MISSED ACTION
!=
LACK OF MOTIVATION BY DEFAULT
```

The literature also makes a point close to temporal empathy:

the same prescribed workload can be manageable for one person and overwhelming for another because work, caring responsibilities, finances, illness burden, social support and other demands differ.

Project correction:

Do not present a general workload/capacity feedback model as project-original.

The possible cross-domain role is narrower:
notice when an institution/system is adding work to the entity whose limited capacity is already part of the problem, then route to treatment-burden / workload-capacity / domain-specific owners.

### Burden-of-treatment / workload-capacity case family

This literature is especially useful for the planned reader probe because it supplies a strong test against projection:

```text
SAME TASK LIST
!=
SAME BURDEN

LOW NOMINAL WORKLOAD
!=
LOW BURDEN IF CAPACITY IS LOW

HIGH WORKLOAD
!=
HIGH BURDEN IF SUPPORT / CAPACITY IS HIGH
```

That means the teaching card should ask what *work the intervention itself adds*, not merely how many other obligations exist.

## 7. Capacity is not one scalar

### Wickens — Multiple Resource Theory

Christopher D. Wickens, *Multiple resources and mental workload*, Human Factors (2008).

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1518/001872008X288394

and 2024 clarification:
https://doi.org/10.1177/10711813241260740

Multiple-resource work warns against treating cognitive capacity as a single pool. Dual-task interference depends on task difficulty, priority and overlapping resource dimensions.

Project consequence:

```text
SPARE CAPACITY SOMEWHERE
!=
CAPACITY FOR THIS TASK
```

Keep `capacity` typed / source-specific where action depends on it.

Do not revive a scalar `C_x(t)` as general human capacity.

## 8. Stress / load across time

### Allostatic load

Juster, McEwen & Lupien (2010) review allostatic load as cumulative multi-system physiological dysregulation under chronic stress.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.neubiorev.2009.10.002

D'Amico, Amestoy & Fiocco (2020) meta-analysis reports small but robust cross-sectional associations between higher allostatic load and poorer global cognition / executive function.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1016/j.psyneuen.2020.104849

Project correction:
do not collapse:
- acute time pressure;
- fatigue;
- chronic stress;
- physiological allostatic load;
- cognitive workload

into one generic `stress reduces capacity` equation.

Temporal empathy should route to the correct owner depending on timescale and mechanism.

## 9. Control over one's schedule matters

### Gerstel & Clawson — Control over Time (2018)

Annual Review of Sociology:
https://doi.org/10.1146/annurev-soc-073117-041400

Focus:
who shapes schedules, how obligations are negotiated/contested, and how inequalities affect control over time.

### Kelly, Moen & colleagues — schedule control

Evidence from workplace interventions suggests that increasing employees' control over timing/location can reduce work-family conflict, while broad "flexibility" is not identical to worker control.

Useful distinction:

```text
FLEXIBLE SYSTEM
!=
AFFECTED PERSON CONTROLS THE FLEXIBILITY
```

This is important for AI / institutional systems that claim to be adaptive while moving variability and coordination cost onto the person.

## 10. Temporal agency / time work

Flaherty, Meinert & Dalsgård (eds.), *Time Work: Studies of Temporal Agency* (2020).

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.2307/j.ctv1tbhq6s

The work studies how people actively alter/customize temporal experience and resist external temporal constraint.

Absorb:
affected entities are not merely passive clocks.

A temporal-empathy account should ask what temporal agency they have:
- can they defer?
- accelerate?
- renegotiate?
- sequence?
- interrupt?
- signal uncertainty?
- obtain help?
- refuse synchronization?

## 11. Perspective across time / historical empathy

Historical-empathy traditions distinguish empathy from sympathy and emphasize contextual perspective-taking while resisting presentism.

This is useful as a methodological warning:

```text
MODEL ANOTHER TEMPORAL POSITION
!=
PROJECT PRESENT-DAY ASSUMPTIONS ONTO IT
```

But historical empathy is not the same construct as practical temporal burden / timing design.

## 12. Future-self continuity

Hal Hershfield's future-self continuity work shows that how people represent their future selves can affect intertemporal decisions.

DOI:
https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1749-6632.2011.06201.x

This owns part of:
- prospective reach;
- vividness of future self;
- present action for future benefit.

Do not collapse future-self continuity into temporal empathy. It is a neighbouring mechanism.

## 13. What the project should absorb

### A. Credit the term

If `temporal empathy` survives as project language, explicitly credit Temporal Design / Pschetz / Bastian.

Do not claim:
`TEMPORAL EMPATHY = FRAMEWORK INVENTION`.

### B. Replace one vector with an owner-routed map

Do not promote `Theta_x`.

Use dimensions only as prompts whose detail belongs to stronger owners:

```text
1. IMPOSED DURATION / WAITING
2. DISCRETIONARY CONTROL / TEMPORAL AGENCY
3. PREDICTABILITY / UNCERTAINTY
4. DEADLINES / HARDENING / IRREVERSIBILITY
5. COMPETING OBLIGATIONS / RESOURCE INTERFERENCE
6. REMEMBER-TO-ACT / CUE BURDEN
7. RECOVERY / FATIGUE / STRESS TIMESCALE
8. SUPPORT / SHARED CAPABILITY
9. ACCELERATION / SPEED PRIVILEGE
10. TRAJECTORY / WHAT DELAY FORECLOSES
```

The value is not in owning each dimension.
The possible project value is making it cheap to notice which owner matters before acting.

### C. Separate four different temporal questions

```text
HOW MUCH CLOCK TIME?
!=
WHO CONTROLS THE TIME?
!=
WHO IMPOSES THE WAIT?
!=
WHAT CHANGES WHILE WE WAIT?
```

This is a better compression than a scalar score.

### D. Represent uncertainty rather than punishing it

The Haze work strongly supports testing interfaces / procedures that let people express uncertain timing without being treated as noncompliant.

Candidate design questions:
- can a person express a time window rather than a false exact time?
- can availability be probabilistic / revisable?
- can a route signal that capacity changed?
- is uncertainty visible to the other side?
- does the institution punish uncertainty it helped create?

### E. Treat recovery time as functional infrastructure

Not every empty interval is spare capacity.
Recovery can be required to sustain future capability.

```text
NO SCHEDULED TASK
!=
AVAILABLE CAPACITY
```

### F. Treat reminders as capability support

A reminder can restore feasibility without transferring authority.

```text
CUE SUPPORT
!=
DECISION CONTROL
```

This may be particularly useful for accessible systems and Local Steward design.

## 14. The remaining cross-domain project hypothesis

After owner subtraction, the defensible remainder is not a new theory of time or empathy.

Candidate:

> A bounded cross-domain prompt can help an observer detect when they are substituting their own temporal condition for an affected entity's, then route the case toward the stronger owner before a consequential window closes.

That is a **teaching / routing hypothesis**.

It must be tested.

## 15. Work Framework can do next

### Work A — owner matrix

Build a compact table mapping temporal questions to:
- stronger owner;
- what they measure;
- what they do not measure;
- project routing phrase;
- example case.

### Work B — six-case temporal-empathy probe

Select six real/public cases with sharply different temporal failure modes:
1. institutional delay;
2. uncertain scheduling / health or care constraint;
3. prospective-memory/reminder burden;
4. competing deadline overload;
5. nonrecoverable hardening;
6. speed-capital / unequal acceleration.

Freeze source packets.

Ask readers first without the temporal-empathy prompt, then compare against a matched/independent reader with the prompt.

Measure:
- consequential clocks noticed;
- affected-entity constraints noticed;
- false assumptions introduced;
- route/action differences;
- reading/time burden.

No score or promotion until blinding is genuinely clean.

### Work C — minimal teaching card

Candidate prompt:

```text
WHOSE TIME IS THIS?

What is imposing the timing?
What is uncertain or unpredictable?
Which windows close, and what changes while waiting?
What other obligations compete for the same capacity?
What cue/support is needed to act in time?
Who can accelerate, defer or renegotiate the process?
What lost time / future cannot be restored later?
```

Test against a shorter baseline. If it adds no reasoning value, delete it.

### Work D — design intervention quarry

Borrow concrete intervention patterns from Temporal Design / HCI:
- represent uncertainty;
- time windows rather than false point estimates;
- explicit response expectations;
- protected recovery / closure;
- notification batching;
- deferral / rescheduling without stigma;
- reminder offloading;
- visible process state / next check time.

Test which travel into institutional / AI-mediated settings without distortion.

ME already earned the need for an eventual intellectual-neighbours document.
Temporal Design / temporal justice / institutional time inequality / administrative burden / disability temporalities should be part of that credit map.
