# Case 5 — NHS patient-initiated follow-up (PIFU)

Status: **STRONG OWNER / SOPHISTICATED SAFETY-NET DESIGN / IMPLEMENTATION VARIATION OBSERVED / NO NEW POLICY OBJECT**

## Apparent temporal-empathy critique

A shallow reading could say:

> PIFU removes routine follow-up and shifts responsibility for noticing deterioration, remembering when to act and obtaining care onto the patient.

That can happen if implementation is poor.

But it is not a fair description of current NHS England guidance.

## Current strongest owner

NHS England, *Implementing patient initiated follow-up: Guidance for local health and care systems*, version 2, 24 September 2025:

https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/implementing-pifu-guidance-local-health-care-systems/

The guidance already requires/encourages a substantial temporal safety structure.

### Suitability / burden

PIFU suitability includes whether the patient/carer:
- can recognise relevant changes;
- understands how/when to contact the service;
- has sufficient knowledge/skills/confidence;
- can access the service;
- can carry the responsibility for the relevant period.

It says PIFU needs careful consideration for complex cases, fixed-timescale clinical needs, safeguarding concerns, monitoring-dependent medicines and difficult service access.

### Shared responsibility / fallback

The guidance allows:
- carer/guardian involvement;
- blended timed + patient-initiated follow-up;
- support to build confidence/skills;
- return to traditional timed follow-up if PIFU does not work for the patient.

### Tracking / review

Owner guidance says providers should:
- log and track PIFU patients;
- record review/end dates;
- generate reports for review/expiry;
- maintain clinically required timed review frequency;
- contact patients who do not initiate where clinical guidance requires review;
- ensure tests are reviewed even if the patient does not initiate;
- investigate incidents/near misses where failure to initiate contributed to poor outcome.

### Prospective-memory / cue burden

The guidance explicitly names the risk that patients may:
- forget when/how to get in touch;
- avoid contacting services because they do not want to "bother" them.

Mitigations include:
- digital + hard-copy information;
- monitoring whether links are opened;
- re-sending information;
- clear contact details/resources;
- keeping GPs informed.

### Activation-to-access

The national guidance explicitly identifies another failure mode:

> patients may disengage if access takes too long after they contact the service.

It advises local target wait times, capacity/demand planning, urgent-access processes and recording patient/staff experience of whether contact led to timely care.

Specialty guides can be more specific. Kidney transplant / CKD guidance, for example, uses a maximum 2-working-day review of a PIFU request and clinician-determined appointment targets with urgent/non-urgent upper bounds.

Therefore:

~~~text
PATIENT INITIATES
!=
SERVICE MAY WAIT INDEFINITELY
~~~

## Current implementation evidence

2026 Nuffield Trust / NHS staff evaluation:

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13263458/

Rapid qualitative evaluation:
- 36 interviewees / staff participants across five English NHS Trusts plus a workshop with 21 additional NHS staff;
- implementation varied materially by specialty/site;
- safety-netting, patient selection/onboarding and admin work changed staff roles;
- some staff described patients selected for PIFU failing to initiate when needed;
- poorly integrated EPR systems created time-consuming workarounds;
- administrative workload could increase;
- some PIFU patients still faced long waits after initiating;
- poor hospital/GP communication could displace workload into primary care.

This is bounded qualitative/service-evaluation evidence, not a population efficacy estimate.

## Conformance reading

The main PIFU problem exposed here is:

~~~text
GOOD SAFETY-NET DESIGN
!=
CONSISTENT LOCAL IMPLEMENTATION
~~~

and:

~~~text
PATIENT CONTROL
!=
PATIENT CARRIES ALL MONITORING RISK
~~~

The current stronger owner already knows this.

### Candidate conformance checks

Without inventing a new policy, a local audit can ask:

~~~text
PIFU SUITABILITY CRITERIA SPECIFIED?
-> ACTUALLY APPLIED?

PATIENT UNDERSTANDING CONFIRMED?
-> OR ASSUMED?

PIFU CONTACT MADE?
-> TRIAGED IN TARGET TIME?

TRIAGE SAYS APPOINTMENT NEEDED?
-> APPOINTMENT REACHED IN TARGET TIME?

NO PATIENT INITIATION?
-> WAS A CLINICALLY REQUIRED REVIEW / SAFETY NET DUE?

TEST RESULT EXISTS?
-> REVIEWED WITHOUT WAITING FOR PATIENT INITIATION?

PIFU NOT WORKING FOR PATIENT?
-> RETURN TO TIMED PATHWAY AVAILABLE / USED?

WORK REMOVED FROM OUTPATIENT CLINIC?
-> DID IT REAPPEAR AS ADMIN / GP / PATIENT WORK?
~~~

These are conformance questions over owner-defined safeguards.

## Result

**OWNER FOUND / DESIGN GAP NOT EARNED / IMPLEMENTATION-FIDELITY + BURDEN-TRANSFER PRESSURE SURVIVES.**

No new PIFU feature proposal.

# Case 6 — Awaab's Law / housing hazard clocks

Status: **STRONG LEGAL/OMBUDSMAN OWNER / REAL POST-IMPLEMENTATION EVIDENCE / CONFORMANCE GAP OBSERVED / NO NEW POLICY OBJECT**

## Strongest current owner

Awaab's Law Phase 1 has applied to social housing in England since 27 October 2025 for emergency hazards and significant damp/mould hazards.

Current government / Housing Ombudsman material defines:
- notice/knowledge routes;
- hazard classification;
- investigation clocks;
- make-safe/work clocks;
- written-summary obligations;
- continuing tenant updates;
- temporary accommodation where relevant;
- material-change re-triggering;
- complaint and hazard clocks running concurrently.

Owner surfaces:
- https://www.gov.uk/government/collections/awaabs-law-in-the-social-rented-sector
- https://www.housing-ombudsman.org.uk/landlords-info/guidance-notes/guidance-on-complaints-involving-hazards/
- https://www.housing-ombudsman.org.uk/landlords-info/complaint-handling-code/the-code-2024/

## Important temporal owner structures

### Trigger reach

Housing Ombudsman guidance says relevant knowledge can arrive through:
- resident reports;
- landlord staff/inspections;
- contractors;
- representatives;
- family/friends/neighbours;
- other agencies.

It also examines what the landlord knew or ought to have known.

~~~text
RESIDENT DID NOT USE ONE PARTICULAR CHANNEL
!=
ORGANISATION NECESSARILY HAD NO NOTICE
~~~

### Concurrent clocks

A complaint can itself contain notice of a hazard/material change.

Where relevant:
- Awaab's Law hazard timeframes apply;
- Complaint Handling Code timeframes also apply.

The Complaint Handling Code separately says landlords must not stop addressing the underlying service request merely because a complaint has been raised.

~~~text
COMPLAINT CLOCK
!=
REPAIR / HAZARD CLOCK
~~~

### Material change / currentness

A material change can require a new investigation and restart the relevant timeframes.

Changes can include:
- worsening condition;
- worsening health effect;
- changed household circumstances.

~~~text
OLD HAZARD ASSESSMENT
!=
CURRENT HAZARD STATE
~~~

### Resolution versus compliance

Current guidance requires written summaries and continuing updates; time limits cover investigation and works.

But owner material also stresses root-cause / durable remediation and continuing communication.

## Actual post-implementation evidence

MHCLG-funded *Awaab's Law Phase 1: Test and Learn research report*, published 13 July 2026:

https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/awaabs-law-phase-1-test-and-learn-research-report/awaabs-law-test-and-learn-research-report-executive-summary-accessible-version

Fieldwork:
- 30 social-housing tenants who had reported relevant hazards since the law came into force;
- 35 registered social landlords via six focus groups;
- December 2025 to March 2026;
- policy/operational document review.

It reports early behaviour/accountability improvements but uneven/fragile implementation.

Observed pressures include:
- variable responsiveness;
- fragmented case handling;
- damp/mould delays after initial inspection;
- vulnerability not consistently identified at first contact;
- written summaries not always received;
- lack of updates/single point of contact;
- ambiguity over definitions and when statutory clocks start;
- incomplete/inconsistent vulnerability data;
- IT interoperability constraints;
- workforce/contractor capacity pressures;
- local workarounds and variation;
- risk that evidencing compliance takes priority over root-cause resolution.

The report highlights end-to-end case management, structured triage, clear/versioned digital requirements and outcome-focused assurance as important implementation directions.

## Conformance reading

This is almost a textbook example of:

~~~text
RULE PRESENT
!=
RULE OPERATIONALISED CONSISTENTLY

CLOCK IN REGULATION
!=
CLOCK TRIGGERED / TRACKED CONSISTENTLY

WRITTEN SUMMARY REQUIRED
!=
TENANT RECEIVED USEFUL CURRENT INFORMATION

DEADLINE ACTIVITY
!=
DURABLE HAZARD RESOLUTION
~~~

No new law/policy is needed from us.

### Candidate conformance checks

~~~text
NOTICE / KNOWLEDGE EXISTS?
-> CAPTURED FROM ALL RELEVANT CHANNELS?

MATERIAL CHANGE?
-> RE-TRIAGED / NEW CLOCK STARTED?

VULNERABILITY INFORMATION?
-> CURRENT + USABLE AT TRIAGE?

HAZARD CLOCK STARTED?
-> START BASIS RECORDED?

COMPLAINT ALSO OPEN?
-> BOTH CLOCKS CONTINUE?

INVESTIGATION DONE?
-> ACTION / BASIS / TARGETS COMMUNICATED?

MAKE-SAFE / WORK ORDERED?
-> ACTUALLY COMPLETED?

WORK COMPLETE?
-> HAZARD / ROOT CAUSE DURABLY RESOLVED?

STATE WORSENS / RECURS?
-> MATERIAL CHANGE / REVIEW ROUTE FIRES?
~~~

## Result

**OWNER FOUND / POLICY-THEORY GAP NOT EARNED / REAL CONFORMANCE GAP OBSERVED IN EARLY IMPLEMENTATION.**

# Cross-case synthesis

PIFU and Awaab's Law come from very different domains.

Both nevertheless show the same cross-domain failure layer:

~~~text
PROTECTION DESIGNED
-> LOCAL IMPLEMENTATION
-> REAL TRIGGER
-> REAL CLOCK
-> REAL OWNER
-> REAL ACT
-> REAL OUTCOME
~~~

The load-bearing question is often:

> where did the safeguard stop propagating?

That earns the working conformance audit, but not a new TRACE/ME primitive.

## New distinction

~~~text
DESIGN GAP
!=
EXECUTION GAP
~~~

A system can have:
- the right distinction;
- the right policy;
- a clear trigger;
- a named route;

and still fail because:
- the trigger was not captured;
- the clock did not start;
- the team did not receive the information;
- capacity was absent;
- the action was not completed;
- the state changed without review;
- the activity metric was met while the outcome remained poor.

## Relation to TRACE

TRACE already explicitly says:

~~~text
DISTINCTION_PRESENT != DISTINCTION_APPLIED
TRIGGER_PRESENT != TRIGGER_FIRED
~~~

The applied cases show that this is not merely formal caution.

It is an operationally important distinction.

No TRACE patch is earned.

## Relation to Mechanical Ethics

ME already asks who carries delay/complexity, what happens while waiting, whether correction reaches the life rather than only the file, and what remains after formal repair.

No ME patch is earned.

## Current disposition

**CONFORMANCE LAYER USEFUL / STRONGER OWNERS PRIMARY / NO POLICY INVENTION / NO TRACE OR ME PATCH / KEEP APPLYING TO REAL SYSTEMS.**
