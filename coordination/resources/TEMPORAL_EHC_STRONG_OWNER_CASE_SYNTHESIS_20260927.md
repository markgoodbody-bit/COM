# EHC temporal preservation — stronger-owner case synthesis

Date: 27 September 2026

Status: **STRONGER OWNER FOUND / 20-WEEK PRIMARY TRIGGER DEMOTED / EVENT-TRIGGERED PATTERN SURVIVES / NO LEGAL CLAIM**

Purpose:

Pressure the provisional EHC 20-week temporal-preservation checkpoint against recent Local Government and Social Care Ombudsman cases.

## Core correction

The project's first candidate used the ordinary 20-week EHC-plan horizon as the main trigger.

That is too late and too plan-centric.

The stronger owner already treats the education-protection clock as distinct from the EHC-plan clock.

The relevant trigger can arise as soon as the council becomes aware that a child may not be receiving suitable education.

Therefore:

~~~text
EHC 20-WEEK CLOCK
!=
SECTION-19 / CURRENT-EDUCATION TRIGGER
~~~

and:

~~~text
WAIT UNTIL PLAN OVERDUE
CAN BE
TOO LATE FOR THE PARALLEL EDUCATION CLOCK
~~~

The 20-week horizon remains useful as one EHC-process currentness checkpoint, not as the primary protection trigger.

## Case A — Essex County Council 24 018 461

Owner:
https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/education/special-educational-needs/24-018-461

Decision date:
12 August 2025.

The case shows both correct and incorrect temporal behaviour.

Public findings include:
- an EHC request in September 2024 stated that Y was not attending school full-time;
- the Council said it considered its alternative-education duty at that point and decided the school's provision was suitable;
- the Ombudsman found the Council should have become aware earlier, in June 2024, when its Early Help team was contacted about Y's attendance;
- the Ombudsman explicitly said it was irrelevant which council team knew because there should be internal communication;
- the Council was also faulted for failing to keep the alternative provision under review;
- when Y's provision later broke down and Y stopped attending, the Council did not start funding at-home tuition until the end of March 2025.

The Ombudsman's stated expectations include:
- consider individual circumstances;
- act whatever the reason for absence where the legal duty is engaged;
- keep part-time education under review;
- put chosen action into practice without delay;
- retain oversight where functions are carried out by schools/others.

Service-improvement actions in the decision included:
- regular updates for families waiting for EHC assessments;
- review of out-of-school procedures;
- staff briefing so front-line staff understand they must alert relevant teams if a child is not attending full-time.

### Lesson

This is much richer than a 20-week trigger:

~~~text
ANY RELEVANT TEAM LEARNS CHILD MAY LACK SUITABLE EDUCATION
->
CURRENT-EDUCATION / SECTION-19 CONSIDERATION
->
RECORD DECISION
->
KEEP ARRANGEMENT UNDER REVIEW
->
REACT WHEN STATE CHANGES
~~~

## Case B — Shropshire Council 25 002 787

Owner:
https://www.lgo.org.uk/decisions/education/alternative-provision/25-002-787

Decision date:
3 March 2026.

Public summary states:
- EHC assessment/plan decision was delayed;
- the Council was at fault for failing to decide whether alternative provision should be put in place when it was made aware in October 2024 that Y was not in school;
- the Ombudsman found no resulting injustice from that particular failure because the school had put appropriate support in place.

### Lesson

Important separation:

~~~text
FAILURE TO CONSIDER / DECIDE
!=
PROOF OF LOST EDUCATION IN THAT CASE
~~~

The process defect can be real even when another route happened to protect the child.

## Case C — Gloucestershire positive and negative controls

LGSCO public decisions/service-improvement records show both failure and competent response.

Examples:

### 25 002 794 — no fault on alternative provision

Public summary says the Council acted without delay on the alternative-provision issue, convened a multi-agency meeting and assessed the child's situation, even while the EHC process itself was delayed.

### 24 020 378 — suitable alternative provision was put in place

Public summary states the Council put suitable alternative education in place when the child stopped attending school, even though the EHC-plan process had separate faults.

### Service-improvement actions from other Gloucestershire cases

The Ombudsman records recommendations/actions including:
- review oversight of children out of school with an EHC Plan;
- require clear recorded reasons for section-19 decisions;
- obtain details of part-time timetables and review their efficacy without delay;
- improve internal processes/communication for section-19 responsibility;
- record why part-time provision is considered suitable where full-time is not arranged.

## Revised smallest pattern

**Parallel-clock preservation**

Do not make the EHC-plan clock the gate for checking the child's current education state.

Potential triggers include:
- any council team receives credible information that the child is not attending or may not be receiving suitable education;
- an EHC request/assessment records reduced or absent attendance;
- existing alternative/reduced provision changes or breaks down;
- a periodic review point arrives while the EHC process remains unresolved;
- the 20-week EHC horizon is crossed.

At a trigger, the system should support the responsible owner to establish/record:

~~~text
CURRENT EDUCATION STATE
+
WHETHER A PARALLEL DUTY / PROTECTION ROUTE MUST BE CONSIDERED
+
DECISION / BASIS
+
WHO OWNS ACTION
+
WHEN THE ARRANGEMENT IS REVIEWED AGAIN
~~~

This is a **routing/checkpoint pattern**, not a legal decision rule.

## Critical distinction

~~~text
CONSIDERED ONCE
!=
ADEQUATELY KEPT UNDER REVIEW
~~~

and:

~~~text
EHC PROCESS CONTINUES
!=
CURRENT EDUCATION CAN WAIT
~~~
