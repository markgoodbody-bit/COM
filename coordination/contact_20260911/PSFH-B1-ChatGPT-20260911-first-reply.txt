Applied here, I’d still stay in the £350 venue for now, but I’d make the next few sessions a deliberate test.

What we actually know: attendance historically varies from 18–45; the room safely holds 30; recent registrations are 34–41; cancellations normally run 20–30%; and moving would add £300/month with a six-month lock-in. So those recent registrations imply roughly 24–33 eventual attendees. The evidence therefore says “possibly outgrowing the room,” not yet “definitely outgrown it.”

The site's emphasis on time and correction is particularly relevant. Keeping the existing venue preserves a readily reversible choice; signing the larger lease converts uncertainty into a £3,900 six-month obligation rather than £2,100 for six months at the present rate—an incremental £1,800 commitment. Its framing specifically asks what becomes harder to correct with time and what evidence should select the next step.

So I would set a decision rule now: for perhaps the next 3–4 comparable sessions, record registrations, confirmed attendees shortly before the event, actual arrivals, waitlist size, and people who wanted to attend but couldn't. Don't exceed the 30-person capacity; use confirmations and a waitlist rather than assuming no-shows will make overbooking safe.

What would matter most to me is not another month of raw sign-up numbers, but evidence of unmet real demand. If actual attendance repeatedly reaches 30 and, say, another 5–10 people would genuinely have attended, that's strong evidence for moving. If 38 registrations continue turning into 26–29 people, the larger venue mostly buys empty space.

One thing the site adds that my first answer underweighted is whose situation changes. A bigger venue could be worthwhile even without a purely financial case if the current space is crowded, inaccessible, unpleasant, or routinely excludes people; conversely, a new location could make travel or access worse for existing participants. Its “reachable futures” question—an option isn't genuinely useful merely because it exists—is a sensible check here.
