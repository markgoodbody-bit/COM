I’d keep the current venue for now rather than lock into the larger one for six months.

The recent sign-ups look strong, but with a 20–30% cancellation rate, 34–41 sign-ups translate to roughly 24–33 actual attendees. That puts you right around the current 30-person capacity—not clearly beyond it. Moving would raise the monthly cost by £300 and commit you to an extra £1,800 over six months, while giving up the flexibility of month-to-month.

For the next few sessions, I’d use a 30-person capacity plus a waitlist, or cautiously accept a few extra registrations if historical cancellation rates are reliable. That tells you whether you're genuinely constrained by the venue rather than merely seeing higher registrations.

The information I’d care about most is:

Actual attendance, not sign-ups: How often do more than 30 people really arrive? A repeated 32–35 is very different from one unusually busy session.

Unmet demand: Are people unable to attend because the session is full? How many join a waitlist, and how many would actually take released places?

Trend persistence: Are the last three sessions the start of sustained growth, or could they be seasonal/promotional spikes?

Economics or community value of extra attendees: Would accommodating another 5–15 people generate enough revenue, funding, membership growth, or impact to justify another £300/month?

Cancellation predictability: If cancellations reliably happen early enough to refill places from a waitlist, the 30-person venue is much less limiting.

A useful decision rule would be: move once actual attendance is repeatedly hitting 30 and you're demonstrably losing worthwhile participants because of capacity. Until then, the £350 venue buys you something valuable besides space: flexibility.
