## Source facts

The researchers start from the observation that ordinary digital calendars and social-planning tools leave little space to communicate uncertainty about time, place or whether someone will be able to attend.

Their earlier interviews with seven people with chronic fatigue syndrome found uncertainty to be a recurring part of daily life that could create difficulties in social interaction and communication.

They designed a speculative interface called Haze. It allowed events to be represented with uncertainty around time, date and location, with that uncertainty made visible to other people so it could be negotiated.

They then used Haze with 14 additional participants from varied backgrounds to discuss uncertainty in social digital communication and scheduling.

The published work reports two qualitative studies with 21 participants in total.

## Ceiling

Use only the facts above for the reader task.

Do not infer:
- that all people with chronic illness experience time in the same way;
- that Haze was a deployed production system;
- that the study established a universal scheduling rule;
- a medical conclusion about any participant.
