# Temporal-empathy reader packet 05

Status: **FROZEN SOURCE PACKET / PUBLIC OBSERVATIONAL STUDY SUMMARY / NO PROJECT ANALYSIS**

## Source

*Association Between Mobile Telephone Interruptions and Medication Administration Errors in a Pediatric Intensive Care Unit*

https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6990809/

## Source facts

The cohort study included 257 nurses and 3,308 patients in a pediatric intensive care unit and analysed 238,540 medication-administration attempts.

The study examined incoming calls and text messages on nurses' institutional mobile telephones around medication administration.

The overall medication-error rate was reported as 3.1% when nurses were not interrupted by incoming telephone calls and 3.7% when they were interrupted by such calls.

The association varied by circumstances. The reported risk differed by day/night shift, nurse experience, nurse-to-patient ratio and the level of care required by patients.

Incoming text messages were not associated with error in the same way as incoming calls in the reported analysis.

The authors state that communication-related interruptions cannot simply be eliminated and that interventions should take contextual factors into account.

## Ceiling

Use only the study facts above.

Do not infer:
- that every phone call causes an error;
- that nurses should never be interrupted;
- that the study proves a single causal mechanism for all medication errors;
- anything about an individual nurse's competence.
