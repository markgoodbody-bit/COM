## Source

Leppin, Montori and colleagues (2015):

*Minimally Disruptive Medicine: A Pragmatically Comprehensive Model for Delivering Care to Patients with Multiple Chronic Conditions*

## Source facts

The paper introduces a **fictitious** patient named Debbie.

Debbie is 55. She has type 2 diabetes, mild depression and a history of treated breast cancer. She is divorced, lives with her teenage son, and works as a bank teller.

After an emergency-room visit for a sprained ankle, follow-up testing shows elevated blood glucose. Her clinician recommends transition to insulin and schedules an appointment with a diabetes educator for diabetes self-management and insulin-use training.

Three months later Debbie returns reporting exhaustion and difficulty sleeping. Her HbA1c is higher. She did not attend the diabetes class, did not feel comfortable using insulin, and continued taking oral medication.

The clinician reiterates the importance of insulin and suggests that attending the diabetes class will give Debbie the confidence she needs. Debbie promises to schedule the appointment.

This packet stops at that point in the published illustrative sequence. Later material is not included.

## Ceiling

This is an illustrative vignette written by the paper's authors, not a report about an identified real patient.

Do not:
- diagnose Debbie;
- invent why she missed the class;
- assume lack of motivation;
- assume the clinician acted negligently;
- infer facts not contained above.
