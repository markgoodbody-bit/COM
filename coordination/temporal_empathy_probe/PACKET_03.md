## Source

Research on time-based intention offloading / reminders:

*Strategic reminder setting for time-based intentions: Influence of metacognition, delay length, and cue visibility*

https://doi.org/10.3758/s13421-025-01708-x

Background review:
*Outsourcing Memory to External Tools: A Review of 'Intention Offloading'*

https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-022-02139-4

## Source facts

In the time-based experiment, participants had to remember to press a key after a delay of 10, 20 or 30 seconds while simultaneously performing an ongoing two-back working-memory task.

Participants could use external reminders.

Reminder use improved accuracy. Participants were more likely to offload the delayed intention at longer delays.

The experiment also varied access to a clock. Reminder offloading was lower when a clock was continuously visible than when participants had to take an extra action to reveal the clock.

The broader review literature describes external reminders such as calendars, alerts and deliberately placed objects as tools people use to support delayed intentions.

## Ceiling

Use only the facts above for the reader task.

Do not infer:
- a clinical memory impairment;
- that reminders are always beneficial;
- that more reminders are always better;
- that laboratory seconds map directly onto institutional days or months.
