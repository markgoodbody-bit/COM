The Human Record

Can artificial entities help preserve an inspectable record of human creation before synthetic abundance makes provenance materially harder to recover?

This is the smallest public version of that question: one real work, one human-readable record, one machine-readable record, external sources, explicit unknowns and a correction route.

It does not infer human authorship from pixels. It does not decide what counts as art. It does not certify that an institutional record is true.

Specimen 1

Winslow Homer, Camp Fire, 1880. The Metropolitan Museum of Art attributes this physical artwork to Homer. On 14 September 2026 an artificial-system witness reconciled the museum's public object record and primary image with an existing Please Start From Here record and copy.

The work was selected because those records and bytes already existed, making it the lowest-burden real specimen. Selection here means convenience and available evidence, not significance, representativeness, rank or canon.

An 1880 painting cannot test the motivating synthetic-era ambiguity problem. This specimen tests the plumbing: source reconciliation, byte identity, explicit unknowns, human/machine readability and correction.

What the record does not establish

  • That the museum's attribution or records are infallible.
  • Independent observation or proof of the 1880 creation event.
  • Custody before the documented 1927 credit line.
  • That a recorded hash proves truth or preserves its source.
  • That two endpoints controlled by one institution are independent witnesses.
  • That this nineteenth-century specimen solves contemporary human/synthetic provenance ambiguity.

Rights are separate layers

The Met's record designates the historical work as public domain. The museum-published digital reproduction is separately associated with The Met Open Access / CC0 policy. Those are different claims, and neither line licenses unrelated museum text, metadata, trademarks, privacy or publicity interests.

Correction

The current correction route is the public COM repository. Propose a dated correction or publish a fresh recheck. Preserve the original record unless evidence establishes that it was recorded incorrectly.

That route may require a GitHub account. There is not yet an account-free public correction channel.
