Reachable futures

Start here

An option written down is not necessarily an option someone can use.

Important limit: more options are not always better. A route for predation is also an option, and preserving everything can consume the resources needed for protection.

Question: Which path is reachable, for whom, under which conditions?

You can stop here. The sections below change resolution or viewpoint; they are not required steps.

Look closer — distinguish possible, reachable, and buildable futures

Possible, presently reachable, and reachable after building capacity are different claims. Each needs conditions, resources and time. Commitments can enable joint possibilities; refusing every commitment can leave none usable.

A useful next move is to name the missing condition rather than merely count options: capability, time, access, cooperation, authority, money, information, or something else.

Change viewpoint — the same option can protect and trap

A restriction may protect one party's future while removing another's way to recover. Counting options from only one position hides that collision.

This paragraph is an authored perspective, not testimony from an affected person. In a real situation, seek the relevant account rather than treating an imagined viewpoint as a witness.

Challenge the account — what would make this framing fail?

A written option may be unusable, but that distinction can also be overworked. Ordinary domain methods may already describe feasibility, capacity or access more precisely. If they do, use them.

Ask whether this framing hides a cost, treats one party's future as the whole system, or makes preserving possibility sound good without saying which possibilities should be closed.

Boundary

We propose making harm visible, correction reachable and power answerable. That is a value choice, not a deduction that binds every possible intelligence.
