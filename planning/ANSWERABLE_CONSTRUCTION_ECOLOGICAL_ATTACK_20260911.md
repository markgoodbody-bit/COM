Aperture: ecological/climate transformation, especially adaptation where physical thresholds, path dependence, cross-boundary externalities, future generations and nonhuman scopes matter.

## HOLDS

### 1. `REACHABLE != VIABLE` survives strongly

This distinction becomes more important, not less, in ecological systems.

A community can have many locally reachable choices while all of them move a coupled water/coast/fire/agricultural system outside viable bounds. Conversely, restricting some present actions can preserve a larger future action space.

The synthesis is right not to equate option count with freedom or good futures.

### 2. commitment can genuinely create capability

Long-lived infrastructure, managed retreat, water-sharing institutions, habitat restoration, insurance pools, evacuation agreements and commons governance all require commitment. Some present options have to be closed for collective capability to exist.

`COMMITMENT_CLOSES_PATHS AND MAY CREATE_NEW_PATHS` travels.

### 3. transition capacity must often be built before visible failure

This also travels strongly.

Relocation capacity, alternative livelihoods, reserves, substitute infrastructure, emergency finance, skills and social support can be practically useless if created only after the old path collapses.

`CORRECTION_CAPACITY_MAY_NEED_TO_BE_BUILT_BEFORE_CORRECTION_IS_NEEDED` survives.

### 4. goal revision is distinct from adaptive means

A pathway can be competently adapted while the objective itself has become untenable.

Examples include defending an exact shoreline indefinitely, restoring an ecosystem to a historical state no longer climatically viable, or maintaining a livelihood system whose enabling conditions have changed irreversibly.

`ADAPTIVE_MEANS != LEGITIMATE_GOAL_REVISION` survives.

## BREAKS

### 1. answer-back cannot be the portable centre

The synthesis says affected positions should not disappear and often frames answerability through the ability to answer back.

That breaks in ecological transition if read literally.

Materially affected scopes may include:
- future people;
- species populations;
- ecological functions;
- downstream communities outside the decision forum;
- people displaced before a consultation exists;
- entities with no recognised institutional voice.

Their absence from direct participation does not erase the causal burden.

So:

```text
AFFECTED_SCOPE_PRESENT != AFFECTED_SCOPE_CAN_SPEAK
NO_DIRECT_ANSWER_BACK != NO_CLAIM
REPRESENTATION_OF_SCOPE != CONSENT_OF_SCOPE
```

Repair: keep **affected-scope visibility, representation/proxy limits and contestability** separate from direct answer-back.

### 2. `enough correction` can be impossible before the decision

Some ecological thresholds destroy correction options.

Species loss, aquifer salinisation, peat/fire regime shifts, soil loss, infrastructure lock-in and settlement exposure can harden beyond useful repair.

A formulation that says commitments should preserve enough correction risks implying that correction will always remain available if governance is designed well enough.

Sometimes the requirement is not correction after commitment but prevention, precaution, reserve capacity and explicit recognition that the move crosses an irreversibility boundary.

Repair:

```text
ANSWERABLE != REVERSIBLE
IRREVERSIBLE_ACTION CAN BE ANSWERABLE
CORRECTION_ROUTE_UNKNOWN/ABSENT -> HIGHER BURDEN BEFORE CLOSURE
```

### 3. domination is not the only failure mode

The synthesis asks how to avoid domination.

Climate/ecological systems can fail through coordination breakdown, collective-action traps, incompatible local optimisation, missing information, delayed investment or biophysical limits even when no actor dominates another.

A village may govern itself democratically and still externalise flood, extraction or fire risk onto another scope.

Repair: the target must include **unanswerable burden and externalised viability loss**, not domination alone.

### 4. local participation does not solve cross-scale legitimacy

Commons/polycentric governance is useful, but ecological systems cross boundaries.

Local users can legitimately govern one resource and still impose costs on:
- another watershed;
- migratory species;
- future users;
- national infrastructure;
- global climate stability.

So:

```text
LOCAL_SELF_GOVERNANCE != COMPLETE_AFFECTED_SCOPE
PARTICIPATION_HERE != LEGITIMACY_EVERYWHERE_AFFECTED
```

The synthesis needs explicit scale-selection and cross-scale coupling.

### 5. `worthwhile capability` becomes anthropocentric if left untyped

Capability language travels well for people and some agentic entities.

It travels less cleanly to ecosystems. An ecosystem does not need to be squeezed into a human-style catalogue of valuable doings/beings for its causal/ethical significance to remain visible.

For nonhuman ecological scopes the stronger portable terms may be viability, functioning, integrity, resilience, habitat, reproduction or other domain-owned concepts.

Repair: `CAPABILITY` is one positive owner, not the universal positive primitive.

### 6. support-before-closure cannot become an impossible veto

The synthesis rightly says losers of transition should remain present and capacity should exist before closure where possible.

But some urgent transitions cannot wait for complete compensation, perfect replacement or unanimous participation. Delay itself can destroy the remaining transition window.

So:

```text
SUPPORT_BEFORE_CLOSURE != ALL_LOSS_REPAIRED_BEFORE_ACTION
UNMET_REPAIR != AUTOMATIC_VETO
DELAY_HAS_BURDEN + CLOCKS
```

Repair: require explicit burden placement, minimum viable transition support and open residue rather than a fantasy of fully clean transition.

## OWNER_BETTER

Several parts are already better owned externally:

- **Dynamic Adaptive Policy Pathways / adaptive pathways** for sequencing adaptation under deep uncertainty and switching before pathways fail;
- **viability theory** for state/control constraints and viability kernels;
- **resilience / transformation literature** for persistence, adaptation and transformation rather than exact restoration;
- **just transition** for distribution, recognition and procedural justice around transition burdens;
- **commons/polycentric governance** for local/nested self-governance;
- domain ecology/hydrology/fire/coastal science for the actual physical thresholds and system dynamics.

Project consequence: do not rewrite these owners in local vocabulary. The project-level remainder is primarily the **coupling between them**: affected scope, changing goals, authority, timing, burden, correction limits and answerability when no single owner spans the whole decision.

## DELETE_OR_REPAIR

### Repair 1 — core question

Current:

> How can bounded entities build worthwhile shared capabilities and durable commitments while preserving enough answerability and correction to avoid domination when values, standing and goals can change?

Narrower portable version:

> **How can bounded entities and groups build viable, worthwhile shared futures through commitments that make real capability possible, while keeping affected scope, externalised burden, changing goals, authority and the limits of correction visible enough to contest and revise where revision remains possible?**

Why:
- removes the implication that every affected scope can answer back;
- removes the implication that correction is always preservable;
- adds externalised burden;
- makes viability explicit;
- keeps revision conditional on physical/social possibility.

### Repair 2 — authority section

Add:

```text
LEGITIMACY_AT_ONE_SCALE != LEGITIMACY_ACROSS_ALL_AFFECTED_SCALES
```

### Repair 3 — correction section

Add:

```text
ANSWERABLE_ACTION MAY STILL BE IRREVERSIBLE
KNOWN_IRREVERSIBILITY -> PRE-ACTION BURDEN, NOT FICTIONAL REPAIR
```

### Repair 4 — positive vocabulary

Change any implication that `capability` is the sole universal positive object.

Use:

> real capability / viability / functioning / other domain-owned positive conditions

where the affected scope is not usefully described by capability theory.
