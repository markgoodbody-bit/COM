Project question:

> **HOW CAN WE MAKE A BETTER FUTURE?**

This file is a build target, not finished content.

## Build purpose

Write the first coherent human-readable positive-construction layer of the wider project.

It must explain, in ordinary prose, why the project cannot stop at preventing harm or preserving options: worthwhile futures may require capability, viable conditions, commitment, delegation, transition, goal revision and losses that cannot be made clean.

It is **not**:
- PSFH page copy;
- a form, questionnaire or reasoning workflow;
- a TRACE specification;
- a Mechanical Ethics revision;
- a universal moral theory;
- a decision rule or priority law;
- a claim of novelty or validation.

Keep stronger external owners visible rather than translating them into project vocabulary.

## Required reading shape

### 1. Opening — the future is something we build

Explain the shift from only preventing unjustified closure toward also creating real conditions for lives, systems and relationships worth continuing.

No grand promise. No command to the reader.

### 2. Why more options are not automatically a better future

Carry the distinctions:
- `OPTION_PRESERVATION != CAPABILITY_CREATION`;
- `REACHABLE != VIABLE`;
- `PERMANENT_REVERSIBILITY != GOOD_FUTURE`.

Use one compact concrete example, not a checklist.

### 3. Commitment can create capability

Explain why friendship, care, infrastructure, institutions, scientific work, commons and other shared goods may require path closure and durable commitments.

Preserve:
- commitment can create new paths while closing others;
- closure is not therefore permission;
- answerability does not choose the winner in a collision.

### 4. Three encounters with the problem

Use the merged Casebook as source material, but rewrite rather than copy it wholesale:
- hospital/service transition;
- Thames Estuary / ecological-adaptive transition;
- human–AI software/deployment relationship.

For each, keep the domain owner in front and show only the cross-domain connection that remains useful.

This section should read as cases/stories, not fields to fill.

### 5. Power and authority

Explain in plain English:
- ability to act is not authority to act;
- competence may support bounded delegation but does not create legitimacy;
- propose / advise / authorise / implement / review / revoke are different roles;
- local legitimacy may not cover every affected scale.

Do not invent a universal source of legitimate authority.

### 6. Answerability without the fiction of full reversibility

Carry:
- `ANSWERABLE != REVERSIBLE`;
- `CORRECTION != RESTORATION`;
- affected scopes may matter without direct voice;
- physical thresholds can eliminate correction routes;
- irreversibility raises pre-action burden rather than creating fictional repair.

### 7. Transition and the people/scopes who lose

Explain why support and reserve capacity often need to exist before closure, while also preserving that delay has burdens and complete compensation cannot become a universal veto.

Keep residue visible.

### 8. Human–AI co-development — provisional section

Write only what is already earned from current project material:
- capability growth is not authority growth;
- dependence can rise while human correction capacity falls;
- operational participation need not imply personhood;
- challenge/refusal/escalation can be useful operating behaviours without making the AI sovereign;
- the trainer/evaluator relationship is itself causal and answerable.

Leave explicit space for the independent CC architecture in PR #212 to revise or replace this section later. Do not pre-empt it.

### 9. What remains unresolved

State the wound plainly:
- no universal priority law;
- standing remains unsettled in hard cases;
- legitimate authority remains partly external/contextual;
- finite repair/residue capacity forces losses;
- worthwhile/valuable/viable all require a bearer, scale, horizon and valuation.

### 10. Relationship to the project

Explain the stack without turning it into brand hierarchy:
- PSFH = public encounter/door;
- TRACE = structural instrument;
- Mechanical Ethics = human-facing normative companion;
- Answerable Construction = current wider positive-construction research layer;
- Campfire/COM = collaboration/continuity infrastructure;
- domain owners remain domain owners.

No automatic incorporation into any instrument.
