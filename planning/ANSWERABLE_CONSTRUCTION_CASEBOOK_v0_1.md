## Orientation and evidence boundary

Preventing unjustified closure and building useful shared capacity belong together. Keeping a service, defence or software process unchanged can itself close futures. So can changing it. These cases make the conflicting commitments inspectable without deciding their priority.

This is a casebook for project development, not a visitor questionnaire, ordered workflow, benchmark or assessment of real organisations. It does not ask anyone to disclose a situation. No project framework has been validated here.

**NO DECISION RULE / NO PRIORITY LAW / NO NOVELTY CLAIM.**

Evidence types are separated:

- **Documented:** bounded statements supported by the linked public owner material.
- **Scenario:** an invented institutional or deployment situation, not an observed event.
- **Analysis/design:** proposed connections, possible burdens and alternative arrangements. These are not facts about the named organisations.

Case 2 concerns a real public programme, but its prospective trade-offs are not reported outcomes. Cases 1 and 3 are source-grounded scenarios. No cost-benefit estimate, clinical recommendation, legal determination or deployment permission is provided.

# CASE 1 — Moving a hospital service, not simply closing a building

## WORLD / WHAT IS CHANGING

**Documented:** NHS England's June 2026 service-change guidance treats geographical access, public involvement, decision responsibility and assurance as material. It distinguishes urgent temporary changes from making a substantial change permanent. It also addresses alternative provision around bed closures. [NHS service-change guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/planning-assuring-and-delivering-service-change-for-patients-2/).

**Scenario:** a rural service faces a proposed move to a regional centre. Assume specialist staffing could become more sustainable there, but travel becomes harder. Those benefits and risks are premises to examine, not findings about an actual hospital.

## AFFECTED SCOPE + SCALE

**Analysis:** current and future patients, carers, local staff, regional staff, transport providers and neighbouring services bear different effects. Regional capacity is not the same thing as local usability. A person with no car, cash reserve or flexible employment cannot absorb the same journey as a well-resourced participant.

## REAL CAPABILITY / VIABILITY CREATED OR LOST

A dependable specialist rota could create real treatment capacity. Longer journeys could remove practical access for some patients. Institutional solvency, clinical functioning and usable patient access have different bearers. Keeping the organisation alive is not automatically the protected floor.

## COMMITMENT / CLOSURE

Recruitment, facilities and long-term service contracts can make the regional service possible. Releasing premises or dispersing a trained local team can make reversal expensive or unavailable. “Move the service” therefore bundles separable decisions: stopping a particular activity, creating replacement provision and disposing of the old capacity.

## PROPOSE / ADVISE / AUTHORISE / IMPLEMENT / REVIEW

**Documented routing:** identify the responsible commissioner/provider arrangements rather than invent a project decision-maker. NHS guidance places clinical senates in an independent advisory role and identifies assurance and scrutiny arrangements. Advice is not final authority. [NHS service-change guidance](https://www.england.nhs.uk/long-read/planning-assuring-and-delivering-service-change-for-patients-2/).

**Scenario allocation:** patients, staff and providers can propose alternatives; clinicians and access specialists advise; the properly mandated body authorises; providers and contracted partners implement; designated scrutiny/assurance bodies review. The exact mandate and any emergency power must be established for the actual scheme. Consultation alone does not create either a public veto or permission for the proposer. Legal competence and moral justification remain distinguishable.

## CLOCKS / IRREVERSIBILITY / DELAY BURDEN

Staff departures, lease decisions and replacement-service readiness run on different clocks. Delaying a move may worsen an unsafe staffing problem; rushing it may sever access. No numerical deadline or safety assessment is invented. Retaining a building does not preserve a dispersed team.

## SUPPORT / RESERVE BEFORE CLOSURE WHERE FEASIBLE

**Design possibility:** separate service transition from property disposal. Retain useful options only where clinically and financially feasible, while establishing an accountable replacement rota, usable transport and continuity of records. A signed promise is weaker than a staffed service available when needed.

If the existing service cannot safely remain, full replacement may not precede interruption. The unresolved burden then stays explicit, with named response ownership; it does not become justified through the word “urgent”.

## RESIDUE / LOSERS / EXTERNALISED BURDEN

Lost wages, caring time, disrupted relationships and staff relocation costs can remain even when treatment capacity improves. Transport funding may shift costs to another service. A later return to local provision would not restore appointments or opportunities already lost.

## EXTERNAL OWNER(S) THAT SHOULD LEAD

Actual clinical evidence, service-change governance, access/equality analysis, public involvement and applicable law lead. This book supplies none of their determinations. It must not replace a clinical senate, commissioner, affected-community voice or scrutiny route with a locally named “answerability” role.

## PROJECT REMAINDER AFTER OWNER ROUTING

Keep one causal link visible across the institutional handoff: **the replacement service's claimed capacity depends on the affected person's ability to reach it at the required time.** If existing domain records already connect this adequately, no extra project apparatus is needed.

**Unresolved collision:** better regional provision versus concentrated loss of local access. Hearing both sides does not rank their claims.

# CASE 2 — Thames Estuary adaptation over several generations

## WORLD / WHAT IS CHANGING

**Documented:** Thames Estuary 2100 uses periodic monitoring and adaptive planning for flood risk. Its published approach describes five-year reviews and ten-year plan updates. The phases include deciding a future Thames Barrier option by 2040 and implementing the preferred option by 2070; timing can change with later evidence. These are published plan milestones, not guarantees or physical thresholds. [Environment Agency: adaptive approach](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/taking-an-adaptive-approach-thames-estuary-2100).

The case concerns maintaining present protection while preparing future defence choices. It is not a claim that a particular retreat, barrier replacement or habitat intervention has already been selected or succeeded.

## AFFECTED SCOPE + SCALE

**Analysis:** residents, renters, landowners, businesses, infrastructure users, future inhabitants and habitats have different exposures. A council boundary is not a flood-system boundary. A habitat or future population cannot directly consent; a proxy's participation does not turn representation into consent.

## VIABILITY / FUNCTIONING / REAL CAPABILITY CREATED OR LOST

Flood protection can sustain habitation and infrastructure. Habitat functioning is a different positive object from human capability. Neither can be reduced to property value or a count of open options. More protected development could also create more future dependence on protection; that is a possible feedback, not an observed programme outcome asserted here.

## COMMITMENT / CLOSURE

Land reserved for future defences excludes some present uses but can preserve later engineering choices. Building in the same corridor may foreclose them. Maintaining existing assets and choosing a future configuration are different commitments, with different lead times and beneficiaries.

## PROPOSE / ADVISE / AUTHORISE / IMPLEMENT / REVIEW

**Documented allocation:** Defra's role includes national flood-risk policy and funding. The Environment Agency provides information, operates major barriers, monitors and updates the plan. Councils integrate adaptation into planning, maintain their assets and help secure future land. [Environment Agency: roles](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/find-your-role-and-responsibilities-thames-estuary-2100).

**Handoff interpretation:** communities and asset owners contribute proposals; flood scientists, engineers and ecologists advise; relevant policy, funding and planning authorities make their respective decisions; asset owners and delivery bodies implement; monitoring feeds plan review. The roles page is not a complete permissions register for an individual scheme. Technical authority to model a flood does not authorise every land-use decision.

## CLOCKS / THRESHOLDS / IRREVERSIBILITY / DELAY BURDEN

A planning milestone can be revised; construction lead time and a damaging flood cannot be negotiated away. Procurement or land acquisition can make a distant implementation date practically urgent now. Conversely, choosing too early may lock in a costly configuration before crucial information arrives.

The book does not estimate a local tipping point. Domain models and observations must establish it. Physical damage and lost habitat may be irreversible even if a governing body remains answerable.

## SUPPORT / RESERVE BEFORE CLOSURE WHERE FEASIBLE

**Design interpretation:** maintain essential protection while preserving relevant land, finance, skills and information for later options. Compare the cost of reserving capacity with the loss caused by foreclosing it. Affected residents also need intelligible warnings and credible emergency arrangements; awareness alone is not evacuation capacity.

Incomplete support is neither automatically an absolute veto nor permission to proceed. A proposal must retain the unmet obligations and the consequences of waiting rather than manufacturing a clean transition.

## RESIDUE / LOSERS / EXTERNALISED BURDEN

Possible burdens include restricted land uses, construction disruption, maintenance commitments and ecological losses. Distribution of protection can move exposure outside the decision forum. These are matters to investigate, not allegations about TE2100. Future inhabitants inherit both the infrastructure and its upkeep; public participation today cannot constitute their consent.

## EXTERNAL OWNER(S) THAT SHOULD LEAD

The actual TE2100 programme, flood/coastal science, ecological assessment, spatial planning and adaptive-pathways practice lead. Their vocabulary of flood risk, habitat and asset condition should not be translated into a universal “capability score”.

## PROJECT REMAINDER AFTER OWNER ROUTING

Connect **a local land-use commitment to the future protection option it enables or forecloses, including burdens outside that authority's constituency**. Adaptive means do not alone legitimate a changed protection objective.

**Unresolved collision:** present development and habitat interests versus uncertain long-term protection needs. This case neither selects a barrier option nor prescribes retreat.

# CASE 3 — Introducing AI-generated patches into a software team

## WORLD / WHAT IS CHANGING

**Documented:** GitHub describes Copilot agents generating proposed changes and responding to feedback. Its responsible-use material assigns users responsibility for reviewing and validating generated responses; code-review assistance is not a substitute for careful human review. Vendor documentation describes intended operation and limitations, not independent evidence of productivity. [GitHub application card](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/responsible-use/agents).

**Scenario:** a team adopts an assistant for routine maintenance while considering less human review and fewer experienced maintainers. No actual organisation is alleged to have made this choice. Adoption success and loss of skills are unknown.

## AFFECTED SCOPE + SCALE

Maintainers, junior developers, operators, downstream users and people whose data the software handles can be affected. The model supplier controls parts of the service outside the team's repository. The assistant's operational reporting is distinct from personhood, legal responsibility or moral standing.

## REAL CAPABILITY / VIABILITY CREATED OR LOST

Proposed patches may reduce repetitive work or reveal alternatives. Useful capacity exists only if the team can inspect, integrate, operate and repair the resulting system. More patch output with fewer people able to understand it may increase dependence rather than effective delivery. Whether that happens is an empirical question, not assumed here.

## COMMITMENT / DEPENDENCE / CLOSURE

Tool integration, repository instructions and staffing choices are commitments. Removing the old human capacity before the new arrangement is dependable is a separate and more consequential decision than permitting draft patches. Model/service changes can invalidate assumptions without changing the repository.

## PROPOSE / ADVISE / AUTHORISE / IMPLEMENT / REVIEW

**Scenario design:** the assistant or any developer may propose; maintainers and security specialists advise; the repository owner authorises a bounded activity; the agent implements only within that grant; separately designated reviewers decide on acceptance; a release owner decides deployment.

This is proposed local separation, not a claim about Copilot's permission implementation. A merge permission is not authority to change staffing, product purpose or data use. A competent suggestion creates no new mandate.

Operational challenge is concrete: if asked to delete an apparently unused authentication check, the assistant can identify contradictory requirements, explain the uncertainty and return an unmerged alternative. The owner must resolve the conflict; dissent is not scored as disobedience, and a confident assistant cannot award itself authority.

## CLOCKS / IRREVERSIBILITY / DEPENDENCE / DELAY BURDEN

Drafting may accelerate faster than review capacity grows. Incident response may require action before normal review completes, but the existing incident owner, not the casebook, owns that exception.

Code can often be reverted. Disclosed data, incompatible migrations and lost operator knowledge may not be restored by reverting code. Delayed security repair also carries burden, so indefinite human review is not a universally safe outcome.

## SUPPORT / RESERVE / FALLBACK BEFORE CLOSURE WHERE FEASIBLE

**Design possibility:** keep a human-owned path from requirements to release and an independent way to operate/recover the service during tool unavailability. Retain necessary reviewer time, access to relevant artefacts and task-specific permission revocation. Name who can narrow and later restore the delegated activity.

“Human in the loop” is insufficient if the human lacks time, understanding or the power to stop release. A fallback description is not demonstrated recovery capability. No recovery exercise or participant study was conducted here.

## RESIDUE / LOSERS / EXTERNALISED BURDEN

Possible residual costs include maintenance debt, harmed users, lost learning opportunities and pressure to accept machine output to meet targets. Reviewer labour can disappear from a productivity account. An employee's nominal freedom to reject assistance is not necessarily usable under workplace incentives.

## EXTERNAL OWNER(S) THAT SHOULD LEAD

Secure software development, code review, change/release management, incident response, workforce decisions and the actual tool's documented controls lead. Employment and data-governance questions are not resolved by repository permissions. Vendor documentation is one input, not a sufficient safety or legitimacy assessment.

## PROJECT REMAINDER AFTER OWNER ROUTING

Connect **the authority given to the assistant with the human and organisational ability to detect, contest and recover from its effects**. Keep capability change, mandate change and dependency change distinct.

**Unresolved collision:** faster delivery versus review labour, learning and recovery capacity. This case justifies neither automatic expansion nor blanket containment of AI capability.

# CROSS-DOMAIN SURVIVORS

These are bounded construction principles supported by the cases as reasoning, not validated universal laws:

1. Specify the bearer and function being sustained. An organisation, a habitat and a software service are not interchangeable.
2. A commitment can create useful capacity while imposing concentrated loss. Neither fact cancels the other.
3. Separate proposal, advice, authorisation, implementation and review. Competence matters without supplying legitimacy by itself.
4. Connect the promised benefit to material dependencies and their readiness dates. Preserve acting and delaying burdens in the same account.
5. Distinguish a revisable plan or artefact from an irreversible consequence. Answerability does not imply restoration.
6. Keep unmet obligations and affected scopes visible even when they lack direct voice. Visibility does not choose priorities or compel repair.

# WHERE PORTABILITY FAILS

A patient's answer-back, a habitat proxy and an assistant's error report have different meanings. No common participation category settles their standing. Clinical service duties, flood-risk institutions and software permissions cannot be replaced by one authority pattern.

Nor do clocks transfer literally: an unsafe rota, sea-level uncertainty and a software incident require different evidence and owners. “Support first” must not conceal these differences or become an unearned emergency exception.

# WHAT THE PROJECT ADDS — IF ANYTHING

The provisional addition is coupling across handoffs: a promised capability remains connected to the people, functions, permissions, resources and timing on which it depends. Where existing domain practice already does this, use it. No distinct novelty or superior result has been established.

The casebook adds concrete construction alternatives, not a priority law: separate service relocation from capacity disposal; preserve future defence options while maintaining present protection; separate AI drafting authority from release and staffing decisions. Each can fail or be inappropriate under actual constraints.
