> **HOW CAN WE MAKE A BETTER FUTURE?**

## 1. Repaired frontier question

Initial question:

> How can bounded entities build worthwhile shared capabilities and durable commitments while preserving enough answerability and correction to avoid domination when values, standing and goals can change?

The first two attacks showed three overclaims:
- not every affected scope can answer back directly;
- meaningful correction is sometimes physically/socially impossible;
- domination is not the only failure mode.

Current narrower question:

> **How can bounded entities and groups build viable, worthwhile shared futures through commitments that make real capability or other domain-owned positive conditions possible, while keeping affected scope, externalised burden, changing goals, authority and the limits of correction visible enough to contest and revise where revision remains genuinely possible?**

This does not say which future is worthwhile or who should win a collision.

```text
ANSWERABLE != JUSTIFIED
VISIBLE_COLLISION != RESOLVED_COLLISION
PORTABLE != UNIVERSAL
```

## 2. Positive construction is not option maximisation

The project's earlier strength remains important: detect premature closure, unusable routes, hidden burden, late correction, captured authority and residue.

But preserving options is not enough.

A system can preserve many nominal options while leaving people or other affected systems unable to do anything viable with them. Durable goods often require closing alternatives: infrastructure, teams, contracts, care relationships, commons, scientific projects and transition plans depend on commitments.

```text
OPTION_PRESERVATION != CAPABILITY_CREATION
MORE_OPTIONS != MORE_SUBSTANTIVE_FREEDOM
PERMANENT_REVERSIBILITY != GOOD_FUTURE
COMMITMENT_CLOSES_PATHS AND MAY_CREATE_NEW_PATHS
```

The positive object is not one universal primitive. Depending on the domain, the relevant condition may be capability, viability, functioning, integrity, resilience, access, habitat, continuity of a function, or another domain-owned concept.

```text
CAPABILITY != UNIVERSAL_POSITIVE_PRIMITIVE
OWNER_TERMS_BEFORE_LOCAL_RENAMING
```

## 3. Viability needs a bearer, function, horizon and scale

`REACHABLE != VIABLE` survives strongly, but `viability` cannot float free of an object.

Ask:

- viability of whom or what?
- viability of which function rather than which incumbent organisation?
- over what horizon?
- at what spatial/institutional scale?
- against which physical/social constraints?

A hospital institution may cease while access to a health function survives elsewhere. A local resource regime may remain viable while exporting risk to a downstream scope. An ecosystem may retain one function while losing another.

```text
VIABILITY_OF_WHAT / WHOM / AT_WHICH_SCALE?
ORGANISATIONAL_SURVIVAL != FUNCTIONAL_CONTINUITY
LOCAL_VIABILITY != SYSTEM_VIABILITY
```

Choosing the bearer is already a distributive/value decision. The framework does not make that choice neutral by naming it.

## 4. Affected scope does not require direct voice

The original synthesis leaned too heavily on answer-back as the portable centre.

Direct answer-back matters where it exists. But materially affected scopes may include:

- future people;
- nonhuman populations/functions;
- displaced people no longer present in the forum;
- downstream or cross-border communities;
- people without practical access to the decision process;
- artificial or institutional participants whose operational challenge capacity exists without settled standing.

So separate:

```text
AFFECTED_SCOPE_PRESENT != AFFECTED_SCOPE_CAN_SPEAK
NO_DIRECT_ANSWER_BACK != NO_CLAIM
REPRESENTATION_OF_SCOPE != CONSENT_OF_SCOPE
PARTICIPATION != STANDING
```

The portable requirement is weaker and more honest: affected scope, representation/proxy limits, burden and exclusion must remain visible enough to challenge the account.

## 5. Commitment can be answerable without being reversible

Some thresholds destroy correction options.

Examples can include ecological loss, infrastructure lock-in, settlement exposure, institutional dependence, irreversible disclosure, or commitments whose unwinding itself produces greater harm.

Do not promise a correction route that physics/social structure cannot provide.

```text
ANSWERABLE != REVERSIBLE
IRREVERSIBLE_ACTION CAN STILL BE ANSWERABLE
KNOWN_IRREVERSIBILITY -> HIGHER_PRE_ACTION_BURDEN, NOT FICTIONAL_REPAIR
CORRECTION != RESTORATION
```

Where revision remains possible, build usable revision/exit/repair routes. Where it does not, the pre-action burden rises: evidence, affected-scope visibility, authority, alternatives, reserve capacity and residue must be more explicit.

## 6. Authority functions must be separated

The original synthesis correctly separated competence from legitimacy but then risked smuggling competence back in as a source of authority.

Separate at least:

- who may **propose**;
- who may **advise**;
- who may **authorise**;
- who may **implement**;
- who may **observe/audit/review**;
- who may **narrow/revoke/restore** a delegation.

Expertise/competence can justify reliance, advice or bounded delegation under an already justified authorising relationship. It does not by itself create a mandate to revise collective goals.

```text
COMPETENCE -> RELEVANT_EVIDENCE_FOR_DELEGATION
COMPETENCE != LEGITIMACY
CAPABILITY != AUTHORITY
REPUTATION != AUTHORIZATION
```

Legal authority may settle who can act within a system without settling every moral objection. Participation may improve legitimacy without creating a universal veto.

## 7. Legitimate here may still impose there

Local participation and self-governance are valuable but incomplete in coupled systems.

A locally legitimate choice can impose burden on another place, scale, generation or affected scope.

```text
LOCAL_SELF_GOVERNANCE != COMPLETE_AFFECTED_SCOPE
LEGITIMACY_AT_ONE_SCALE != LEGITIMACY_ACROSS_ALL_AFFECTED_SCALES
PARTICIPATION_HERE != CONSENT_EVERYWHERE_AFFECTED
```

Positive construction therefore needs explicit coupling across scales, not a presumption that more participation in one forum closes the legitimacy question.

## 8. Answerability underdetermines priority

This is not a defect to hide.

Two options can both:
- disclose dependencies;
- identify affected scopes;
- hear objections;
- preserve review;
- fund some mitigation;
- distinguish authority roles;

and still impose different serious losses.

Answerability helps prevent false cleanliness. It does not choose the winner.

```text
ANSWERABILITY != PRIORITY_RULE
PROCEDURAL_QUALITY != MORAL_CLEANSING
LOSSES_RECORDED != LOSSES_JUSTIFIED
```

The core wound remains: when protected claims genuinely collide, selection/priority remains external and contestable. The project must not recover a hidden priority law through words like `shared`, `valuable`, `viable`, `necessary` or `worthwhile`.

## 9. Goal revision is a separate authority problem

Adaptive means can change while the objective remains fixed. Sometimes the objective itself becomes impossible, obsolete, unjust or harmful.

```text
ADAPTIVE_MEANS != LEGITIMATE_GOAL_REVISION
```

Ask separately:

- who can propose the new objective?
- who can authorise revision?
- which affected scopes were protected under the previous objective?
- what prior commitments/promises remain?
- who loses when the success criterion changes?
- what cannot be erased by redefining success?

A later objective cannot simply reclassify yesterday's promised access or protection as irrelevant and call the residue repaired.

## 10. Build transition capacity before clocks close — where feasible

The original synthesis's prebuilt-capacity direction survives, but not as an absolute veto.

Useful prebuilt capacity can include:
- finance/reserve;
- alternative infrastructure;
- skills/training;
- transport/access;
- relocation/evacuation routes;
- technical fallback;
- legal/administrative capacity;
- human ability to continue/recover without a critical AI dependency;
- challenge/correction routes.

But sometimes action cannot wait for complete substitution or compensation.

Separate:

1. desired support;
2. funded/feasible support;
3. minimum viable transition support, if one can be justified;
4. unmet obligations/residue;
5. harms of acting now;
6. harms of delay.

```text
CORRECTION_CAPACITY_MAY_NEED_TO_BE_BUILT_BEFORE_CORRECTION_IS_NEEDED
SUPPORT_BEFORE_CLOSURE != ALL_LOSS_REPAIRED_BEFORE_ACTION
UNMET_REPAIR != AUTOMATIC_VETO
DELAY_HAS_BURDEN + CLOCKS
```

Urgency itself does not create authority. Delay itself is still an action path with consequences.

## 11. Formation / entrustment remains provisional pending CC build

The earlier synthesis proposed reciprocal formation and task-specific entrustment for human–AI relationships.

Keep only the typed ceilings for now:

```text
PARTICIPATION != PERSONHOOD
OPERATIONAL_ANSWER_BACK != POLITICAL_VOICE
COMPETENCE != LEGITIMACY
ENTRUSTMENT != OBEDIENCE
CURRENT_CAPABILITY != PERMANENT_AUTHORITY
ROLE_CONTINUITY != RUNTIME_IDENTITY
```

The detailed architecture is now owned by CC PR #212 and should not be pre-empted here.

Open question:

> Can a human–AI relationship widen meaningful initiative while keeping both the artificial participant and the human/operator/institution answerable and correctable, without collapsing into obedience training, reputation sovereignty, paternal containment or dependence?

## 12. Domain owners lead; project remainder is coupling

The attacks strengthen owner routing.

Examples:
- adaptive pathways / DAPP for sequencing under deep uncertainty;
- viability theory for constrained dynamics;
- resilience/transformation science for persistence/adaptation/transformation;
- just-transition scholarship/practice for distribution/recognition/procedure;
- domain ecology/medicine/infrastructure/etc. for actual physical/technical facts;
- formal institutional/legal processes for authorised roles;
- professional/team-safety methods for specific challenge/escalation practices;
- security/trust-domain mechanisms for technical identity/authentication where relevant.

The project should not rewrite these owners in local vocabulary.

Possible project remainder:

> keep cross-owner couplings visible when no single owner carries affected scope, changing objectives, authority roles, clocks, externalised burden, correction limits and residue together.

That remainder may still prove too broad or redundant. Casebook PR #211 should pressure it concretely.

## 13. Provisional constructive sequence — descriptive, not mandatory

Not a visitor workflow, checklist or decision rule.

```text
SITUATE THE CHANGING WORLD
-> NAME THE RELEVANT BEARER / FUNCTION / SCALE
-> ROUTE TO DOMAIN OWNERS
-> IDENTIFY REAL POSITIVE CONDITIONS TO CREATE/PRESERVE
-> MAKE COMMITMENTS / CLOSURES EXPLICIT
-> SEPARATE AUTHORITY FUNCTIONS
-> SHOW AFFECTED SCOPE + EXTERNALISED BURDEN
-> SHOW CLOCKS + IRREVERSIBILITY + DELAY BURDEN
-> BUILD TRANSITION / CORRECTION CAPACITY WHERE GENUINELY POSSIBLE
-> ACT UNDER EXTERNAL AUTHORITY / DOMAIN RULES
-> KEEP RESIDUE + CHANGING GOALS OPEN TO REVIEW WHERE REVIEW REMAINS POSSIBLE
```

This sequence describes structures worth keeping visible. It does not grant clearance to act.

## 15. Current project-level compression

> **Build and govern commitments that create viable positive conditions without hiding affected scope, externalised burden, authority, changing objectives, irreversibility or residue — and preserve correction/revision where it is genuinely possible rather than promising it where it is not.**

This is still broader than TRACE and Mechanical Ethics and may remain a project-level orientation rather than become either framework.
